Where does feeling come from?

An ongoing investigation into sentience: propose an answer, work out what it predicts, check that against published evidence, and have independent reviewers try to break the result. Earlier updates contain claims that were later withdrawn; each such page carries a notice. Each update is written to be read with no background.

Update 9 · 4 October 2026Your hypothesis, checked against existing studies: not refuted, not confirmed, never put at real riskAfter the audit in Update 8 said the project had drifted, this round went back to your working idea: that feeling is what motivates, and thought works out how to satisfy it. We set it against two established rival accounts, had the wording checked for fairness before any scoring, and had the evidence scored independently.
  2. Update 8 · 4 October 2026An audit of everything so far: what we overstated, and a change of directionThis round we asked a fresh reviewer to go back over every claim the project currently treats as standing, and to check whether our public summaries matched the reviews behind them. They mostly did not. This update reports what was wrong, what has been changed, and where the work goes next.
  3. Update 7 · 4 October 2026 · later corrected, see notice on pageTwo markers struck off, and the gap at the centre of the whole fieldThis round used elimination. Scientists have proposed various brain measurements as signs that someone is having an experience. We checked each against situations where people later say what they experienced, and had the results scored independently. Two were eliminated. The more important finding is what nobody has measured.
  4. Update 16 · 4 October 2026What can value do without feeling? The clearest pattern was the reverse: feeling that action did not followYour hypothesis says feeling is the motivator and thought works out how to serve it. This round asked what happens when something's value (how good or bad it is for you) is processed without being felt, using other people's published studies. Predictions for four views were written down first and corrected by a fairness check; two evidence gatherers collected studies without scoring them; a separate adjudicator scored (all are separate AI agents given the written plan and the evidence, not our conclusions). The clearest pattern was not the one we went looking for.
  5. Update 15 · 4 October 2026Is there experience with no feeling in it? The best evidence leans against the feeling-first idea, without settling itWe left the sleep recordings (Updates 13 and 14, brain-wave recordings of people woken and asked what they had been experiencing) and went back to what is distinctive about your hypothesis: that feeling is the base of all experience and thought is added on top. If so, there should be no experience without some feeling in it. This round asked whether that is true, using other people's published studies. Predictions for five views (yours and four rivals) were written down first, checked for fairness, and then scored (the fairness checker, the evidence gatherers and the adjudicator who scores are separate AI agents, each given only the written plan and the evidence, not our conclusions). The result is not what the hypothesis hoped for, and one thing we told you beforehand was wrong.
  6. Update 14 · 4 October 2026Two predictions, written down first, passed their tests, one narrowly and one on recordings that were not fully fresh — and what they show may simply be lighter sleepLast time, four studies of people woken from sleep found no reliable brain signature of experience. This round followed up the one well-known published claim in the area, that slow brain waves at the back of the head go with not experiencing anything. Two studies: one asked whether the difference really is at the back; the other took a lead a reviewer stumbled on and tested it once, on recordings that played no part in finding it. That test passed, narrowly. The checks made afterwards suggested a different reading, which became a third study with its own prediction; that passed clearly, and its review then corrected what it means.
  7. Update 13 · 4 October 2026Our own studies on open sleep recordings: four studies, no reliable brain signature found, most tests too weak to settle itYou asked us to take our own direction and try to find something new. Reading other people's papers had reached its limit, so this round we did original work: four studies on open recordings of people woken from sleep and asked what they had been experiencing. Each was written down in advance, run, and then re-run and attacked by an independent reviewer, who got the same numbers. None found a reliable brain signature; Study 2 found a modest difference between people. This page says exactly what that does and does not show.
  8. Update 12 · 4 October 2026Does time create sentience? What can and cannot be saidYou asked us to investigate whether time creates feeling or has a part in creating it. We wrote down six versions of the idea; a fairness check of the wording added a seventh. We then gathered the published evidence, had it scored independently, and ran one test of our own on public sleep recordings.

Update 11 · 4 October 2026The edge of what the evidence can decideRound 11 looked for evidence on the live contrast: is a feeling the same thing as a value signal the planning mind can use, or a further layer built on top of it? We found no study that tests it. The independent scorer was then asked to step back over the last three rounds and say plainly where things stand. This update reports that answer, a correction to Update 1, and two things we need from you.

This round went looking for such cases. None was found. But having the rival stated properly led the scorer to a view about your hypothesis itself, and it comes with a fork in the road.
  11. Update 6 · 3 October 2026 · later corrected, see notice on pageIs feeling the brain's own electric field? And a test on 220,000 lottery ballsTwo pieces of work this round. The first followed last update's lead and gave the electric-field theory a fair hearing, scored independently. The second was our first experiment on real-world data: if wanting something can tilt chance, lottery balls should favour the numbers most people bet on.
  12. Update 5 · 3 October 2026A fairer test, an honest "not yet", and what a sentience field could be made ofThis round we changed how we work, to stop overstating results. We wrote down what each rival idea predicts before looking at any evidence, had one assistant gather the evidence without judging it, and had a third, independent one do the scoring. The answer that came back is modest. It is also the first one we have not had to take back.
  13. Update 4 · 3 October 2026 · later corrected, see notice on pageWhere in the brain is feeling? One firm result, and the wall every question hitsThis round went straight at "where does it come from": for each part of the brain, does feeling survive when that part is destroyed, and does stimulating it produce a feeling? The reviewers cut the conclusion down to one firm result. They also overturned last update's headline. Four rounds in, a pattern is clear enough to state.

Update 3 · 3 October 2026 · later corrected, see notice on pageTwo kinds of "chooser" are ruled out, and a list of experiments nobody has runThis round tested five new ideas about what feeling is for, and asked whether the "choosing field" from Update 2 could ever be detected. Independent reviewers again cut several of our verdicts back. What is left is smaller than we hoped but firmer: one clear elimination, one correction to a claim we made last time, and six concrete experiments that would move the question and have never been done.

  16. Update 1 · 3 October 2026 · later corrected, see notice on pageWhere does feeling come from? What the first round ruled out.We read the research, ran four small evolution experiments and one physics calculation.
