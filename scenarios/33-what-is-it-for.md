# Scenario 33 — Round 18: what is conscious feeling for?

**Predictions written and committed before evidence is gathered.** Wording to be checked
for fairness before any evidence is scored.

## The question

Which abilities are found only when something is consciously experienced, and which of
those depend on *feeling* in particular? The owner's first question was whether sentience
helps survival over an equally clever automaton. Round 16 asked what value can do without
feeling; this round asks the mirror question across all abilities.

## A limit stated first

If an ability fails whenever a stimulus is not consciously experienced, that shows the
ability and experience go together. It does not show experience does the work: the
unseen stimulus may simply be too weakly processed to support anything demanding. This
round can find out *which abilities travel with experience*; it cannot by itself show
what experience contributes. The adjudicator is asked to keep the two apart.

## Candidate abilities

a. Learning across a gap in time (trace conditioning) and other complex learning.
b. Holding information in mind for seconds and working on it.
c. Chaining several mental steps (e.g. unseen arithmetic, reading unseen sentences).
d. Combining information across senses or domains for the first time.
e. Overriding a habit / exercising control in a new situation.
f. Trading off unlike needs against each other (pain against food, effort against reward).
g. Reporting and explaining one's own state to others; modelling other minds.
h. Simple things: reflexes, priming, well-practised routines, simple conditioning.

## Accounts and what each predicts

**E. Epiphenomenal / no function.** Experience does no work.
1. No ability is reliably absent without experience, given strong enough unconscious
   processing; apparent failures shrink as methods improve.

**G. Global availability** (workspace views). Experience goes with information being
broadcast for flexible use.
1. b, c, d, e are absent or sharply limited without experience; h is intact.
2. Nothing special about feeling: a, f go with experience only insofar as they need b–e.

**U. Learning** (unlimited associative learning). Experience goes with open-ended
learning.
1. a is absent without experience; simple conditioning (h) is intact.
2. Animals showing a also show the other marks of experience.

**C. Common currency** (feeling-first). Felt pleasantness/unpleasantness exists to weigh
unlike needs on one scale and set priorities.
1. f is absent or disordered when feeling is absent or blunted, even with b–e intact.
2. Organisms that make flexible trade-offs between unlike needs show other signs of
   feeling; simple organisms with fixed priorities do not.
3. b–e can proceed on neutral material with no feeling.

**A. Action conflict.** Experience is needed only to resolve competing plans for the
voluntary muscles.
1. Conflicts within the skeletal-muscle system require experience; conflicts elsewhere
   (pupil, gut, reflexes) are resolved without it. c, d need it only when they lead to
   competing actions.

**S. Social/self-model.** Experience is the brain's model of its own state for report and
for understanding others.
1. g is what goes with experience; b–f can be done without it in principle, and
   dissociate from report in practice (blindsight-like performance).

## What separates them

E against all on prediction 1. G vs U on whether a goes with experience independently of
b–e. C against G, U, A, S on whether trading off unlike needs depends on *feeling*
specifically. A on whether non-action conflicts need experience. S on dissociations
between performance and report.

## What would count

For "ability X without experience": positive results count only with an adequate
awareness test, no post-hoc selection of unaware participants, and replication. For
"cannot": failures count only from adequately powered studies. Blindsight, sleep,
anaesthesia and brain-injury evidence count as well as masking, each labelled.

## What the author already believes (disclosed)

From memory, unverified: several headline claims of sophisticated unconscious processing
(unseen arithmetic, unseen sentence reading, unconscious working memory) have had failed
replications; trace conditioning without awareness is disputed; round 16 found unfelt
value not established even for simple behaviour. The author expects the list of things
done without experience to be shorter than textbooks say, expects that to be explainable
by weak processing as easily as by any function of experience, and expects direct
evidence on f (trade-offs) to be thin.
