# Scenario 11 — Crossing the report barrier

Round 5. **Part 1 (this section) was written and committed before any evidence was
gathered.** Parts 2 and 3 are filled in afterwards, the scoring by an independent
reviewer and not by us.

## The three accounts being separated

- **D, deep-first.** Feeling is generated by old deep structures (upper brainstem,
  hypothalamus and neighbours). Cortex is needed to think and talk about it.
- **C, cortex-makes-it-felt.** Deep structures produce reactions that are not in
  themselves felt. A network in the outer frontal and parietal cortex is what makes them
  felt (higher-order and workspace theories; LeDoux & Brown).
- **R, distributed-redundant.** Feeling depends on many regions, deep and cortical, with
  enough redundancy that no single loss removes it.

## Part 1 — Predictions, written first

All cases involve people who can report, at the time or afterwards.

| # | Situation | D predicts | C predicts | R predicts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Both frontal lobes largely lost or removed** (tumour surgery, injury) in a patient who can still speak | Feelings reported as present; planning and concern reduced | Felt emotion abolished or greatly reduced; patient should say so if asked | Present, possibly altered |
| 2 | **Dreaming sleep**, when the outer frontal cortex is known to be largely switched off, with report on waking | Dreams are emotionally intense regardless of frontal activity | Dream emotion should be weak, or should rise and fall with frontal activity (for example much stronger in lucid dreams, where frontal cortex comes back on) | Present |
| 3 | **Deep structure strongly active, person awake and able to report, no feeling reported** | Should not happen for a true generator (PAG, hypothalamus). May happen for triggers such as the amygdala | Should happen, whenever the cortical network is not recruited | Should be rare and partial |
| 4 | **Timing**, in patients with electrodes both deep and on the cortex | Deep activity comes first and the moment of feeling is locked to it | The moment of feeling is locked to a later burst of frontal–parietal activity; deep activity alone does not predict it | No consistent leader |
| 5 | **Frontal disconnection operations** (old lobotomy and leucotomy records; cingulotomy) | Raw unpleasantness still reported; worry about it reduced | Felt unpleasantness itself reduced | Altered, not abolished |
| 6 | **One half of the cortex put to sleep** for a few minutes (pre-surgical test) | Feelings continue | Feelings continue (the other half is awake) | Feelings continue |

Row 6 does not discriminate and is listed so we do not later pretend it did.

**What would count as a clear result.** D is in trouble if row 3 turns up clean cases
for PAG or hypothalamus, or if row 4 shows feeling locked to cortex and not to deep
activity. C is in trouble if rows 1 and 2 show full-strength feeling with the frontal
network gone or off. R is in trouble only if a single structure turns out to be
decisive in row 3 or 4.

Known weakness in advance: memory. "Did not feel it" and "does not remember feeling it"
look the same in a later report. Rows 2 and 3 are most exposed.
