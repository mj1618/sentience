# Scenario 21 — Does time create sentience, or have a part in creating it?

Round 12. New question from the owner. **Part 1 written and committed before evidence
was gathered.** An independent fairness check of the wording comes next, then neutral
evidence gathering, then independent scoring.

## The problem with the question

Every process takes time. A brain, a thermostat and a waterfall all unfold in time. So
"time is needed" is trivially true of anything that happens, and says nothing about
feeling. For the idea to mean something, time has to do more than be the medium. Below
are six ways it could, each stated so it can fail, and one rival.

## The rival

- **N, nothing special.** Feeling depends on how a system is organised. Time is only the
  medium in which that organisation operates, as it is for digestion. No property of
  time itself is needed over and above "the process runs".

## The conjectures

- **T1, a feeling needs duration.** No instantaneous brain state is felt. A feeling
  exists only across a stretch of sustained activity with a minimum length. Activity
  shorter than that, however strong, is not felt.
- **T2, a feeling needs a direction of time.** What matters is that brain activity is
  irreversible: it runs differently forwards than backwards (it has an "arrow of time",
  which goes with using energy and producing entropy). The more irreversible the
  activity, the more conscious the state.
- **T3, a feeling needs a model of the future.** Feeling arises only in a system that
  anticipates: it represents its own states ahead in time and evaluates them. Good and
  bad are about where things are heading.
- **T4, a feeling needs the experience of time passing.** Experienced "flow" is part of
  what any feeling is. Take away the sense of time and feeling goes too.
- **T5, experience comes in moments.** Experience is built from discrete slices of time
  (one account ties each to a physical event such as a quantum collapse). The slice is
  the basic unit of sentience.
- **T6, real time, not just relative timing.** What matters is the actual physical rate:
  feeling depends on processes running at particular absolute speeds, not merely on the
  order and relative timing of events. The same organisation run much slower or faster
  would not feel the same, or at all.

## Part 1 — Predictions, written first

| # | Situation | The conjecture predicts | N predicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (T1) | **Very brief brain activity** of normal strength | Not felt unless activity is sustained beyond a minimum time; the threshold is about duration, not strength | Felt if it reaches the right circuits with enough strength; duration matters only through total strength |
| 2 (T1) | **Events rearranged after the fact** (a later stimulus changes how an earlier one is experienced) | Expected: experience is assembled over a window, so later input can alter it | Not required |
| 3 (T2) | **Sleep, anaesthesia, disorders of consciousness** | Irreversibility of brain activity falls whenever experience is absent, and stays high whenever experience is reported (dreaming, ketamine) | Irreversibility follows how active and driven the brain is, not experience; it will be high in some states without experience (seizures, task-driven activity) and need not track dreaming |
| 4 (T2) | **Feeling specifically** | Stronger feelings go with more irreversible activity | No such relation |
| 5 (T3) | **People who cannot imagine their future** (severe amnesia after hippocampal damage) | Feeling is reduced or absent | Feeling intact |
| 6 (T3) | **Feelings about now with no future at stake** | Rare or weak | Common and strong |
| 7 (T4) | **States in which time seems to stop or vanish** (deep meditation, some drug states, "flow") | Feeling is absent or greatly reduced | Feeling can be intense |
| 8 (T4) | **People with disordered time perception** | Feeling disturbed in step with time perception | No necessary link |
| 9 (T5) | **Is experience discrete?** | Clear evidence of discrete frames at a fixed rate, tied to a physical rhythm | Continuous, or frame-like only for some tasks |
| 10 (T6) | **Brains run slower or faster** (cooling, warming, cold-blooded animals, drugs that change brain speed) | Feeling changes in kind or is lost when the absolute rate leaves a certain range | Feeling persists; only its speed changes |

**What would count.** Each conjecture is in trouble if its row comes out as N predicts in
good data. N is in trouble if a specifically temporal property (duration threshold,
irreversibility, anticipation, felt flow, discrete frames, absolute rate) tracks reported
experience or feeling in a way that organisation alone does not explain.

**Stated in advance.** Rows 1–3 and 9 concern experience in general; only rows 4–8 bear
on feeling specifically. A marker that tracks consciousness level (row 3) may still be a
marker of something else (arousal, activity), as with the markers examined in round 7.
