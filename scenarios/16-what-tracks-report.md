# Scenario 16 — Which candidates track what people report?

Round 7. **Part 1 written and committed before evidence was gathered.** Scoring is by an
independent adjudicator.

## Why this question

Update 6's lesson: rival claims about what feeling *is* predict the same reports, so
experiments cannot crown one. What experiments can do is strike off candidates that fail
to follow what people say they experienced. This scenario runs that elimination.

## The test cases

Situations where being responsive and having experience come apart, with a report
available at the time or on waking:

| Case | Responsive? | Later report |
|---|---|---|
| Awake | yes | experience |
| Dreaming (REM sleep) | no | vivid experience |
| Non-dreaming sleep, woken with nothing to report | no | none |
| Non-dreaming sleep, woken mid-dream (same sleep stage) | no | experience |
| Ketamine anaesthesia | no | vivid experience |
| Propofol or gas anaesthesia | no | usually none |
| Absence seizure | no (awake-looking) | none |
| "Vegetative" patients who follow commands in a scanner | no | (covert) experience |
| Sleepwalking | acts, but not responsive | usually none |

## The candidates

| Candidate marker | Favoured by |
|---|---|
| A. Overall brain activity / energy use | (baseline) |
| B. Fast (gamma) rhythm and synchrony | binding and some field theories |
| C. Strength or coherence of the brain's electric field | field theories |
| D. Late widespread "ignition" over the front of the brain (P3b wave) | workspace theories |
| E. Activity in a rear "hot zone" of cortex | integrated-information, recurrent-processing theories |
| F. Complexity of the brain's response to a magnetic pulse (PCI) | integrated-information theories; used clinically |
| G. Activity of deep arousal structures (upper brainstem, thalamus) | deep-first theories |
| H. Long-range communication between front and back of cortex | workspace, higher-order theories |

## Part 1 — Predictions, written first

Our expectation for each candidate, before gathering evidence:

| Candidate | Predicted to track report across all cases? | Where we expect it to fail |
|---|---|---|
| A. Overall activity | No | High in seizures and REM alike; ketamine raises it |
| B. Gamma synchrony | No | High in seizures and under some anaesthetics |
| C. Field strength | No | Largest in seizures and deep sleep, when nothing is reported |
| D. Front "ignition" (P3b) | No | Absent when no report is required even though experience occurs |
| E. Rear hot zone | Probably yes | Should distinguish dreaming from dreamless awakenings within one sleep stage |
| F. PCI | Probably yes | Should be high in REM and ketamine, low in dreamless sleep and propofol |
| G. Deep arousal structures | Partly | Active in "vegetative" patients and sleepwalkers without evidence of experience; active in REM |
| H. Front–back communication | Unclear | Reduced in REM yet dreams occur |

**What would count.** A candidate is struck off if it is clearly present in a case with
no experience reported, or clearly absent in a case with experience reported, in good
data. A candidate that survives every case is not thereby "the feeling"; it is a
candidate that has not yet been eliminated.

**Limits stated in advance.** Reports after waking depend on memory. All of this
concerns experience in general (mostly seeing and dreaming); none of the markers has
been tested specifically against *feeling good or bad*.
