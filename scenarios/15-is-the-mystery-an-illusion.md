# Scenario 15 — Is the mystery of feeling produced by a limited self-model?

Round 7. **Part 1 written and committed before evidence was gathered.** Scoring is by an
independent adjudicator.

## The accounts

- **I, illusionism.** Feelings do real work, but the conviction that they have an
  extra, indescribable inner quality is a product of how the brain models itself. The
  brain's picture of its own states is coarse and cannot see how they are made, so they
  seem simple, private and beyond words. Explain that conviction and nothing is left over.
- **Q, realism about felt quality.** Feelings really have an inner quality over and
  above what they do. People find it hard to describe because it is there and is unlike
  anything else, not because their self-monitoring is limited.

## Part 1 — Predictions, written first

| # | Situation | I predicts | Q predicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **People differ in how finely they can monitor their own states** (trained meditators, people high or low in emotional self-awareness) | The sense that feelings are simple and indescribable weakens as monitoring gets finer; experts report feelings as made of parts | The sense of an indescribable quality stays, or sharpens, with better monitoring |
| 2 | **Errors about one's own experience** | Common and large, including about current experience, because the self-model is a rough sketch | May exist, but core judgements about what one is feeling now are reliable |
| 3 | **Across cultures and in children** | The puzzle intuition appears wherever people have this kind of self-model, without being taught philosophy | Also universal (it reflects a real feature) |
| 4 | **Altering the self-model** (depersonalisation, ego-dissolving drugs, deep meditation) | The *seeming* changes: feelings can seem unowned, unreal or not mysterious, while the bodily and behavioural side continues | The quality persists; reports change only in how it is interpreted |
| 5 | **A machine with a comparable self-model** | Would insist, unprompted, that it has indescribable inner states | Its insistence shows nothing either way |
| 6 | **Does the puzzle intuition exist in ordinary people at all?** | Should be widespread | Should be widespread |

Rows 3 and 6 are not expected to separate the accounts; they test a shared assumption.
Row 5 is confounded for any machine trained on human writing.

**What would count.** I is in trouble if row 1 shows the opposite (finer monitoring,
*stronger* sense of indescribable quality) and if row 4 shows the seeming is untouched by
changes to the self-model. Q is in trouble if rows 1 and 4 show the sense of ineffable
quality can be dialled up and down with the self-model while everything else stays put.
If ordinary people do not have the puzzle intuition (row 6 fails), both accounts lose
their starting point and the "mystery" may be a product of philosophical training.
