# Scenario 31 — Round 16: what can value do without feeling?

**Predictions written and committed before evidence is gathered.** Wording to be checked
for fairness before any evidence is scored.

## The question

The owner's hypothesis says feeling is the motivator and thought works out how to serve
it. Since Update 10 the open fork has been: is feeling *the same thing as* a value signal
available to thought, or something *added* to it? Experiments in which value is processed
without being felt can speak to this.

## Definitions (fixed here)

- **Value:** a signal that something is good or bad for the organism, shown by its effect
  on behaviour or physiology (approach, avoidance, effort, learning, skin conductance).
- **Unfelt value:** value that affects behaviour or physiology while the person (a) fails
  an adequate test of awareness of the cue or outcome carrying it, or (b) reports no
  difference in how they feel, when asked. (a) and (b) are different and are reported
  separately; (b) is the one the question is really about, (a) is what most experiments
  manipulate.
- **Simple control:** reflexes, autonomic responses, Pavlovian approach/avoidance,
  response vigour, habit (stimulus–response) learning.
- **Planning:** behaviour needing a model of what leads to what — choosing by inference
  over new combinations, revaluing a goal without re-experiencing it, multi-step choice,
  flexible transfer to a new response.

## Accounts and what each predicts

**I. Identity.** Felt valence *is* value made available to the planner.
1. Unfelt value can drive simple control.
2. Unfelt value cannot drive planning.
3. There are no cases of value guiding planning while the person reports no feeling
   about the options.

**X. Extra ingredient, needed as motivator.** Feeling is something added to value and is
what moves the system.
1. Unfelt value drives at most reflexes and autonomic responses; even simple instrumental
   learning and effort should fail or be much weaker without feeling.
2. Unfelt value cannot drive planning.
3. As I.3.

**N. Not needed.** Value does the work; feeling is a report or by-product (cognition-first
views, illusionism).
1. Unfelt value can drive simple control.
2. Unfelt value can drive planning, if the information is available to the system.
3. Cases of value-guided planning without reported feeling exist (e.g. in people with
   blunted or absent feeling who still choose sensibly).

I and X differ on prediction 1; I/X and N differ on 2 and 3. If 2 holds only because the
*cue* was invisible (so the planner lacked the information for reasons unrelated to
feeling), that does not count for I or X; the adjudicator is asked to judge this.

## What would count

The adjudicator decides whether each prediction is supported, contradicted or undecided,
weighting: adequacy of the awareness test (the known weakness of this literature),
replication, pre-registration, and whether feeling (b) rather than cue awareness (a) was
measured. Null results from underpowered studies do not count as "cannot".

## What the author already believes (disclosed)

From memory, unverified: early reports of learning from invisible rewards have had
replication trouble; critics argue that most "unconscious" influences on decisions vanish
under adequate awareness tests; there are reports of drug self-administration and of
changed consumption without reported feeling; people with blunted emotion after frontal
damage are said to plan poorly in real life while reasoning normally on tests. The author
expects the evidence to be mostly about cue awareness (a), and thin on feeling (b).
