## Where they split

- **Brain emulation on a digital computer** separates function-is-enough (Y) from
  substrate views (N). No one can run this.
- **A simulated creature with a regulated body** separates valuation-as-function (Y)
  from everything else. This *can* be built today, which is uncomfortable: if that
  family is right, feeling machines are easy.
- **Language models** separate cortex-style theories (maybe) from body-regulation
  theories (no).

## What Scenario 8 does to this table

If raw feeling comes from deep body-regulation structures and not from cortex, the
cortex-style column loses weight *as an account of feeling*. That pushes language models
toward N, and leaves the live disagreement between "the regulating function is enough"
and "it has to be living tissue or the right physical structure".

The owner's hypothesis lines up with the valuation column: a language model would be
thought without sentience; a system with real stakes of its own would be the candidate.

## Limit

We cannot measure feeling in any of these systems. The table cannot be tested directly;
it only tells us which *human* evidence matters most, namely whatever separates the
columns: is feeling generated by body-regulation machinery, and does that machinery's
material matter?
