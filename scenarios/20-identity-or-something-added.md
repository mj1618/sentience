# Scenario 20 — Is feeling the value signal itself, or something added to it?

Round 11. **Part 1 written and committed before evidence was gathered.** An independent
fairness check of this wording comes next, before evidence is gathered.

## The accounts

- **ID, identity** (the defensible form of the owner's hypothesis). A feeling of good or
  bad *is* a value signal being available to the planning system. No value signal that
  the planner can use goes unfelt; value signals confined to automatic systems are not
  felt.
- **HO, higher-order** (LeDoux & Brown; Rolls; Lau). Value signals are not in themselves
  felt. A feeling exists only when a further, higher-order representation of the state
  is formed, in a network centred on the outer frontal cortex. Value signals can guide
  even flexible choice without being felt, and a feeling can be reported in the absence
  of the matching value signal.

## Part 1 — Predictions, written first

| # | Situation | ID predicts | HO predicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Value signals in the brain for things a person is not attending to or cannot see** | Present only as automatic valuation; cannot guide flexible choice until available to the planner, at which point a feeling is reported | Present, unfelt, and able to influence later flexible choice |
| 2 | **Flexible, criterion-meeting choice with no reported feeling about the options** | Does not occur | Occurs |
| 3 | **Disrupting the outer frontal cortex** (magnetic pulses, lesions) | Reported feeling and value-guided choice change together or not at all | Reported feeling falls while value-guided choice is preserved |
| 4 | **People who are poor at identifying their own feelings** (alexithymia) | Feelings are present and guide choice normally; only labelling is impaired | Feelings themselves are reduced while value-guided choice is intact |
| 5 | **Reported feelings with no matching value state** (misattributed arousal, placebo emotion, suggestion) | Should be rare; where a feeling is reported, choice behaviour shifts to match | Expected: the higher-order system can construct a feeling the value system does not back, so choice need not follow the report |
| 6 | **Confidence about one's own feelings versus accuracy** | Reports of feeling track value signals closely | Reports can come apart from value signals in either direction |

**What would count.** ID is in trouble if value signals guide criterion-meeting flexible
choice while the person reports no feeling about the options (rows 1, 2), or if frontal
disruption removes reported feeling and leaves value-guided choice intact (row 3). HO is
in trouble if reported feeling and planner-accessible value never come apart in any of
these situations despite adequate tests.

Flexible choice and "unfelt" are defined as in Scenario 18, Part 1b.
