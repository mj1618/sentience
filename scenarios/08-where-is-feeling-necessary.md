# Scenario 8 — Where in the brain is feeling necessary?

Evidence: `research/11-necessity-map.md` (verdicts here are copied from that brief, not
strengthened). Status: **drafted; red-team outcome at the end.**

## Conjecture

> Raw feeling is produced by old, deep brain structures (upper brainstem, hypothalamus
> and neighbours). The cortex elaborates it but does not make it.

Two maps test it. **Necessity:** destroy a structure; does feeling persist?
**Sufficiency:** stimulate a structure in an awake person; do they report a feeling?

## Necessity map (lesions)

| Structure lost | Does feeling persist? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Cerebellum | Yes | one case, feeling not specifically tested |
| Hippocampus | Yes | patients' own reports |
| Amygdala | Yes, except externally triggered fear; all 3 patients still panicked when breathing CO₂ | report (full text read) |
| Insula, both sides | Yes: hunger, thirst, pain, happiness, fear of needles | two patients (full text read); some other cortex spared |
| Anterior cingulate | Yes; surgery there makes pain less bothersome | lesions: report; surgery: uncontrolled series |
| Orbitofrontal / prefrontal | Yes | report |
| Primary touch cortex | Unpleasantness persists though location is lost | one case |
| Whole neocortex (children born without it; decorticate animals) | Emotional *behaviour* persists: preferences, joy, distress, play | **behaviour only, no self-report** |
| Basal ganglia / ventral pallidum | Pleasure and drive lost in some cases | recalled, not verified |
| Hypothalamus | **Thirst abolished** despite the body needing water; other feelings intact | patients' reports, single cases |
| Midbrain "PAG" | Mutism; larger lesions, coma. Feeling never tested | ~3 human cases |
| Upper brainstem core | Everything goes, because the person is in coma | 47 + 36 stroke cases |
| Spinal cord, body-signal nerves | Feeling persists | group studies |

## Sufficiency map (stimulation in awake people)

| Site | What people report |
|---|---|
| Outer cortex generally | Almost nothing emotional. Pain in 1.4% of 4,160 stimulations, from two small sites only |
| Insula | Pain (rear), unpleasantness (front), rarely ecstasy |
| Mid-cingulate | Foreboding with determination: "something bad is going to happen… I knew I had to fight" (identical on six trials, nothing on sham) |
| Amygdala | Fear, sadness, sometimes happiness; varies by person |
| Accumbens / septal region | Smiling, laughter, euphoria (5 of 6 patients) |
| Region below the subthalamic nucleus | Sudden despair and crying, gone within 90 seconds of switching off (one patient; recalled) |
| Hypothalamus | Panic, graded with current |
| PAG | Terror, feeling of impending death, urge to flee (via a review; exact wording not retrieved) |
| Reward pathway (VTA / medial forebrain bundle) | Surge of motivation in 7 of 7 |

## What the maps say (brief's answers)

1. **No structure removes all feeling while leaving the person awake.** The only lesion
   that removes everything removes wakefulness too. In the lesion record, feeling and
   being awake cannot be separated.
2. **Candidate generators** (stimulation produces a feeling *and* damage removes it):
   the **hypothalamus** is the cleanest (panic on stimulation; thirst abolished by
   damage). The **PAG** produces the most intense negative feeling on the map, but
   whether damage removes feeling has never been tested. The **accumbens–pallidum**
   system is a candidate for pleasure (lesion half rests on recalled material).
3. **Disqualified as generators:** amygdala and insula. Stimulating them produces
   feeling, but the feeling survives their loss. They are triggers or routes.
4. **No single structure is shown necessary for both pain-type and pleasure-type
   feeling**, apart from the wakefulness core. Fear, thirst and pleasure come apart.
5. **The candidate generators all predate the cortex** and are present in the earliest
   vertebrates (lamprey: habenula, dopamine system, basal ganglia verified).

## Verdict

**The map favours deep-structures-first for raw feeling. It does not prove it.**

Strongest objection: in every verified case of preserved feeling some cortex survived,
and the only cortex-free cases give behaviour without report. Stimulating a deep
structure in someone with intact cortex might only be triggering a cortical experience.

Strongest objection to the opposite view: it has to retreat to "any remaining cortex
will do", and the cortex that remained does not produce feeling when stimulated.

## Why this matters for the owner's hypothesis

- It is the most direct support so far for "sentience is separate from, and older than,
  thought": the thinking cortex can lose its supposed feeling centres and feeling goes on.
- Update 2 suggested a third ingredient (plain wakeful awareness). Point 1 raises a
  different possibility: **the wakefulness core and the feeling core may be the same
  machinery.** If so, "awake" is not a neutral stage on which feelings appear; it is
  already a bodily-regulation state. This is a conjecture for next round, and it has a
  known counter-case (patients who are awake with flat feeling and an empty mind).

## Experiments that would decide it

1. Test feeling, not just speech, in patients with PAG-region lesions.
2. In children born without cortex, tests that go beyond reflex: learned preferences,
   paying a cost to avoid, trade-offs. (Partly done informally; never systematically.)
3. During brain surgery with deep electrodes, time precisely when the feeling starts
   relative to deep and cortical activity.
