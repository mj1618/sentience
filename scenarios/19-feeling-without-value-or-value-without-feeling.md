# Scenario 19 — Can feeling be removed while the sense of value stays available?

Round 10. **Part 1 written and committed before evidence was gathered.** An independent
fairness check of this wording comes next, before any evidence is scored.

## Why

Round 9 could not separate the owner's hypothesis from its closest rival, because no
study removed the feeling while leaving the knowledge of value in place. This scenario
looks for conditions that do.

## The accounts

- **H.** Felt valence is necessary for flexible, self-initiated action. Knowing that
  something is good or bad, without feeling it, is not enough to move a person to act on
  their own initiative.
- **K\*.** Flexible action runs on learned representations of value that the planning
  system can access. If those are intact and accessible, flexible action is intact,
  whether or not anything is felt.

## What a usable case needs (fixed in advance)

1. **Feeling reduced or absent**, shown by the person's own ratings and, ideally, by
   bodily measures.
2. **Value knowledge intact and accessible**, shown independently: the person can state
   or rank what is better or worse, or what is dangerous.
3. **Flexible action measured** by the round 9 criterion (sensitivity to devaluation or
   to broken contingency, correct first choice among new options, or effort weighed
   against reward with the option to decline), or by clear real-life self-initiated
   behaviour.

A case missing any of the three does not separate the accounts and will be recorded as
"no test".

## Part 1 — Predictions, written first

| # | Condition | H predicts | K\* predicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pain felt without unpleasantness** (asymbolia) with the danger understood | Self-initiated protection lost | Protection preserved wherever the danger is known |
| 2 | **Born without pain**, danger taught by instruction | Protection remains poor despite instruction; improves only through other felt consequences (fear of scolding, sight of blood) | Protection improves in step with instruction and understanding |
| 3 | **Pain-killers that leave pain "there but not bothersome"** | Less self-initiated avoidance at the same sensed intensity | Avoidance unchanged if the person still knows the stimulus is harmful |
| 4 | **Drugs that flatten emotion** (emotional blunting on antidepressants) | Reduced self-initiated pursuit and avoidance, and weaker learning from reward and loss, with knowledge of the task intact | Choices intact if value knowledge is intact |
| 5 | **Depersonalisation** (feelings seem unreal or absent) | Reduced self-initiated, value-guided action | Intact |
| 6 | **Intact in-the-moment pleasure and knowledge, low drive** (as reported in schizophrenia) | Not a test (feeling present) | Not a test |
| 7 | **Felt value and known value in conflict** (the person knows A is better but B feels better; craving against judgement) | Self-initiated choice follows the felt value | Choice follows whichever value representation is stronger or more accessible; no special role for the felt one |
| 8 | **Told that a reward is now worthless, without experiencing it** | Change in choice depends on the instruction producing a change in how the outcome is anticipated to feel | Choice changes at once from the instruction alone |
| 9 | **Bodily feedback removed** (spinal injury, autonomic failure) | Not a test unless feeling is shown reduced | Not a test |

**What would count.** H is in trouble if a case meets all three requirements and
flexible action is intact. K\* is in trouble if a case meets all three and flexible
action is lost. If no case meets all three, the result is "still untested" and the round
ends by specifying the experiment that would create such a case.
