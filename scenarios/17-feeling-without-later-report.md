# Scenario 17 — Feeling signalled in the moment, in states without later report

Round 8. **Part 1 written and committed before evidence was gathered.** Scoring is by an
independent adjudicator.

## Why

Round 7 found that no marker of experience has been tested against feeling, and that
patients under general anaesthesia can signal pain in real time and not remember it.
States like that cross the report barrier for *feeling* specifically. This scenario
collects what is known about them.

## Accounts

- **D, deep-first.** Feeling is generated by deep structures. Felt pain should follow the
  incoming injury signal and how well it is blocked (pain-killing drugs), largely
  regardless of the state of the cortex.
- **C, cortex-makes-it-felt.** Pain is felt only when the cortical network is in a
  working state. Signals of pain under anaesthesia should come only at moments when
  cortical measures look "awake-like".

## Part 1 — Predictions, written first

| # | Situation | D predicts | C predicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Isolated-forearm responders under general anaesthesia**: do cortical recordings differ between those who respond and those who do not, or at moments when pain is signalled? | Little or no cortical difference; pain signals follow surgical stimulation and pain-killer dose | Clear cortical difference: responders and pain moments show awake-like cortical activity |
| 2 | **Separating the two drug effects.** Pain-killers (opioids) and sleep-inducing agents can be varied independently | Signalled pain falls with pain-killer dose and is little affected by the sleep-inducing dose, as long as the patient can respond at all | Signalled pain falls with the sleep-inducing dose |
| 3 | **Light sedation with amnesia** (procedures done under drugs that block memory): pain reported at the time versus recalled later | Real-time pain common; later recall low | Same (not discriminating) |
| 4 | **Bodily signs of pain without any response** under deep anaesthesia (heart rate, movement, stress hormones) | These may be felt but unreportable; cannot be decided | These are unfelt reflexes |
| 5 | **Emotion in dreams** versus brain measures at the time | Dream emotion follows deep and limbic activity | Dream emotion follows frontal cortical activity |
| 6 | **Patients with very little cortical function who show pain behaviour** (prolonged disorders of consciousness) | Pain behaviour indicates felt pain | Pain behaviour without cortical markers is reflex |

Rows 3, 4 and 6 are not expected to separate the accounts; they are included to
establish how often "nothing reported later" hides something signalled at the time.

**What would count.** D is in trouble if pain is signalled only at moments of awake-like
cortical activity and tracks the sleep-inducing dose. C is in trouble if pain is
signalled with cortical measures in the unconscious range and tracks pain-killer dose.
