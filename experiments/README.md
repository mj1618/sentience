# Experiments nobody has run

Sketches of real-world experiments that came out of the investigation. Each is a
starting point for someone with a lab, not a finished protocol. **None has been
reviewed by a specialist.** Sensitivities are our arithmetic.

## 1. Which feelings go first?

- **Question.** Is feeling one thing or several? (Scenario 6.1)
- **Design.** Volunteers under slowly deepening sedation (one agent at a time: propofol,
  then separately nitrous oxide, ketamine). At each step, brief standard probes for pain
  unpleasantness, itch, air hunger (mild CO₂), thirst (after salt load), and pleasantness
  (sweet taste, warmth), each rated separately for *intensity* and *how much it matters*.
- **Readout.** The dose at which each rating falls to half.
- **Interpretation.** One shared threshold: one source. Clearly separate thresholds, in
  different orders for different drugs: several sources.
- **Main confound.** Ability to rate declines with sedation; needs a non-affective
  rating task as control.

## 2. Does feeling follow the level of a need, or its rate of change?

- **Question.** Scenario 9. Existing thirst data confound the two.
- **Design.** Hold blood CO₂ flat at three levels for ten minutes each with ventilation
  fixed; separately ramp through the same levels at two speeds. Continuous discomfort
  rating.
- **Interpretation.** Rate-only: discomfort decays toward zero on every plateau.
  Level-only: flat on plateaus, identical on fast and slow ramps at the same level.
- **Main confound.** Slow adaptation of the sensors themselves.

## 3. A quantum coin with something real at stake

- **Question.** Does anything tilt genuinely random events toward what feels better?
  (Scenarios 4 and 7.) Existing tests used trivial stakes or computer pseudo-randomness.
- **Design.** A certified quantum random source decides, trial by trial, between a
  pleasant and an unpleasant outcome that the subject actually cares about. In animals:
  sweet versus bitter drop for thirsty mice, fully automated. A random mask is applied
  to the source so any bias in the device cancels. Sham arm: same device, outcome
  delivered to an empty cage.
- **Sensitivity.** Five-sigma detection of tilt ε needs about (2.5/ε)² trials.
  100 mice × 1,000 trials a day × 60 days = 6 million trials → ε ≈ 10⁻³.
- **Interpretation.** A null narrows the valence-aimed chooser at the level of external
  events. It says nothing about a chooser confined to events inside the brain.
- **Main confounds.** Device drift; any path by which behaviour could influence the
  source.

## 4. The lever test beyond rats

- **Question.** Does a planning system that must *experience* an outcome to update its
  value exist outside mammals? (Scenario 3.)
- **Design.** Train the animal to work for a food while hungry; feed to fullness on that
  food away from the task; test in extinction (no food delivered). Then allow one taste
  while full and test again.
- **Species.** Honeybee, shore crab, octopus, zebrafish, with a rat arm as positive
  control.
- **Interpretation.** Rat-like pattern (no drop until re-tasting): the two-system
  arrangement is old and widespread. Immediate drop: the animal's action system reads
  body state directly. No drop even after re-tasting: habit only.

## 5. Xenon isotopes, done properly

- **Question.** Does a nuclear property with no ordinary chemical effect change
  anaesthetic potency? (Scenario 7; original result is weak.)
- **Design.** No second anaesthetic. Gases interleaved within each day, cylinders
  coded. Blends (0, 50, 100% spin-bearing) to look for a dose-response. Body temperature
  logged. Pressure chamber if needed to reach full effect.
- **Interpretation.** Not a test of any theory of mind. A clean positive would show a
  spin-sensitive step somewhere between gas and behaviour.

## 6. Deep stimulation with a feeling question after every trial

- **Question.** Is feeling generated in deep structures, or only made felt by cortex? (Scenario 11; proposed by the independent adjudicator.)
- **Who.** Patients who already have electrodes in the midbrain "PAG" region (for chronic pain) or the rear hypothalamus (for cluster headache).
- **Design.** Sham trials mixed in; current stepped up gradually; heart rate, skin response and breathing recorded; after every trial a fixed question about what was felt and how strongly, plus a continuous dial. Scalp recording alongside to time the onset.
- **Interpretation.** Deep-first predicts a feeling whenever the body's response is driven, growing with current and locked to the deep activity. Cortex-makes-it-felt predicts trials with bodily response and no feeling, with feeling tracking frontal and parietal recruitment.

## 7. Same stimulation, felt on some trials and not others

- **Question.** As above.
- **Who.** Epilepsy patients with electrodes in amygdala, insula or cingulate and also in frontal cortex.
- **Design.** Repeat identical stimulation many times. In an existing study the same current produced a feeling in only 2 of 9 patients. Compare what spread where on felt and unfelt trials.
- **Interpretation.** Cortex view: the difference is frontal recruitment. Deep-first: the difference is spread to hypothalamus and midbrain.
