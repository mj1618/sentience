# Round 9 — fairness pre-check of Scenario 18, Part 1

Made before scoring; no evidence file was read. Sources: Berridge 2003 (*Parsing reward*), Berridge & Robinson 2016 and LeDoux & Brown 2017 (abstracts, Europe PMC); otherwise general knowledge.

## 1. W is not Berridge's view as stated

- **"Liking" is not felt pleasure.** Berridge puts the word in quotes for the core hedonic reaction, which can itself be unconscious; conscious pleasure is a further step. Calling felt pleasure "liking" misattributes.
- **'Wanting' can be unfelt, not is unfelt.** It is normally experienced as craving or urge. The claim is only that it can occur without awareness and without 'liking'.
- **Two kinds of wanting.** Berridge 2003 separates "implicit incentive salience 'wanting'" from "cognitive incentive goals". Incentive salience is cue-triggered, Pavlovian, mesolimbic-dopamine, and unaffected by what the person knows or expects. Cognitive wanting is declarative, cortical, act–outcome, and normally aimed at outcomes expected to be liked.
- **Consequence.** Berridge does not claim unfelt 'wanting' produces devaluation-sensitive, planned action. He would reject the W cell in row 1 ("goal-directed") and "Works" in row 2. He would accept rows 3 and 7 only as cue-triggered or compulsive pursuit, and has no stated position on rows 4, 5 or 8. For flexible action, real W sits close to H.

## 2. K is a strawman

Nobody holds that declarative knowledge without any value signal moves action. Dickinson and Balleine are not K: their incentive-learning account makes goal value depend on experienced hedonic reactions, which favours H.

**Strongest real version, K\*** (model-based control; LeDoux; Rolls): flexible action runs on learned value representations (orbitofrontal/ventromedial) available to a planner. These are acquired through affective teaching signals. Conscious feeling is a separate cortical construction, not the causal step. Lose the value signal and action fails, whether or not feeling is also lost. (LeDoux allows feelings some influence on deliberate choice, so he is not a pure case.)

K\* predicts impairment in rows 4, 5, 8 and 9, the same as H. Those rows refute only the strawman.

## 3. H is not yet able to fail

"Flexible, thought-guided" lets any unfelt-reward effect be reclassified as automatic afterwards. "Felt valence" is also open: if felt craving counts, row 7 cannot hurt H. Row 6's "go together" over-commits H, since necessity does not imply that apathy requires lost pleasure.

**Operational criterion (fixed now).** Behaviour is *flexible/goal-directed* only if it shows at least one of:

1. sensitivity to outcome devaluation, tested without feedback;
2. sensitivity to contingency degradation;
3. correct first-trial choice between novel options or by a never-reinforced response;
4. effort allocated by a cost–benefit comparison (reward × cost interaction, or a choice to decline).

*Automatic* means cue-triggered approach, general invigoration, response vigour scaling with cue magnitude alone, or responding acquired over repeated reinforced trials.

*Unfelt* means chance-level objective detection and no change in reported affect, anticipatory or consummatory. Felt craving, urge and dread count as felt valence.

Classification is done blind to the result. **H fails** on one replicated finding that is flexible and unfelt by these definitions.
