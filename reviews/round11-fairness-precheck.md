# Round 11 — fairness pre-check of Scenario 20, Part 1

Made before scoring; no research/20-* file read. Full text: LeDoux & Brown 2017; Rolls 2020 (PMC7154119). Abstracts only: Brown, Lau & LeDoux 2019; Lau & Rosenthal 2011; LeDoux & Daw 2018; Rounis et al. 2010; Smittenaar et al. 2013; Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry 2022. Carruthers and Dickinson: general knowledge, unverified.

## 1. HO is not stated as its proponents hold it

- **"HO" is two views.** *HO-L* (LeDoux & Brown; Lau): feeling is a re-representation in a working-memory network. *HO-R* (Rolls): feeling arises when a state enters the multistep syntactic planning system. Rolls writes that we are conscious of emotional states "because they would be being used (or are available to be used)" by that system. **That is ID's own sentence.**
- **Flexible choice without feeling.** LeDoux & Brown 2017 claim only that subcortical defensive circuits produce bodily and defensive responses nonconsciously, and that nonconscious working memory is "more robust than previously observed". They make no claim about value-guided goal-directed choice. LeDoux & Daw 2018 allow goal-directed action by "implicit" forecasting. Rolls places single-step action–outcome ("goal-dependent") learning on the implicit route, but multistep planning on the conscious one. So HO claims unfelt defensive, habitual and at most single-step goal-directed control; nobody claims unfelt multistep planning.
- **Site.** Not "outer frontal cortex". LeDoux & Brown's network is lateral and medial prefrontal, parietal and insular; their figure includes orbitofrontal and ventromedial cortex. Rolls names no lateral site.
- **Row 3.** No proponent predicts "feeling abolished, value-guided choice spared". Lau's finding (Rounis 2010) is that dorsolateral disruption lowers visibility ratings and metacognitive sensitivity while simple discrimination is unchanged. The same region supports model-based control (Smittenaar 2013), so HO-L expects planning to suffer too.
- **Row 4.** The HO cell is invented. LeDoux & Brown mention alexithymia once, as showing "primarily quantitative changes in subjective experience". Alexithymia concerns naming discrete emotions, not good/bad valence.
- **Row 5.** Half right. They hold that "fear can exist independent of the lower-order inputs". But they also say emotions, once assembled, "can contribute to decision making". HO predicts choice **does** follow the constructed feeling. Misattributed arousal is reinterpretation of a present lower-order state, not an empty higher-order state.
- **Row 6.** HO's cell has no direction and cannot fail.

## 2. ID

- **Holders.** Closest: Carruthers (valence as a globally broadcast, nonconceptual representation of value) and workspace readings. Rolls at the planner level. **Not Dickinson**: the hedonic interface is causal (feeling sets goal value through incentive learning), and stored value is later used without current feeling. **Not Cleeremans**: he claims experience has value and does work (round 10's H), within a higher-order framework.
- **Fairness.** Acceptable to Carruthers, provided "automatic systems" is not read to settle where single-step goal-directed control belongs.
- **Circularity.** As worded, availability is inferred from the choice (rows 1–2) or from the report (rows 5–6); ID is then true by definition. It can be made non-circular: define availability (A, below) by breadth of use, excluding the affect rating and the choice under test. ID then asserts A ⇒ reported feeling, which can fail. If the authors decline this, ID is a definition and nothing is scored.

## 3. Rows

| Row | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1, 2 | One-directional: can refute ID, cannot refute HO. Discriminates only if A is shown; devaluation-sensitive choice alone does not show A. |
| 3 | Uninterpretable as written: the site serves both report and planning; "change together" is expected by both. Usable only reversed (row 3′). |
| 4 | No test. ID's "only labelling is impaired" is an escape via unreported feeling. |
| 5 | Cannot fail for either. Both predict choice follows the constructed feeling; under ID a suggested feeling *is* planner-available value. |
| 6 | Cannot fail for HO; under ID "value signal" is undefined independently of report. |

**Report.** Every discriminating outcome is a difference in reported affect at matched use. On both accounts the report is issued by the planner or higher-order system, so ID can call a lost report a labelling failure and HO can call it lost feeling. The outcome is interpretable only by convention: self-report is the criterion for both sides. **No observation separates ID from HO without self-report.** No-report markers are calibrated on report.

## 4. Amendment (append before scoring)

**Definitions.**
- **A, available**: the same value drives at least two distinct criterion-meeting uses other than the affect rating, one of them multistep or first-trial novel (criterion 3); or a sustained, decodable frontoparietal value code. Single-step devaluation sensitivity alone is not A.
- **F, felt**: self-rated good/bad valence, anticipatory and consummatory; emotion naming does not count. Floor = unfelt. Neither side may appeal to unreported feeling.
- **HO** is scored as **HO-L**. **HO-R is declared not separable from ID** except on row 2′.

**Corrected table.**

| # | Situation | ID | HO-L | HO-R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1′ | A met, performance matched, across conditions | F equal | F can differ | as ID |
| 2′ | Single-step goal-directed choice (criteria 1, 2, 4), A not met | silent | may be unfelt | may be unfelt |
| 3′ | Bilateral lateral/polar prefrontal disruption, verified by a metacognitive drop | F unchanged if A unchanged | F falls or decouples from value | silent |
| 4, 5, 6 | As Part 1 | no test | no test | no test |

**Scoring.**
- **ID fails**: A met with F at floor, replicated (row 1′); or F falls with A unchanged (row 3′).
- **HO-L fails**: verified disruption with F and A unchanged (row 3′). If F and A never dissociate across adequately powered matched designs (row 1′), score "strained", not failed.
- A not shown, or A and F both change: **no test**.

## 5. If nothing separates them

If F and A never come apart, HO-L is strained; against HO-R nothing observable decides. The choice then rests on argument: whether a state can be felt without being represented as one's own; whether empty higher-order states are coherent; parsimony; and whether "feeling" in ID is a discovery or a stipulation.
