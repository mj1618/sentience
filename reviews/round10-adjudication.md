## 2. The decisive question

**No.** No case was found in which felt valence was at floor by self-report and flexible action on criteria 1–4 was measured. I did not repeat the gatherer's searches, so the accurate wording is "none found", not "none exists".

**Blunted-but-not-floor with intact action:** strictly, not found either.

In depersonalisation feeling was attenuated (unpleasant sounds only, d=0.81) but action was not measured, and real-life functioning (Michal 2016) is impaired and confounded by depression and anxiety. In Langley 2023 model-based control was intact but participants reported no blunting. In spinal injury and autonomic failure feeling was not reduced.

Weight against H: effectively none. Even a genuine case would deserve little: H is a necessity claim, so residual feeling suffices, and easy laboratory tasks could mask a graded loss. It would count only as dose-response evidence (large rated reduction, no reduction in a sensitive action measure); nothing here reaches that. Equally, H may not claim partial blunting with impaired functioning as support.

## 3. Row 8

**Mediation was not tested by anyone.** No study rated anticipated affect after a purely instructed devaluation; no satiation study reported the individual-level relation between rating change and choice change in the sections read.

- **Choice change without affect change (against H):** none demonstrated. Instructed studies leave affect unmeasured. Korb 2020 shows grip force falling with ratings unchanged, but that is action reduced with feeling present, which a necessity claim permits, and not a choice task. In Hogarth 2015 choice shifted with mood (r=0.43), itself an affect change; desire was not re-rated. Not a counter-example.
- **Affect change without choice change:** yes. Tricomi 3-day group; Pool 2022, where 212 of 306 were classed as not adjusting responding despite a site-level fall in liking (individual liking changes not reported). This does not hurt H (necessary is not sufficient); K\*-HO calls it habit.

## 4. Overall shift and the identity point

**Shift: none.** Ratio 1:1. The null is by absence of measurement, not by measured co-variation. Nobody measured feeling and value signal together alongside flexible action, so the round does not show they never come apart and does not favour identity over K\*-HO on evidence either.

**On K\*-ID.** My view: the owner's hypothesis, as it can currently be defended, is an identity claim.

1. Once K\* is stated correctly (the planner's value signal, not verbal knowledge), every distinctive prediction of H dissolved. H predicts nothing that K\*-ID does not.
2. Against K\*-HO, H's only content is that feeling is not a further state beyond planner-accessible value. That is the identity claim.
3. "Felt valence is necessary for flexible action" then holds by definition, and cannot be cited as a finding about where sentience comes from.

For the project:

- State H as "felt valence is the value representation being accessible to the planner", labelled a position in the first-order versus higher-order debate, not an empirically supported causal hypothesis.
- Do not describe rounds 9 and 10 as tests H survived.
- Own the commitment: on identity, any system with planner-accessible value signals has felt valence. If the owner rejects that because feeling is meant to be an extra ingredient, H is a causal claim about that ingredient with no evidence yet distinguishing it from K\*-HO.
- The live empirical contrast is identity versus higher-order: can reported feeling and the value signal be dissociated?

## 5. Sharpest separating experiment

Preregistered, one protocol, two arms.

- **Participants:** (a) people screened for floor trial-wise ratings of anticipated and experienced pleasure, wanting, dread and anticipated regret toward the task outcomes (severe numbing in depersonalisation disorder, severe anhedonia), with matched patient controls; (b) healthy volunteers.
- **Task:** instrumental training for two real consumable outcomes; one devalued by instruction only; choice test in extinction; plus effort-for-reward with the option to decline.
- **Before test:** anticipated-affect ratings per outcome, with rating order counterbalanced to check that rating does not create the affect.
- **Requirement 2, independent of choice and report:** incentive-compatible bids and anticipatory skin conductance or cue-triggered transfer; orbitofrontal imaging if available.
- **Fixed readings:** floor ratings with devaluation-sensitive choice: H (and identity) fail. Floor ratings, intact bids and autonomic signal, prompted choice lost: K\*-HO fails. In arm (b), an equivalence-bounded test in participants whose anticipated affect did not change: a choice shift counts against H; none, with full mediation, against K\*-HO.

If screening finds no one at floor, report that as the result.
