# Response to the round 4 red team

Reviews: `round4-map-redteam.md`, `round4-chooser-conjectures-redteam.md`. Reviewers read
the key papers in full text (through a summarising fetcher; quotes are second-hand).

## Withdrawn

| Claim | Why |
|---|---|
| "The map favours deep-structures-first" (S8) | The rival view (cortex makes it *felt*; deep structures supply unfelt inputs; LeDoux & Brown) predicts every preserved-feeling case, because both key patients kept the outer frontal and parietal cortex that view relies on. Likelihood ratio near 1. A third reading, feeling is distributed with redundancy, is what one patient's own authors concluded, and we had not listed it. |
| "Hypothalamus is the cleanest candidate generator" (S8) | Panic on stimulation and loss of thirst are different feelings from different parts. One of the two thirst cases had no demonstrated hypothalamic damage; in the other the authors blame failure of the salt *sensor*. That is exactly the ground on which we had demoted the amygdala. |
| The "generator" test itself (S8) | Stimulation spreads to distant networks; a lesion cannot tell a generator from a necessary input. Applied evenly, **no structure passes**. |
| "Outer cortex is nearly silent to stimulation" (S8) | The 1.4% figure was for pain only. Our own source lists mirth, anxiety and sadness from several cortical sites. One fear series was amygdala, misfiled. |
| "Wakefulness core and feeling core may be the same machinery" (S8) | Coma prevents testing; that is all the lesion record shows. We also left out the mirror case: patients who are awake with the brainstem intact and the cortex destroyed, and show no sign of awareness. |
| "If feeling is deep, language models are less likely to feel" (S10) | Does not follow. Where something sits in a mammal says nothing about which computation matters. |
| "Future-aimed and hidden-fact-aimed choosers are ruled out" (round 3 response, C19, Update 3) | The precognition experiments used computer pseudo-random targets (so they test "hidden fact", not "future quantum event"), and the stake was an untailored erotic picture versus a grey screen for a paid online panel with a mean age of 49; whether participants wanted it was never measured. Four study arms came out 49.48, 50.10, 49.65, 50.07 and differ from each other more than chance allows. What the data support: guessing a hidden computer draw at trivial stakes is not helped by more than about +0.15%. **No per-event limit follows.** |
| "Rate, not level: refuted as the whole story" (S9) | The thirst evidence came from steadily rising ramps, so level and rate were confounded. It counts against a *pure* rate account only. Verdict: **untested**; the deciding experiment (hold the level flat) has not been run. |
| Controllable pain "rated higher" as evidence (S9) | "Control" meant choosing the next intensity, not escaping; intensity was rated, not unpleasantness. |

## What stands

- **Insula, anterior cingulate and amygdala are not necessary for reported feeling.** Two patients, papers read in full. This lowers theories that centre feeling on those regions. With caveats: feeling was altered in both (one muted, one exaggerated), pain was not formally tested in one, both were densely amnesic, and decades of rewiring are possible.
- Children born without cortex: "near-total", not total (17 of 22 in one series had some tissue).
- Machines table: useful as a map of disagreements only. The "valuation" column is our own construct; no named theorist says a simulated homeostat feels.

## Credence shifts justified (reviewer's, accepted)

Insula/cingulate/amygdala-centred accounts: down substantially. Cortex-first vs
deep-first: essentially none. Function vs substrate: none. Language models: none.

## The lesson of four rounds

Every question we have pushed on ends at the same wall. We know about feeling only
through **report**, and report needs the thinking, speaking parts of the brain. So for
anything that cannot report (deep structures on their own, children without cortex,
animals, machines) we have only behaviour, and every theory explains the behaviour.
Call it the **report barrier**. Evidence that does not cross it cannot separate the
theories. Round 5 looks for designs that cross it.

## Process
