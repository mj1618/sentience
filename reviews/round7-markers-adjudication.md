# Round 7 — Independent adjudication: which markers track report?

Spot-checked via Europe PMC: Sarasso 2015, Cohen 2020, Siclari 2017, Murphy 2011, Sanders
2017. All abstracts match the evidence file. One addition: Sanders 2017 also states that
**5 of 12 isolated-forearm responders signalled pain**; none recalled anything.

## 1. Fairness and design

**The "later report" column is weaker than the scenario states.**

- *Propofol/gas, "usually none"*: defensible only as "usually no recall at surgical depth".
  22–28% report dreaming on emergence; 74% of interviews at lighter propofol sedation
  contained experience (Radek 2018); 4.6% respond on the isolated forearm and recall nothing.
  Should read "often none; unknown how much is forgotten".
- *Sleepwalking, "usually none"*: wrong on current data. Cataldi 2024: 81% of interviewed
  episodes had some experience (but mostly confusional arousals; nobody left the bed).
- *Absence seizure, "none"*: no report study was found; it rests on behaviour, the very
  thing the cases are meant to dissociate from experience.
- *Command-following patients*: not a report. Experience is inferred from a neural
  behaviour; a different criterion from the other eight cases.
- *NREM "nothing to report"*: cannot be told from a forgotten dream.

**Asymmetry.** Positive reports (wake, REM, NREM dream, ketamine) are firm; every negative
case is "no recall". So "absent while experience reported" is a strong strike; "present
while nothing reported" is a weaker one. The scenario treats them as equal.

**Marker sharpness.** Only D (P3b) and F (PCI, cutoff 0.31) are sharply defined. A
conflates flow with metabolism; B local power with long-range synchrony; C was never
measured as field strength (EEG amplitude stands in; coherence untested); E is
operationalised as low posterior delta; G lumps brainstem with thalamus; H changes sign with
the measure used. No threshold for "clearly present" was pre-set.

**Empty cells.** About 26 of 72 cells are empty, and more are stage- or group-level only.
The command-follower column is empty for all eight markers. Missing: A (N+, CMD, SW);
B (ABS, CMD); C (KET, CMD); D (N+, KET, PROP, ABS, CMD, SW); E (KET, CMD; PROP, ABS
indirect); F (N+, ABS, SW, CMD); G (N+, CMD); H (N+, ABS, CMD, SW). The only within-stage
dream/no-dream contrast is one study (Siclari 2017), covering B, C, E alone.

## 2. Verdicts

Standard applied to all: a strike needs the marker and the report status from the same
study, or a report status that is not in dispute. Cross-study inference onto a contested
report column gives "insufficient, leaning struck".

| Marker | Verdict | Deciding case and source | Authors' prediction |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Overall activity | INSUFFICIENT (leaning struck) | Stage-level only; NREM −23 to −44% while ~50% of NREM awakenings yield reports, never split by report. Absence data mixed. Tracks in disorders of consciousness (Stender 2016, 94%). | "No": unconfirmed; rationale faulty |
| B. Gamma | INSUFFICIENT (leaning struck for power) | Gamma power persists through propofol loss of responsiveness (Murphy 2011, n=8), but no reports collected. Posterior high-frequency power tracks dreaming within NREM. Seizure data absent. | "No": unconfirmed |
| C. Field strength (as amplitude) | STRUCK OFF | N−: posterior slow-wave power ~10× higher with no experience than with a dream, same subjects (Siclari 2017). Anti-tracks. | Right |
| D. P3b | STRUCK OFF | Awake: stimulus seen, no P3b when no report required (Pitts 2014; Cohen 2020). | Right |
| E. Rear hot zone | SURVIVES SO FAR | Tracks in REM, N−, N+, SW (Siclari 2017; Cataldi 2024) | "Probably yes": not contradicted |
| F. PCI | SURVIVES SO FAR | Tracks in W, REM, N−, KET, PROP (Sarasso 2015; Casarotto 2016) | "Probably yes": not contradicted |
| G. Deep arousal structures | INSUFFICIENT (leaning struck) | Thalamus raised in absence seizures (Bai 2010) but no report data; NREM stage-level only; does not separate VS from MCS (Stender 2015). | "Partly": rationale wrong |
| H. Front–back communication | INSUFFICIENT (leaning struck) | Feedback reduced under ketamine (Lee 2013, n=30) but no reports collected, and other measures rise (Vlisides 2017). REM mixed. | "Unclear": unfalsifiable |

**Predictions scored.** Two confirmed (C, D). Two not contradicted but mostly untested (E, F).
Three "will fail" predictions (A, B, G in part) are **not established** at the strike
standard; H's "unclear" earns no credit. Specific errors:

- A: "ketamine raises it" is false for whole-brain metabolism at anaesthetic dose (Långsjö
  2005), and in any case high activity in REM and ketamine is *tracking*, since both have
  experience. The stated rationale does not describe a failure.
- G: "active in vegetative patients" is contradicted: thalamic and brainstem metabolism is
  reduced (Stender 2015). "Sleepwalkers without experience" relied on the wrong report column.
- B, C: the seizure half of each rationale has no report data behind it.

## 3. Are the survivors really tested?

- **E** was measured against report in 4 of 9 cases (REM, N−, N+, SW), all from one
  laboratory and method; a small independent attempt failed (Wong 2020, 59%, underpowered,
  not like-for-like).
- **F** was measured in 5 of 9 (W, REM, N−, KET, PROP), all one research group, 6–18 people
  per unresponsive condition, cutoff fitted in-sample, and the no-report conditions were
  *selected* for giving no report. Its decisive test, NREM with a dream report (N+), is
  absent from the evidence gathered.

So "survives" is roughly half "unmeasured".

**Memory or reportability confound.** E is exposed: it predicts what is *reported on
waking*, so it may mark memory encoding. Partial mitigation: "experience without recalled
content" showed the same pattern (n=20), but wholly forgotten experience remains
indistinguishable from none. Neither E nor F is a marker of reporting machinery. F may index
*capacity* for experience rather than its occurrence, which N+ would test.

## 4. Feeling specifically

None of the eight markers has been tested against valence. Everything concerns experience
in general (seeing, dreaming). What exists on felt pain points away from these markers:

- Sanders 2017: 12 of 260 anaesthetised patients responded to command; 5 signalled pain;
  none recalled it. Felt pain can be reported in the moment and leave no later report, so the
  "later report" method systematically misses it.
- Gaskell 2017 (same cohort, verified abstract): frontal EEG patterns and depth indices did
  not distinguish responders from non-responders.
- Dream emotion: no marker was contrasted between pleasant and unpleasant dreams.

Nothing gathered shows that E or F tracks the presence of pain or pleasure.

## 5. Implications for theory families

- **Workspace/ignition**: the P3b is struck off as a marker. The theory is not refuted;
  later non-frontal activity still predicts sampled reports (Sergent 2021). The specific
  claim that the P3b is the signature is refuted. Front–back communication (H) is unsettled.
- **Field strength**: amplitude is struck off; any field theory committed to "bigger field,
  more experience" is refuted on that commitment. Theories that appeal to field *structure*
  or coherence are untested here, since nobody measured it.
- **Integrated information / complexity**: its two markers survive, on thin, mostly
  single-group data. Survival does not single out this family: recurrent-processing
  accounts predict the same.
- **Deep-first arousal**: neither struck nor supported.
- **Overall activity**: not cleanly struck; no within-stage test exists.

## 6. Plain language

We asked which brain measurements rise and fall with what people say they experienced
across sleep, dreams, anaesthesia and seizures. Two were clearly ruled out: the sheer size of
the brain's electrical waves (largest in dreamless sleep) and a late frontal brain wave that
turns out to appear only when people are asked to respond. Two were not ruled out: activity
at the back of the cortex, and how complex the brain's echo is after a magnetic pulse. But
those two were checked in only about half the situations, mostly by one laboratory each, and
"nothing reported" may just mean "nothing remembered". Four others could not be judged either
way. Most importantly, none of this tells us about feeling good or bad: the one relevant
finding is that some anaesthetised patients signalled pain and remembered nothing afterwards.
