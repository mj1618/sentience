**Row 1′.** The row's precondition, A met, is satisfied nowhere. HO-L is not "strained": that label requires adequately powered matched designs in which F and A fail to dissociate, and no such design exists for valence. The null is by absence of measurement.

**Row 2′.** ID is silent by construction. HO-L's "may be unfelt" cannot fail, and cannot be confirmed here because F was never rated under masking and goal-directed status was never verified by devaluation. I do not award CONSISTENT. Unscored: in Garofalo the goal-like component (outcome-specific transfer) vanished for unseen cues, which cuts mildly against unfelt goal-directed control, but the manipulation is cue visibility, not feeling.

**Row 3′.** No study meets the row: bilateral, verified by a metacognitive drop, with F or A measured. Lapate is the nearest and fails four ways: unilateral; the drop was for face orientation, not emotion, and the efficiency measure was p=0.094; PANAS mood is not F as defined; A was not assessed. The HO-L-fail rule (verified disruption, F and A unchanged) is therefore not triggered. The likeability bias does not score for HO-L as "F decouples from value": it is equally a regulation failure that changed the value. Lesion data (rated feeling not reduced; conceded by proponents in Cushing 2024) lean against HO-L informally, but have no metacognitive check, no A, and unverified laterality. The verification tool itself is unsettled (Rounis not replicated by Bor; Bor's null inconclusive).

## 2. Decisive questions

- **(a) A met with F at floor, replicated?** No. Not once, replicated or otherwise.
- **(b) F falling with A unchanged?** No. Rainville 1997 (abstract) changed rated unpleasantness by hypnosis but took no behavioural measure. Borckardt 2011 and Taylor 2012 used excitatory, unilateral stimulation with no choice measure. No study lowered rated feeling by prefrontal disruption and measured choice. Tricomi/Pool (round 10) show ratings falling with habitual responding unchanged; habit is not A.
- **(c) Verified prefrontal disruption with F and A both unchanged?** No. Lapate is closest and does not qualify (above).

"None found", not "none exists"; I did not repeat the searches.

## 3. Overall shift

**None. Ratio 1:1.** Most informative finding: no located study measures A and F in the same people under a manipulation of either; and the one causal tool row 3′ depends on (bilateral dlPFC theta-burst lowering metacognition) did not replicate.

## 4. Stepping back

**What the three rounds establish.** (i) The owner's hypothesis survives only as an identity claim. (ii) That claim is Rolls's theory in other words. (iii) It differs from LeDoux–Brown/Lau only in predicted differences in self-report, read by a convention both sides must accept. No row in three rounds amounted to a test. What has been learned is where the hypothesis sits in an existing debate, not where sentience comes from.

**Is an empirical question left?** One, narrow and conditional. *Holding fixed that the same value drives several independent uses, including a novel or multistep one, can rated good/bad feeling be moved?*

**The experiment.** Within-subject, preregistered.  A established per condition by incentive-compatible bids, a first-trial novel inference or two-step choice using the same value, and a frontoparietal value decoder. F by trial-wise anticipatory and consummatory valence ratings. Two manipulations: Rainville-style hypnotic suggestion to reduce unpleasantness; and bilateral lateral prefrontal theta-burst against vertex, with a perceptual metacognition check in the same session. Equivalence bounds fixed in advance. Readings: F falls, A equivalent — ID fails. Metacognitive drop verified, F and A both equivalent — HO-L fails. Both move — no test. Run the hypnosis arm first; it is cheap.

**Limits even if it is run.** It cannot touch Rolls's version. Its result is readable only if self-report is the criterion; drop that convention and each side has an escape. And it would decide which *function* accompanies reported feeling, not why that function is felt. On the owner's question in its strong form, the project has reached the edge of what evidence decides.

**The philosophical arguments that then bear.**
- *Stipulation or discovery.* If "felt" just means "available to the planner", ID is true by definition and explains nothing.
- *Empty higher-order states.* HO-L implies a feeling can exist with no first-order state; whether that is coherent is the standard objection to it.
- *Ownership.* Whether a state can be felt without being represented as one's own.
- *Parsimony.* ID posits one state fewer.
- *Scope.* ID attributes feeling to any system with planner-available value (many animals, possibly machines); HO-L restricts it. Without report, evidence cannot settle this.

## 5. Sentences the authors may use (verbatim)

1. "Round 11 asked whether a feeling of good or bad simply is a value signal being available for planning, or a further, higher-order representation of it; we found no study that measured both in the same people while one of them was changed, so the question was not tested."
2. "All three rows were scored 'no test' for both accounts, and the balance between them did not move."
3. "We found no case of a value signal being broadly available for planning while people rated their feeling at zero, no case of rated feeling falling while that availability stayed the same, and no case of prefrontal disruption leaving both unchanged; 'none found' does not mean 'none exists'."
4. "The brain-stimulation effect such a test would rely on is itself uncertain: a replication attempt with 53 analysed participants did not reproduce the original drop in people's insight into their own perception, and that null result has in turn been criticised as underpowered."
5. "The nearest thing to a test, a study of 27 people given stimulation to the left prefrontal cortex, found self-rated mood unchanged; it stimulated one side only and measured neither rated feeling about a stimulus nor value-guided planning, so it counts for neither account."
6. "Our hypothesis, in the only form we can defend, says the same thing as one existing higher-order theory (Rolls's) and differs from the other (LeDoux and Brown's, and Lau's) only in what people would report feeling."
7. "Evidence of this kind can at most show which brain process accompanies reported feeling; it cannot show why any process is felt at all, and we have not found where sentience comes from."
