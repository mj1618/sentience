## 1. Fairness

**Account statements.** F-loop and F-pattern are stated acceptably in one line each. N
is stated as two claims fused together: a claim about consciousness (what matters is
firing and wiring) and a claim about physiology (the field is a by-product with small
effects). A neurons-only theorist needs only the first. That fusion lets physiological
findings be booked against N.

**Mis-stated rows.**
- **Row 2.** Scenario has F predicting experience changes in step with a weak applied
  field. Both proponents predict *no* experiential effect of spatially unstructured
  external fields (McFadden: incoherent; Pockett: only an inverse spatial pattern would
  cancel experience). As written, the row tests a claim nobody holds.
- **Row 4.** "Fields cancel" is not Pockett's argument (she excludes the cerebellum by
  lamination) and is McFadden's only in 2022, resting on a citation (Andersen 2020) that
  argues the opposite. The premise is also false: the cerebellum is open-field.
- **Row 6.** Scenario has F predicting residual midline unity. Both proponents predict
  division because the field is too local. F and N predict the same thing.
- **Row 5.** N's "cannot cross" is a strawman of neurons-only *about consciousness*;
  it follows only from the bolted-on physiological claim.

**Rows that could not fail.** Row 1 for N ("small effects at most" absorbs anything short
of large rate changes) and for F-pattern (no commitment). Rows 3 and 8 for both, as the
authors pre-flagged. Row 6 for both once corrected. Row 2 for F once corrected:
McFadden counts positive applied-field effects as support and dismisses nulls as
incoherent input, which is heads-I-win.

## 3. Overall

**(a) Do endogenous fields play a causal role in neural computation?** Evidence shifts
moderately toward yes for *influence*: fields of natural magnitude bias spike timing in
awake primates, and field coupling can propagate activity without synapses in dense
hippocampal tissue. A *functional role in normal awake cortex* remains undemonstrated;
nobody has removed the endogenous field while leaving synapses intact.

**(b) Is the field identical to experience?** Shift: **none**. Rows 2, 6 and 7, the
ones that could have discriminated on experience, returned no test.

**Does (a) support (b)?** Barely. (a) is a necessary condition for F-loop, so its
survival removes one objection (the field is inert). But (a) is fully absorbed by N:
ephaptic coupling is one more way neurons influence neurons, and a functional account
simply adds it to the wiring diagram. F-pattern does not need (a) at all. Net shift
between "field is the experience" and "neurons only": **none**, with at most a slight
gain for F-loop relative to F-pattern. Booking row 5 as a win for F over N would be the
conflation.

McFadden needs the field to be local (split brain) yet integrating (unity), and resolves
it with relays of synchronised neurons through fibres. The unifying work is then done by
wiring, which is N's claim.

## 4. Testability

Weakly testable for F-loop; close to untestable for F-pattern. If the conscious pattern
does not act on its own brain, a subject's report (produced by neurons) cannot reflect a
change in it, so no behavioural experiment reaches it. For F-loop, any field change that
alters experience must alter firing, so N can always attribute the effect to firing in
unrecorded cells. Experiments can therefore shift credence, not settle identity.

1. **Field cancellation with firing monitored.** Closed-loop field clamp (Fröhlich's
   slice method, extended in vivo) over sensory cortex in awake macaques or implanted
   patients, applying the inverse of the measured field through a high-density grid,
   with units recorded and a threshold perceptual report. Pockett explicitly predicts an
   inverse spatial pattern abolishes that experience. F-loop predicts disrupted binding
   and report, larger than the spike change. N predicts perceptual change only in
   proportion to altered spiking, likely small.
2. **Residual test with adequate fields.** Intracranially verified fields of at least
   1 V/m, skin anaesthetised, retinal-control montage, units recorded, near-threshold
   detection. Ask whether perception varies with field phase after regressing out spike
   rate and timing. F: a residual. N: none. Shape the field to the endogenous pattern
   so the "incoherent field" reply is unavailable.

## 5. Plain language

Brain cells make tiny electric fields when they fire, and there is now decent evidence
that those fields nudge the timing of nearby cells and, in some tightly packed tissue,
can pass activity along without the usual connections. That is a finding about how
brains work. It is not evidence that the field *is* the feeling. To show that, someone
would have to change the field while leaving the cells' firing the same and find that
experience changed, and no one has done it. Where the theory's supporters have been
pressed (scanners, split brains, seizures), their answers end up predicting the same
things as the ordinary view. So after this exercise
the honest position is: the brain's field probably does a little real work, the idea
that it is consciousness has neither gained nor lost ground, and this round did not
actually test it.
