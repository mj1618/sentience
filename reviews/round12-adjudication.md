# Round 12 — Independent adjudication: does time create sentience?

Scored against Part 1b of scenario 21 (criteria fixed before evidence) using `research/21-time-evidence.md`. No repo file was modified.

## Spot-checks (full text)

- **Deco 2022 (PMC9187708)** — confirmed: non-reversibility lower than waking for "sleep and all anaesthesia conditions (except for ketamine)". Ketamine-alone data are small (2,210 s); no reports; the appeal to ketamine experience is post hoc.
- **Sanz Perl 2021** — abstract confirms the opposite reading of the same recordings: all reduced states, ketamine included, closer to equilibrium.
- **Kringelbach 2023** — confirmed: movie-watching "similar to anesthesia and deep sleep", below rest. **Lynn 2021** — confirmed r = 0.774, p = 0.024 with motor response rate.
- **New, not in the evidence file: Berjaga-Buisan et al. 2026 (Cell Reports; PMC13506674).** Human EEG, 5 volunteers each under xenon, propofol, ketamine, all unresponsive. All ketamine participants later reported vivid experiences; the others reported none. A model-derived non-equilibrium index stayed at waking level under ketamine and fell under propofol and xenon. Caveats: n = 5 per drug; index fitted from a whole-brain model, not a direct irreversibility measure; slow oscillations dominated propofol and xenon, so power and arousal are uncontrolled; same group as the monkey papers.
- **Idesis 2024 (PMC10836715), ferret** — "active-awake/REM" is one merged class; uninformative.
- No multivariate irreversibility study of REM with dream reports or of generalised seizures was found.

**Row 1.** Ray 1999 [abstract]: threshold rises sharply below 250 ms, which is intensity buying off duration, not a floor. Libet and single-pulse TMS phosphenes are recalled only. No paradigm shows a floor; nor was the short-duration end read in full text. Low confidence.

**Row 4.** No study relates irreversibility to self-rated valence; the HCP "emotion" task has no rating (rule 4).

**Row 5.** Feinstein 2010: five amnesic patients' self-rated sadness and happiness at or above control levels, meeting the fail condition for T3e. Limits: n = 5, page summary, no autonomic data, future thinking not measured in these patients.

**Row 6.** Cobos 2002/2004 (19 + 19; abstracts, lesion levels unknown) ratings normal; Roger (n = 1) affective pain 10/10 in 3 of 4 trials; Heims 2004 one test worse; Critchley recalled only. Not CONTRADICTED because no case shows brainstem or hypothalamic prediction was itself disrupted.

**Row 7.** Hruby 2024 (n = 50): more loss of time sense, mildly better affect, rated afterwards. The criterion asks for intense valence, concurrent or immediate; psychedelic and flow evidence is recalled only.

**Row 9.** Reviews and reanalyses (VanRullen, White, Brookshire, Keitel; mostly abstract or partial) agree there is no single rate across tasks and modalities. Herzog's flexible-window model is untouched by this criterion.

**Row 10.** No dataset has self-rated feeling under a rate change with cognition intact; timing studies measure counting; cooling impairs cognition by about 35 °C.

## Task 2 — Row 3: marker of what?

States classed before looking at results: (a) REM dreaming, ketamine with later-reported experience; (b) generalised seizures, unresponsive states with normal activity.

- **Ketamine.** One monkey dataset, no reports, anaesthetic dose: reduced under two analyses, not reduced under a third. The one human dataset with reports (n = 5) shows a related index preserved. Mixed, leaning preserved where experience was verified; tiny samples, no power control.
- **REM.** No usable data: two single-channel studies [abstracts] disagree in direction, neither has dream reports.
- **Seizures.** No generalised-seizure data. Focal seizures on single-channel intracranial EEG [abstract]: maximal irreversibility in 31 of 32 recordings, consciousness not reported. Points away from T2*, but wrong seizure type and a different measure.
- **Unresponsive wakefulness.** Lower than minimally conscious and controls, but activity and metabolism unmatched, so cell (b) stays empty.
- **Off-criterion.** In awake, experiencing people, movie-watching sits below rest at a level the authors liken to anaesthesia and deep sleep; LSD lowers it; tasks raise it in step with motor response rate.

**Verdict.** Not shown to be a marker of experience; not shown to be a mere arousal marker either (movie below rest does not fit "more driven, more irreversible"). The most economical reading: it falls with slow-wave, synchronous dynamics and rises with task and motor demand. The criterion's cells are essentially empty. **The evidence is not enough to say.**

## Task 3 — The two questions

**Q-org.** No temporal organisation is shown to be necessary for feeling. Episodic future thinking is probably not necessary (row 5, small). A single fixed frame rate is not supported (row 9). Sustained activity beyond an intensity-proof floor is not demonstrated (row 1). Bodily prediction is untested in the sense the theory means (row 6). Some integration over a window of tens to hundreds of milliseconds is supported for perception generally, which no rival disputes.

**Q-time.** No evidence. No study holds organisation fixed while varying absolute duration, rate, or substrate dissipation and then measures self-rated feeling. Rows 4 and 10 are empty; row 3 is a marker row.

**Shift for N* versus the time-itself conjectures: none.** N* was not tested, so it gains nothing either; it stands by default, not by evidence.

## Task 4 — Answer to the owner

- (a) **Trivially true:** feeling, like every process, takes time.
- (b) **Supported:** perception is assembled over short windows; brain dynamics are more time-asymmetric in waking than in deep sleep, propofol anaesthesia and unresponsive patients (a confounded correlate); feeling persists in a small group who cannot form new memories.
- (c) **Contradicted (on the fixed criteria, modest data):** that feeling needs the ability to imagine one's future; that experience runs at one fixed frame rate.
- (d) **Untested:** whether absolute rate, absolute duration or substrate irreversibility matter with organisation held fixed; whether irreversibility tracks feeling; whether it tracks experience in REM and generalised seizures; whether disrupting bodily prediction removes feeling.
- (e) **Not decidable by evidence:** T7 (real passage); the implicit Husserlian form of T4*; N* versus T6* for rate changes no living brain tolerates.

## Task 5 — Sharpest feasible experiments

1. **Within-state report contrast.** Pre-register one multivariate irreversibility measure with spectral-power matching; apply it to existing serial-awakening high-density EEG sleep data (experience reported vs not, within REM and within NREM), the open Liège wake/sleep/ketamine/propofol/xenon recordings, and EEG of generalised absence seizures.
2. **Row 4 directly.** Within-subject MEG or fMRI with trial-by-trial self-rated valence intensity against an effort- and response-matched neutral condition; secondary arm for row 10: the same ratings under ±1 °C passive core-temperature shifts with cognition verified intact.

## Task 6 — Sentences the authors may quote verbatim

1. "Every process takes time, so saying that feeling needs time is true of everything and tells us nothing by itself."
2. "We found no study that tests whether time itself (absolute speed, absolute duration, or physical irreversibility) matters for feeling when a system's organisation is held fixed; that question is untested, not answered."
3. "Brain activity is more one-directional in time during waking than in deep sleep, propofol anaesthesia and unresponsive patients, but the same measure is lower while people watch a film than while they rest, and rises with how often people press buttons, so it has not been shown to track experience."
4. "The decisive cases are missing or conflicting: for ketamine, three analyses of one monkey dataset disagree, and we found no adequate data for dreaming sleep or generalised seizures."
5. "In one study of five people with severe amnesia, self-rated sadness and happiness were as strong as in controls or stronger, which counts against the idea that feeling requires imagining one's own future; the sample is small."
6. "Research on rhythms in perception does not support a single fixed frame rate for experience."
7. "We found no study relating the time-asymmetry of brain activity to how strongly people rate their feelings."
8. "Whether the real passage of time is needed for feeling cannot be settled by any evidence we know of."
