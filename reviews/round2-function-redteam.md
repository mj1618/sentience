**Pattern.** Every sim result that carries a conclusion is either true by construction (J, C, test 5, readout = 1.00) or reverses under a fairer baseline or one parameter change (B, imagination, battery). What survives: plasticity pays when costs are low relative to change; self-reward needs discounting; a state × offer interaction beats ignoring either.

## Hypotheses not considered

1. **Social signal.** Feelings exist to be expressed credibly (pain displays recruit care). Test: does pain expression vary with audience; do solitary species lack graded displays?
2. **Commitment device** (Frank 1988). Emotions are hard to override so threats and promises are credible. Test: is involuntariness strongest for strategic emotions (anger, guilt)?
3. **Interrupt** (Simon 1967). Feeling pre-empts a serial planner; intensity tracks urgency to switch, not value. Test: vary competing-task priority at fixed stimulus value.
4. **Report criterion only.** One valuation system; "felt" is what exceeds a metacognitive threshold. Test: signal-detection analysis of Winkielman/Lamb-type designs.
5. **Derivative signal.** Valence reports rate of change of prediction error (mood as momentum), not level. Test: fix outcome level, vary trajectory.
6. **Temporal bridge for credit assignment.** A sustained global state links acts to delayed outcomes. Test: does trace (not delay) conditioning need awareness?
7. **Memory prioritisation.** Feeling tags events for consolidation. Test: block affect at encoding; memory advantage should go while online choice stays.
8. **Allostatic prediction.** Feelings forecast needs before deficits. Test: add predictive valence to Experiment B and compare with reactive.
9. **Intrinsic motivation.** Curiosity and boredom value information. Test: evolve an exploration bonus in Experiment A instead of fixed eps.
10. **No separate function.** Feeling is constitutive of integration. Test: none of the sims can exclude it; say so.
11. **Constructed origin** (Barrett). Only arousal and coarse valence are innate. Test: cross-cultural and developmental variation in reported feelings.
