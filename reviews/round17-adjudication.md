# Round 17 adjudication: what switches experience off?

Scored per prediction. All molecular and animal endpoints are responsiveness only. Many figures are abstract-only or tool-extracted; verdicts capped accordingly.

## 1. Molecular level

- **Pred 1.** L-classical contradicted (non-immobilizers, cut-off, lipid-free luciferase). P supported. L-modern, Q: no unique credit.
- **Pred 2.** L-classical contradicted (etomidate ~10x). P supported for injected agents; volatile differences small, fitting L-modern equally.
- **Pred 3.** Undecided for all; no target identified. No credit to L or Q.
- **Pred 4a (Q).** Undecided, no lean. Khan 2024 (n=8, fixed order, latency): stabiliser gives resistance. Li 2025: stabiliser and destabiliser both increase sensitivity. Taxane patients: similar anaesthetic concentrations (P=0.15).
- **Pred 4b (Q).** Undecided, weak lean to Q. Li 2018 is large (n=20 per group) but abstract-only, blinding and purity unverified, unreplicated in eight years.
- **Pred 7.** P supported for etomidate and propofol immobility (hypnosis reduced, not abolished); not for volatile agents (shifts ≤24%); xenon untested. L-modern undecided, lean contradicted: Pavel 2020 is chloroform-only, two brains per condition, unreplicated; TREK-1 has a direct binding site; the TREK-1 knockout failed replication (also a lost P claim).
- **M:** lean supported (one group; target versus vulnerability unresolved). **S:** lean supported in invertebrates only.

L-classical is refuted. The "in common" question is unanswered: injected GABA-type agents are largely explained for immobility, volatile agents fractionally, xenon not at all in vivo. Best-supported answer: no single common molecular target.

## 2. Systems level

- **N.5:** undecided, lean against any global measure. Support is one across-drug cohort of 15–18. Within-drug tests are null (Bajwa 2025, five no-experience reports, underpowered) or near chance (Casey 2022, validated AUC 0.62). Regional positives use other measures; N may not switch.
- **B.5:** undecided, weak lean against. No direct human test; 74–90% experience reports under propofol and dexmedetomidine sit awkwardly, though at light, arousable doses.
- **N.6:** undecided; experience untested. Pal 2020 (rats) restored behaviour without cortical connectivity.
- **B.6:** partly supported, responsiveness only, some GABA-type agents, one laboratory. Independent pilot largely failed. Lesion raises dose 36–47%; none for ketamine. Reversal also works from thalamus and prefrontal cortex.
- **T:** undecided; stimulation reverses unresponsiveness, but thalamic lesions alone spare arousal. **D:** undecided (single lab).

## 3. Headline

Yes: the sources establish much about what removes responsiveness and memory, and almost nothing about what removes experience. A "no experience" report cannot separate absence from forgetting, no marker separates them at matched unresponsiveness, and surgical-depth doses are untested by awakening.

## 4. Owner's interests

(a) Quantum/field: two unrefuted, unconfirmed leads, neither about experience. Open, not supported.
(b) Feeling-first/brainstem: a real brainstem lever on responsiveness for some drugs; nothing ties it to experience; human imaging puts the state change in thalamus and cingulate.

## 5. Missing studies

1. **Xenon replication.** Independent lab, both sexes; isotopes ≥99% enriched, mass-spectrometry verified; coded cylinders, blinded video scoring, measured chamber concentration, hyperbaric xenon alone, pre-registered. A ≥20% spin difference favours quantum accounts; a tight null removes Q's best datum.
2. **Within-drug awakening study.** Surgical-depth propofol and ketamine, ≥100 no-experience awakenings, pre-registered PCI plus posterior and cingulate measures, with arousal stimulation at constant dose. A marker with AUC >0.8 favours N; experience returning with arousal while the marker stays low favours B.

## PERMITTED

1. Anaesthetics reliably stop people responding and remembering; whether they stop experience is much less certain.
2. In volunteer studies at light doses, most people woken from unresponsiveness reported having experienced something, usually dreams.
3. In two large studies, roughly one in twenty to one in ten surgical patients followed a command while apparently anaesthetised, and almost none remembered it.
4. No brain measurement has yet been shown to tell reliably whether an unresponsive person is experiencing anything.
5. The old idea that anaesthetics work simply by dissolving in the fat of cell membranes has been refuted.
6. For some injected anaesthetics a specific protein target is known; for anaesthetic gases, what they share is unknown.
7. One mouse study found that xenon atoms with nuclear spin were weaker anaesthetics; it has not been repeated in eight years.
8. Studies of drugs that act on microtubules disagree about direction.
9. A small brainstem area in rats strongly affects responsiveness to some anaesthetics; this comes from one laboratory and says nothing about experience.

## FORBIDDEN

- "Anaesthesia switches off consciousness" / "we know what switches experience off."
- "Anaesthesia does not switch off experience" / "patients are conscious during surgery."
- "Quantum effects have been shown in anaesthesia" / "quantum theories have been ruled out."
- "Anaesthetics act on microtubules."
- "The brainstem is the seat or switch of experience."
- "Complexity measures can detect consciousness under anaesthesia."
- Any exact figure presented as checked.
