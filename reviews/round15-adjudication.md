# Round 15 adjudication (Scenario 30, Amendment governing)

**1. Prediction 1**
- *Narrow (emotion):* DES, the only neutral single-moment method, finds no feeling in 71–92% of moments (five samples, 76 people, ~1,570 moments), far past the 20% line. By the pre-set rule: **undecided, strong lean against A**, for B, C, E; D is hurt less. Limits: one lab, no independent replication, authors concede faint feeling cannot be excluded. Checklists (0–10%) are leading, so weak; Trampe's 10% still misses A's <5%.
- *Broad:* **undecided, moderate lean against A.** Nobody measured it. The gatherer's bound (≥~50–60% with neither Feeling nor Sensory awareness) is correct arithmetic but not the plan's quantity: hedonic bodily feelings and urges may sit in other codes or go unasked. Usable only to say A's <5% looks hard to reach.
- *Open:* whether a direct neutral probe finds faint tone, bodily feeling or urge in moments DES codes feeling-free. A fail refutes only noticed-feeling A.

**2. Prediction 3: no count possible; weak lean against A.** Nobody systematically asked blunted people whether the world seemed unreal. In Price 2009, about 3 of 33 quoted participants (P22, P28, P25) meet the "thinned" rule; most describe flatness only. Quotes were author-picked from a self-selected sample. The minority is not clean evidence for A: detachment tracked depression scores (Goodwin 2017), P22 used cannabis, and the descriptions are depersonalisation, excluded as circular. The depression explanation is equally unproven.

**3. Prediction 4 (scored part): no count against A; no support for A.** MPE episodes need not be contentless, items were leading, episodes self-chosen; 0.15–0.36% (gatherer's tabulation) shows only that this dataset supplies no affect-free pure awareness. Better-method evidence runs the other way but is tiny: 3 of 12 open interviews on objectless sleep awareness reported no feeling. The 33% denying "emotions" while rating peace near ceiling show "no emotion" is not "no feeling": narrow probes can miss calm tone, so DES's narrow figures cannot be carried to the broad definition.

**4. Overall.** A is not refuted but took the round's only strong hit: by the best method available, noticed emotion is absent from most moments. A survives only if feeling is read broadly, and that version is untested. Prediction 3, the only one separating A from D, yielded nothing countable and leans slightly the wrong way. Nothing this round counts for A.

*Settling study:* random beeps, ~100 unselected adults, 6/day for 5 days after a training day (~2,500 moments), preregistered, two labs independent of Hurlburt. Open probe first ("What, if anything, was in your experience at the moment of the beep?"), then, about that moment: "Any pleasantness or unpleasantness, however slight: pleasant, unpleasant, neither?"; "Any bodily feeling such as pain, discomfort, hunger, tension or comfort?"; "Any urge or wanting?", mixed with same-form control questions (inner speech, colour) to measure yes-saying. Against A: >20% of moments "none" on all three. For A: <5%.

**5.** "Nobody has tested this head-on" was false for the narrow question: Heavey & Hurlburt tested it against Watson's "always" claim in 2008, with further samples in 2022; Gasper (2019) reviews the belief by name; it dates to Wundt (1897). It holds only for the broad version and for blunting-thins-reality.

**PERMITTED**
1. When people are beeped at random and asked what was in their experience, they report an emotion in about one moment in four or fewer.
2. All of that evidence comes from one research group and has not been independently repeated.
3. That method counts emotions, not every kind of feeling, so it leans against the feeling-first idea without settling it.
4. Nobody has yet asked, moment by moment and neutrally, whether any feeling at all was present.
5. Most quoted people on emotion-dulling medication describe a flat world, not an unreal one, but nobody has counted this properly.
6. Meditators describing "pure awareness" nearly always also report peace, though the questions invited that answer.
7. We were wrong to say this had never been tested; researchers have argued over it since at least 2008.
8. The feeling-first idea is still standing, but it gained no support this round.

**FORBIDDEN**
- "Most of our experience has no feeling in it."
- "The feeling-first idea has been refuted / confirmed / survived the test."
- "At least half of moments have no feeling of any kind."
- "The feeling was there but unnoticed."
- "Pure awareness is always blissful" or "never comes without feeling."
- "Blunting feeling makes the world unreal" or "leaves experience untouched."
- "Nobody has tested this."
