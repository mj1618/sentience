| **C8** Sensation and unpleasantness separable | **Berthier 1988** [V-abs, PMID 3415199]: n = 6, unilateral lesions, insula damaged in all 6. No primary sensory loss; no withdrawal and absent or inadequate emotional response to pain and to threatening gestures; did not learn protective responses. **Price 1985** [V-abs, PMID 2993984]: morphine 0.04–0.08 mg/kg lowered *both* intensity and unpleasantness ratings, dose-dependently; at 0.04 and 0.06 only the unpleasantness drop reached significance; at 0.08 both did. **Gracely 1979** [V-abs, PMID 424753], n = 40: fentanyl lowered intensity but *not* unpleasantness — the opposite pattern. **Kupers 1991** [V-abs, PMID 1663226], n = 14 neurogenic-pain patients: morphine lowered unpleasantness, not intensity. **Rainville 1997** [V-abs, PMID 9252330]: hypnotic suggestion changed unpleasantness with intensity unchanged. **Foltz & White 1962** cingulotomy: no abstract online [R]. | Headline **matches**. "Morphine reduced unpleasantness but not intensity" (research/03 B3) is **stronger** than the source; cingulotomy leg unchecked. | Unpleasantness can be changed while rated intensity stays the same (hypnosis, six insula-damage patients). Opioid evidence is mixed: low-dose morphine eased unpleasantness more than intensity in one study; fentanyl did the opposite in another. Cingulotomy is unchecked. |
| **C9** Wanting and liking separable | **Berridge & Kringelbach 2015** [V-full, PMC4425246]. The causal evidence is rat taste-reactivity. They write 'liking' in quotes to mean an *objective* reaction "whether or not" accompanied by conscious pleasure, and say it can occur with no felt pleasure. Human evidence is thin: **Leyton 2002** [V-abs], n = 8, dopamine release correlated with wanting (r = 0.83), not mood; **Evans 2006** [V-abs], 8 vs 8 Parkinson's patients, same pattern; Parkinson's patients rate sweet taste normally (cited in B&K; primaries not read) [R]. | Mechanism claim **matches**. Update 1 and research/03 read 'liking' as felt pleasure: **stronger** than Berridge, who explicitly does not equate them. "In addiction wanting grows as liking falls" not verified in a human primary. | In rats, the "go and get it" signal and the pleasure reaction run on different chemistry and can be changed separately. In people the evidence is a handful of small studies (8–16 people) in which dopamine tracked reported wanting and not reported pleasure. Berridge's 'liking' is a measured reaction, not necessarily a felt one. |
| **C10** Intelligence does not substitute for pain | **Rini, Irvandy & Pradhea 2025** [V-full, APIC 29:1290–1301]: 29 case reports, **41 patients, all CIPA / HSAN-IV (NTRK1)**. Fever 87%, self-injury 73%, recurrent fractures 68%, **developmental delay 63% (26 of 41)**. Case reports only; the text elsewhere says only 3 of 41 did not self-harm, conflicting with 73%. **Cox 2006** [V-full, PMC7212082]: SCN9A, **n = 6** children aged 4–14 from 3 families, sweating normal, "considered of normal intelligence by their parents and teachers, and by the caring physicians" (no formal testing). All 6 had lip or tongue injuries from biting in the first 4 years; all had frequent bruises and cuts; "most" had fractures or osteomyelitis found late. Index boy (not one of the 6) died before 14 jumping off a roof. | **Wrong population for the numbers.** The 73% and 68% come from a group where most have developmental delay, so they cannot be paired with "normal intelligence". The claim rests on Cox's 6 children plus one death, intelligence judged informally, injuries mostly in infancy: **stronger** than the evidence. | In the one well-described group with pain loss and normal intelligence (six children), all had injured themselves and most had unnoticed fractures; one related boy died in a risky stunt. Suggestive, from seven people. The "seven in ten" figures belong to a different condition that also impairs development and must not be used here. |
| **C11** Loss of drive stops spontaneous thought, ability intact | **Laplane & Dubois 2001** [V-full] is a *review* with no patient count of its own; the IQ statement cites **Laplane 1989** [V-abs, PMID 2786440]: **n = 8**, bilateral basal-ganglia (mainly pallidal) lesions after anoxia or poisoning, "inertia and loss of drive, with preservation of intellectual function". Review text: WAIS and Raven's "generally normal"; but reduced word fluency and free recall, more Wisconsin card-sort errors, occasional slowness. "Empty, a total blank", "no thoughts", not bored — yet "not all patients with AAD experience this"; some have compulsive mental activity (counting). Affect also flattened ("neither fear nor desire"). Lesions: pallidum, caudate, also thalamus and frontal. | Broadly **matches**, slightly **stronger**: "stops" should be "can stop"; "ability intact" hides mild executive deficits; drive and affect are lost together, so drive is not isolated. | In a small number of patients (the key series has eight) with damage to deep brain nuclei, people do nothing unless prompted and many, not all, report an empty mind. When prompted they perform roughly normally on general intelligence tests, with mild weaknesses in fluency and recall. Feeling is flattened too. |
| **C12** Tested theories addressed vision only; none addresses valence | **Cogitate 2025** [V-full, PMC12137136]: n = 256; stimuli were clearly visible pictures (faces, objects, letters, false fonts; 3 orientations; 0.5–1.5 s). Vision only; no emotional or valence manipulation. Templeton list of adversarial projects [V-full page]: none names affect, emotion or pain. | Cogitate part **matches**. "None addresses valence" is supported for the listed Templeton projects only. Constraint 9's "GNWT, IIT, RPT and HOT were tested on visual content only" is **stronger**: true of the published adversarial test, not of each theory's wider literature (GNWT has auditory and touch studies [R]). | The one finished head-to-head test of the two leading theories used only pictures. None of the funded head-to-head projects we could find is about feeling good or bad. Whether anyone elsewhere has is unchecked. |
| **C16** No bias above ~3×10⁻⁵ | **Bösch, Steinkamp & Boller 2006** [V-full, author version at ebo.de, Table 4]. 380 studies. Fixed-effect π = .499997 (SE .000001, z = −3.67, *against* intention); random-effects π = .500035 (SE .000014, z = 2.47). The three largest studies (one report, Dobyns et al. 2004) π = .499989–.499997, against intention. Without them (377): fixed .500048 (z = 3.59), random .500286 (z = 4.08). Heterogeneity extreme (Q = 1508). Devices: electronic-noise or radioactive-decay RNGs. | Numbers in research/07 are **exact**. But 3.5×10⁻⁵ is the random-effects *point estimate*, not an upper bound: its 95% upper limit is ≈ 6×10⁻⁵, and without the three largest studies the estimates are 5×10⁻⁵ to 3×10⁻⁴. The ledger "bound" is **stronger** than follows. | The three biggest experiments found no effect in the intended direction (deviation about 1 in 100,000 or less, the wrong way). Pooled estimates depend on method (slightly negative to about 3 in 100,000 positive); small studies look bigger, consistent with selective publication. A safe statement is "no reliable effect; anything real is below roughly 1 in 10,000". |
| **C18** Markers appear even in a 302-neuron worm | Worm: **Ghosh 2016** [V-full, PMC5147516] — food deprivation (1 h, more at 5 h) raises crossing of a drying barrier to food odour. Accurate. **Cohen & Rabinowitch 2024** [V-abs] — second-order conditioning. Accurate. **Merritt 2019** [V-full, PMC6382802], cited in research/06 as [10a] for *trace* conditioning, is about *blocking* and context; the word "trace" does not occur. **No worm trace-conditioning source found.** Fly: **Mohammad 2016** [V-full, PMC4826436] — wall-following reduced by diazepam, raised by stress. Accurate. **Yamada 2023** [V-abs] — second-order. Accurate. **Triphan 2025** [V-abs] — carousel "play-like", only some flies; others avoid it. Bees: **Bateson 2011** [V-full, PMC3158593] and **Solvi 2016** [V-abs] — judgement bias, accurate. **Gibbons 2022** [V-abs + 2025 correction, P = 0.033, n = 32] — trade-off graded by sucrose, not hunger. **Galpayage Dona 2022** [V-abs] — ball-rolling. | Mostly **matches**. **Wrong** on worm trace conditioning ([10a] does not support it). Anxiety-like and play markers are fly and bee only, not worm. | A 302-neuron worm shows two of the standard markers: a hunger-dependent risk trade-off and second-order conditioning (plus blocking). Trace conditioning in the worm is unsupported and should be removed. Drug-sensitive anxiety-like behaviour and play-like behaviour are reported in flies and bees, each from one or two labs. |
