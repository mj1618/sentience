## 4. The two syntheses

Both generalise narrower adjudicator remarks; neither has been reviewed.

**Report barrier (C24).** Strongest objections:
1. "Every theory explains the behaviour" confuses accommodation with prediction. A theory that predicts nothing in advance should lose ground.
2. The nulls have duller explanations. The adjudicators traced them to mis-stated rivals (C in round 5, F in round 6, I and Q in round 7), abstract-only reading with no web search, and experiments not yet done. "Decisive rows empty" is practical, not principled; the project lists ten feasible experiments.
3. The barrier is porous: calibrate a mechanism in reporting humans (felt versus unfelt processing of one stimulus) and extend by homology; train non-verbal report (wagering in monkeys).

**Identity claims (Update 6).** "All predict the same reports, because whatever a person
says is produced by the same cells" is a non sequitur. Theories differ on *which neural
conditions accompany* report, which is exactly what round 7 used to strike the P3b.
Identities elsewhere (heat, genes) were settled by intervention plus best explanation. The adjudicator said "shift credence, not settle
identity"; the update turned that into "cannot".

Verdict: fair as a description of seven rounds of desk research; overreach as a claim
about the question; in practice an excuse.

## 5. Drift from the owner's question

Yes. Rounds 2–7 went to fields, choosers, lottery balls, illusionism and markers of visual
experience. The rows that bear on "sentience as motivator" (C8–C12) are still "unreviewed
(published)".

What has been learned that bears on it, minimally:
- Motivation and felt pleasure dissociate (C9, unreviewed), so "sentience is the motivator" must say which.
- A rat's planner updates value only after the outcome is experienced (C14); this fits feeling as teacher of thought, with a rival unexcluded.
- Valence signalling exists in a worm; so the hypothesis turns on whether unfelt valuation can motivate.
- Loss of drive stops thought while awareness persists (C11): for the motivator claim, against the two-ingredient sum.
- Limbic cortex is not required for reported pain.

Next: one predictions-first round on **felt versus unfelt valuation in reporting humans**
(subliminal reward and effort, affective blindsight, pain asymbolia and opioid
dissociation, apathy versus anhedonia, akinetic mutism), with owner's hypothesis stated
against named rivals, and a full-text review of C8–C12. This applies the contrastive method to valence and targets the ledger's own open issue.
