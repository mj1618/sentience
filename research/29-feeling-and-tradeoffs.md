# Brief 29 — Feeling and trade-offs between unlike needs (ability f)

Evidence brief for Scenario 33 (Amendment governs): FEELING (felt pleasantness /
unpleasantness), ability f, accounts C-narrow, C-strong, P, A (A.2), U.1, and the
comparative evidence. Gathered 2026-10-04. **This brief does not score or adjudicate.**
It builds on Brief 24 and does not repeat it; where Brief 24 already covers a source it is
cited as "B24".

## How to read this brief

**Access labels** (stated for every source):

- **FT** = full text retrieved and the relevant methods/results passages read by me
  (publisher/repository PDF or Europe PMC open-access XML). Targeted passages, not cover
  to cover.
- **FT-s** = full text retrieved but summarised for me by a fetch tool; I did not read the
  raw passages. Numbers from these are less secure.
- **ABS** = abstract only (Europe PMC).
- **SNIP** = search-engine snippet or secondary description. Treat as unverified.

**What was manipulated** (per the Amendment): **[F]** = feeling measured or manipulated;
**[Aw]** = awareness; **[beh]** = behaviour only, feeling inferred. Most comparative
studies are [beh].

**Ability f as defined in the Amendment:** choice between options serving different
needs, where the exchange rate shifts flexibly with the level of each need and is not a
fixed, trained priority. I tag each trade-off study with which parts of that definition it
actually tested: **(two needs)**, **(graded in A)**, **(graded in B)**, **(need level
manipulated)**, **(learned cues)**, **(interaction tested)**.

**Recurring limitation, stated once.** No study found combines (a) a trade-off between
unlike needs with need level manipulated, (b) graded feeling ratings about the options,
and (c) a group or condition in which that feeling is reduced while awareness of the
options is shown intact. C.1 and the C-vs-A question therefore have no direct test in what
I found. Everything below is one or two of the three.

**Replication status.** All human trade-off studies above are from Cabanac's own
laboratory, n=3–12, 1983–2002. I found **no independent-laboratory replication** of the
quantitative claim (summed ratings predict the chosen compromise) and no pre-registered
test. Alliesthesia itself (pleasantness shifts with internal state) is widely reproduced
by others, e.g. thermal alliesthesia (Liu et al. 2021, ABS, n=35: change in pleasure
predicted from change in thermal sensation, R²=0.77–0.79) and drug-cue alliesthesia (Avery
et al. 2017, ABS, n=17 smokers: cigarette pictures rated more pleasant when abstinent;
mid-insula interoceptive activity change predicted the rating change).

**Critiques found.** (i) Circularity in animals (above). (ii) A unified value scale in
humans is challenged by Garcia, Lebreton, Bourgeois-Gironde & Palminteri 2023, *Nat Hum
Behav* (ABS; 9 experiments): in choices between experienced and described options,
experienced values were systematically neglected, "suggesting they recruit different
representational systems" — about value representation, not about feeling. (iii) Brown &
Birch 2025 (ABS): "pain can sometimes interfere with rather than support making trade-offs
rationally". (iv) B24's Korb 2020 / Eikemo 2017 / Leyton 2007: effort or choice changed
with graded ratings unchanged. Not found: a published direct attempt to refute Cabanac's
ratings-predict-compromise result.

**Birch, Burn, Schnell, Browning & Crump 2021 (LSE report), FT.** Eight criteria;
criterion 5 = motivational trade-offs "in which the disvalue of a noxious or threatening
stimulus is weighed (traded-off) against the value of an opportunity for reward … Enough
flexibility must be shown to indicate centralized, integrative processing of information
involving an evaluative common currency." Verdicts:

- Octopods: high/very high confidence on criteria 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8; **medium on 5** —
  "We have found no study that directly tests for motivational trade-offs in cephalopods."
- True crabs: high/very high on 1, 2, 4, 6, 7 (not 5).
- Anomuran (hermit) crabs: high on 1, 2, 6; **medium on 5**.
- Crayfish/lobsters: high on 1, 2, 4.
- "In cases where we do not have high … confidence … this is invariably because of a lack
  of positive evidence, rather than because of clear evidence that the animals fail."
- On *C. elegans*: "We know from the case of C. elegans that this can be achieved by a
  simple mechanism in which hunger inhibits threat detection (Ghosh et al., 2016). A system
  that represents different needs, though not ruled out, is not required."
- Cnidarians "would not convincingly satisfy any of the criteria".

Descriptive consequence for C.2: in this report the taxon with the strongest independent
signs (octopods: wound-tending, analgesic-reversible conditioned place avoidance) has no
direct trade-off test; the taxon with trade-off tests (hermit crabs) has only medium
confidence on the trade-off and fewer independent signs. Covariation of trade-offs with
independent signs is therefore largely **untested**, not shown and not refuted.

### 2b. Octopus (independent signs, no trade-off)

Crook 2021, *iScience* 24:102229. FT-s. *Octopus bocki*; acetic-acid arm injection n=8 vs
saline n=7. Conditioned place avoidance of the initially preferred chamber (one-sample t,
p=0.003; saline p=0.19; between groups p=0.006). Lidocaine given in the non-preferred
chamber produced preference for it only in acid-injected animals (p=0.005; saline p=0.51).
All acid-injected animals groomed the injection site with the beak for the full 20 min;
never seen after saline. Nerve recordings: >30 min ongoing activity silenced by lidocaine
(p≈0.045). Single lab; small n; lidocaine reversal "variable". Not a trade-off study.

### 2d. Fish and other vertebrates

- Millsopp & Laming 2008, *Appl Anim Behav Sci* 113:247. SNIP (via a thesis abstract).
  Goldfish: avoidance of a feeding zone increased with shock strength; fish more likely to
  enter as food deprivation increased. (two needs)(graded in A)(need level manipulated).
  n and statistics **unverified**. A trout comparison (Dunlop, Millsopp & Laming 2006) was
  not retrieved.
- Sneddon, Braithwaite & Gentle 2003, *J Pain* 4:431. ABS. Rainbow trout; n not in
  abstract. Acid-injected fish lost the normal avoidance of a novel object; morphine
  restored it. Authors read this as nociception capturing attention. Independent sign
  (analgesic reversal); also an instance of a noxious state *impairing* a competing
  response.
- Adcock & Tucker 2021, *Sci Rep* 11:6937. ABS. Calves after disbudding spent longer
  suckling after a startle (accepted more risk) than controls. Feeling not measured.
- Mouse circuit work (feeling not measurable): Alhadeff et al. 2018, *Cell* 173:140 (ABS):
  hunger "attenuates behavioral responses and affective properties of inflammatory pain
  without altering acute nociceptive responses", via AgRP → parabrachial neurons and NPY
  Y1 signalling, "as effectively as hunger or analgesics". Burnett et al. 2016, *Neuron*
  92:187 (ABS): hunger suppresses thirst, anxiety-like behaviour, innate fear and social
  interaction, "often only when food is accessible". These show need competition resolved
  partly by one need's circuit damping another's signal.

Reading limits: people who know they must drink and have water available under-drink when
thirst is not felt, and a rule (fixed intake) substitutes only partly. But the lesion
removes the osmotic need signal itself, not only its felt quality, and these patients
often have wider hypothalamic damage (obesity, sleep, memory). It is therefore not a case
of "feeling blunted with awareness of the options intact and the need signal otherwise
available". No study measured a trade-off (e.g. effort for water) in these patients.

### 3b. Congenital insensitivity to pain; asymbolia [F absent; sensation absent in CIP]

- Injury pattern (reviews and case series, ABS/SNIP): recurrent unnoticed fractures,
  burns, Charcot joints, oral self-injury from infancy; problems continue into adulthood
  (Cassidy & Shaffer 2008, ABS, adult Charcot spine). Cox et al. 2006 *Nature* (ABS)
  established SCN9A loss of function in three families; the widely repeated detail that
  the index boy died jumping from a roof is **from memory, unverified here**.
- Learned avoidance by rule: one secondary source says a patient "learned not to bite
  himself even though doing so caused him no discomfort" (SNIP). No quantitative study of
  rule-based avoidance found.
- Danziger, Prkachin & Willer 2006, *Brain* 129:2494 (ABS; n not in abstract): CIP
  patients' "semantic knowledge regarding the pain of others did not differ from control
  subjects"; lower and more variable pain ratings for injury videos lacking pain behaviour.
  So knowledge of what is harmful is present by test while injuries continue.
- Pain asymbolia: B24 (Berthier 1988, n=6). Schilder's original 11 cases (Jahn & Steinberg
  2020, ABS). Not found: any asymbolia study measuring a pain-versus-reward trade-off.

Same limit as 3a for CIP: the nociceptive signal is missing. Asymbolia is the cleaner case
(sensation present, unpleasantness absent, "unable to learn appropriate escape or
protective responses" per Berthier) but rests on clinical description.

### 3c. vmPFC damage and consistency of value comparison [F not measured]

- Fellows & Farah 2007, *Cereb Cortex* 17:2669 (ABS): 21 frontal-lesion patients vs 19
  controls; those with ventromedial damage "significantly more inconsistent in their
  preferences"; frontal damage sparing it normal. Simple pairwise preference under
  certainty.
- Henri-Bhargava, Simioni & Fellows 2012, *Neuropsychologia* 50:1536 (ABS): VMF n=15,
  other frontal n=8, controls n=23. Preferences among 12 items in each of five categories
  (fruits, vegetables, colours, landscapes, puppies): VMF "significantly more erratic …
  across all stimulus categories"; decision times and perceptual judgments normal.
- Camille 2011 (B24): more revealed-preference violations.
- Reber 2017 and Howard 2020 (B24): after selective satiation (a change in need level),
  **reported desire/pleasantness shifted normally but choice did not follow** in vmPFC
  patients (n=6) and after OFC-network TMS (n=28 vs 28).

Reading limits: comparisons were within category, not between unlike needs; feeling was
not rated in Fellows/Henri-Bhargava/Camille. Reber and Howard are the nearest thing to a
need-level manipulation with graded ratings: feeling intact, need-sensitive choice
disordered. That is evidence about sufficiency of feeling for the adjustment, not about
its necessity.

### 3d. Anhedonia, negative symptoms, apathy: effort-for-reward trade-offs

- Pillny et al. 2026, *Psychol Bull* (ABS; pre-registered; 68 studies, 115 effects,
  N=3,697): reduced effort allocation in schizophrenia spectrum g=−0.40, bipolar g=−0.39,
  major depression g=−0.28, at-risk samples g=−0.01. Moderators include positive and
  negative symptom severity. Modelling studies point to "increased effort sensitivity,
  reduced reward sensitivity, and cognitive impairment".
- Blouzard et al. 2023, *JAMA Psychiatry* 80:548 (ABS; 20 studies, 837 patients, 666
  controls); preprint version reported g=0.43 [0.29, 0.57], larger deficit on high-reward
  trials, and a high- vs low-negative-symptom difference (k=5).
- Saleh et al. 2021, *Brain* 144:1247 (ABS; n=82 small-vessel disease): apathetic
  patients accepted fewer effort-for-money offers, less responsive to low rewards.
- From B24: in schizophrenia in-the-moment hedonic ratings are normal (Cohen & Minor 2010
  meta-analysis); SHAPS did not predict effort choice in Treadway 2009; Korb 2020 —
  effort changed, ratings did not.

Reading limits: effort versus money is not a trade-off between two bodily needs with need
level manipulated. The groups with a robust effort deficit are not shown to have reduced
*graded* feeling about the options; where graded feeling was measured it was normal. So
these data show trade-off disorder **without** demonstrated blunting of in-the-moment
feeling, and do not supply the C.1 test.

### 3e. Homeostatic and other syndromes

- Prader–Willi: Deweese, Roof & Key 2023 (ABS; n=20): hyperphagia related to altered
  satiety regulation rather than heightened food-cue salience. No trade-off task found.
- Feelings after insular destruction: Damasio, Damasio & Tranel 2013, *Cereb Cortex*
  23:833 (ABS; n=1): bilateral insula destroyed, "all aspects of feeling were intact".
  Feinstein et al. 2016, *Brain Struct Funct* 221:1499 (ABS; n=1): insula, anterior
  cingulate and amygdala damaged bilaterally; pain experience and expression intact, "at
  times excessive". Bears on where feeling is generated (P's insular models; C-strong's
  brainstem claim), not on trade-offs.
- Not found: any trade-off study in alexithymia, depersonalisation, or after SSRI
  blunting; any study crossing hunger/thirst level with pain or effort in a
  reduced-feeling group.

## 5. P — interoceptive regulation

**For "feeling tracks bodily regulation":** alliesthesia (1a) — pleasantness follows
internal state; Avery 2017 (ABS, n=17) links the pleasantness shift to mid-insula
interoceptive activity; gut signals drive negative alliesthesia in rats, abolished by
vagotomy (Cabanac & Lafrance 1992; Cabanac & Zhao 1994, ABS). Seth 2013, *Trends Cogn Sci*
(theory; abstract not retrieved in full). Barrett et al. 2004, *JPSP* 87:684 (ABS): better
heartbeat detectors emphasised arousal more in emotion reports.

**Against, or limiting:**

- Shamsabad, Edwards, Brewer & Murphy 2025, *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* (ABS; 94 eligible
  studies, 30 in quantitative synthesis): "little evidence of a relationship between
  cardiac interoceptive accuracy and measures of emotion".
- Heartbeat counting validity: Zamariola et al. 2018, *Biol Psychol* 137:12 (ABS; N=572):
  scores >95 % under-reports; actual-vs-reported heartbeats r=0.16; no discrimination
  among the top 60 %. Billaux, Desmedt et al. 2025 (ABS): scores track time estimation;
  "no correlation between HCT scores and psychological constructs". Desmedt et al. 2023
  (ABS): newer tasks reviewed; high validity "potentially elusive".
- Tan et al. 2026 (ABS; k=44): depressive symptoms vs cardiac accuracy r=−0.10.
- Loss of bodily feedback: Cobos et al. 2002, 2004 (ABS; 19 spinal-cord-injured vs 19
  controls): "no decrease in emotional experience"; picture valence/arousal ratings equal.
  Chwalisz, Diener & Gallagher 1988 (ABS): intense emotion persists; weak trend to less
  intensity with less feedback. Heims et al. 2004 (ABS; pure autonomic failure): gambling
  task, emotion recognition normal; worse only on an emotional attribution test.
- Insula destroyed, feelings intact (3e).
- Feeling outside homeostasis: Brondel & Cabanac 2007 (hedonic change to films), Cabanac
  2002 (hedonic ratings of maths and ethics options predict choice). If taken at face
  value, pleasure is reported for material with no direct regulatory content.

**What separates P.1 from C** would be (i) feeling that follows interoceptive prediction
error when no competing need is in play, and (ii) trade-offs proceeding normally when
interoceptive feeling is absent. For (i), alliesthesia with a single need (sweet taste
after a glucose load; warmth when cold) shows pleasantness shifting with need level with
no second need present — both accounts predict this. For (ii), no study found. The
peripheral-feedback results bear on a James–Lange reading of P; Seth's version concerns
central predictions and is not directly tested by them.

## 7. U.1 — species-level UAL cluster

**Birch, Ginsburg & Jablonka 2020, *Biol Philos* 35:56.** FT-s. UAL = associative learning
with (1) compound stimuli, (2) novel stimuli, (3) second-order conditioning, (4) trace
conditioning, (5) flexible revaluation. Said to be present in "most vertebrates", "some
cephalopod molluscs (the coleoid cephalopods)", "some arthropods (including honey bees and
fruit flies)"; lacking in "gastropod molluscs (such as *Aplysia*), annelids and
nematodes". Hedge: "the learning abilities of many species … have not been sufficiently
studied, so the distribution of UAL may be broader". UAL is offered as a **positive
marker, sufficient not necessary**: "it is not intended to tell us which animals are not
conscious." Predictions (b) the five elements are phylogenetically correlated; (d,e)
masking/blindsight abolish UAL but spare limited learning; (g) the three groups show the
whole package.

Note against the Amendment's U.1 wording ("species lacking it do not [show other marks]"):
the framework's own authors decline the negative half.

**Evidence on the cluster holding together.**

- Adult *Drosophila* show second-order conditioning (Tabone & de Belle 2011; Yamada et al.
  2023, ABS). Larval *Drosophila* show conditioned inhibition but **not** sensory
  preconditioning or second-order conditioning (Sen et al. 2024, *Learn Mem*, ABS). Same
  species, different life stage.
- *Aplysia* trace conditioning: asserted in a critique (SNIP); primary source **not
  retrieved**. *C. elegans* trace conditioning: reported as not demonstrated (SNIP).
- Full five-element cluster demonstrated within single species for the claimed taxa: I
  found no table doing this; Birch 2020 cites examples (rats, rabbits, pigeons, goldfish)
  per element. **Unverified** whether any invertebrate has all five shown.

**Critiques.** A *Biol Philos* 2021 paper (SNIP; s10539-021-09796-0, author not confirmed)
argues trace conditioning counts as evidence for consciousness on "only a small handful of
theories", making the approach "tacitly theory-heavy". Droege et al. 2021, *Anim Cogn*
(ABS): the human trace-conditioning/awareness link rests on a distractor manipulation, so
attention, "neither sufficient nor necessary", is doing the work. Whether trace
conditioning needs awareness in humans is disputed (another agent's brief).

**Does UAL's distribution coincide with independent sentience markers?**

| Taxon | UAL (Birch 2020) | LSE 2021 / insect review | Trade-off marker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertebrates | most | not assessed there | yes (rat, fish, iguana) |
| Coleoid cephalopods | some | very strong (octopods 7 of 8 high) | untested |
| Decapods | arthropods "some" | strong (true crabs 5 of 8 high) | medium (hermit crab); yes with interaction (shore crab 2024) |
| Insects | honeybee, fruit fly | adult Diptera, Blattodea 6/8 (SNIP) | disputed (bee); absent in one cockroach preprint |
| Gastropods, annelids | lacking | not assessed | not searched |
| Nematodes | lacking | *C. elegans* cited as a simple mechanism | **present and graded (Ghosh)** |
| Cnidarians | — | fail all criteria | inhibitable reflexes only |

Two cautions. LSE criterion 7 is itself "associative learning that goes beyond
habituation and sensitisation", so agreement between UAL and the eight-criteria score is
partly built in. And taxa lacking UAL were mostly not assessed on the other markers, so
the "species lacking it do not" half is absence of evidence. The one place the markers
come apart on present evidence is *C. elegans*: graded need-dependent trade-off, no UAL.

## 8. Where each piece touches the predictions (description only)

**C.1 (f lost/disordered when feeling blunted, b–e intact).** Bearing cases: adipsia
(intake fails without thirst; rule only partly compensates; need signal also lost); CIP
(injury continues despite knowledge; signal lost); asymbolia (sensation intact, protective
learning said to fail; clinical description). Disordered trade-off with feeling reported
intact: Reber 2017, Howard 2020, schizophrenia effort deficits with normal in-the-moment
ratings, Korb 2020. Feeling reduced with choice near normal or closer to expected value:
B24's De Martino, Shiv, Heims. No study with graded feeling reduced, awareness verified
and a need-level-dependent trade-off measured.

**C.2 (flexible trade-off organisms show other signs; fixed-priority organisms do not).**
For: rats; shore crabs (interaction plus anxiety-like and stress signs); goldfish/trout
(SNIP/ABS); bees if the interaction stands (plus bias studies). Against or complicating:
*C. elegans* graded trade-off in a 302-neuron animal by a sensory-gain loop; *Physarum*
quality-versus-light choice; bacterial receptor-ratio weighting; homeostatic RL agents;
octopus with strong independent signs and no trade-off test; cockroach credited on other
criteria yet showing no flexible adjustment (preprint); hermit-crab trade-off statistically
fragile; iguana trade-off insensitive to deprivation. In Cabanac 1992 pleasure in animals
is operationalised by choice.

**C.3 / C-strong.** Not gathered here beyond: feelings persist after cortical (insular,
cingulate, amygdala) destruction (n=1 each); Solms 2018 and Merker 2007 (ABS) locate
affect in upper brainstem. Whether b–e on affect-free material is conscious is another
brief's subject.

**P.1.** Single-need alliesthesia fits P and C alike. Interoceptive-accuracy–emotion link
weak in meta-analysis and its main measure is of doubtful validity; feeling survives loss
of peripheral feedback and of insula. No test separating "tracks interoceptive prediction"
from "tracks trade-offs".

**A.2.** Skeletomotor intention conflict is reported as experienced, a pupillary conflict
is not (one group, n=14–18, no independent replication). No test of f under unawareness of
action tendencies.

**U.1.** UAL taxa broadly coincide with taxa scoring on the eight criteria, with partial
circularity (criterion 7) and little evidence on taxa said to lack UAL. Its authors claim
sufficiency only. Trade-off and UAL markers diverge for *C. elegans*.
