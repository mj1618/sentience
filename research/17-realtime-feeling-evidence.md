# 17 — Evidence: feeling signalled in the moment, in states without later report

Evidence only, for scenario 17. No scoring against accounts D or C.

**Tags.** [V-full] = methods/results read in full text. [V-abs] = abstract only read (Europe PMC); full text
paywalled or unreachable. [R] = recalled from memory, not checked this round.
**Access note.** Sanders 2017, Gaskell 2017, Linassi 2018, all Russell papers and Hilgard's papers could not be
opened in full text. Their numbers below are abstract-level only.

## Row 1 — Isolated forearm technique (IFT) responders and cortical measures

- **Sanders et al. 2017, Anesthesiology** (ConsCIOUS-1). https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27984262 [V-abs]
  n=260, six sites, IFT tested once shortly after intubation. Responders 12/260 (4.6%). "5 of 12 responders
  reported pain through a second hand squeeze" (real time). No explicit recall in any participant (n=253
  questioned). Depth-of-anaesthesia monitor values "showed a wide range" in both groups but were higher in
  responders: before intubation 54 ± 20 vs 42 ± 14 (P=0.02); after 52 ± 16 vs 43 ± 16 (P=0.02). Sympathetic
  activation signs 50% vs 2.4%. Volatile exposure before intubation lowered odds of responding (OR 0.2).
  Not verified: which monitors, in how many patients, exact wording of the pain question, and whether pain
  signallers differed from other responders in EEG or opioid dose.
- **Lennertz et al. 2023, BJA** (ConsCIOUS-2). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10375493/ [V-full]
  n=338 completed, age 18–40, 69% female, 10 sites. Responders 37/338 (11%); women 31/232 (13%), men 6/106 (6%);
  adjusted OR for female sex 2.7. Command vs syllable-matched nonsense statement, randomised order: 13
  responded to command only, 4 to nonsense only, 7 to both (P=0.049). Pain item: "if you are in pain squeeze my
  hand two times"; **18/37 (49%) endorsed pain**. Only 4 answered a comprehension item correctly and 3
  incorrectly. Induction doses did not differ between responders and non-responders (propofol 2.1 vs
  2.2 mg/kg; fentanyl median 100 µg in both; no remifentanil recipient responded, 0 vs 14). Continuous
  anaesthesia before laryngoscopy lowered odds (OR 0.43). Forced-choice word memory at chance in both groups.
  Explicit recall: 1 definite (0.3%), 5 possible. **No EEG or depth-monitor values are reported in this
  paper.** Limits: heterogeneous responses, unblinded assessor, single time window.
- **Gaskell et al. 2017, BJA.** https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29121278 [V-abs]
  Frontal EEG in a 90-patient subset of ConsCIOUS-1; 6 responders. Three responded immediately after
  intubation with an alpha-delta dominant pattern (delta >20 dB, alpha >10 dB); their spectra were "similar to
  those of non-responders (P>0.05)" at those sites. Three others (one site) lacked the pattern, responded
  later, and had ketamine and less volatile. "None of the derived depth-of-anaesthesia indices could robustly
  discriminate." Critique: n=6 responders, frontal channels only, nothing reported on pain signals specifically.
- **Russell 2013a, Anaesthesia** (propofol-remifentanil, BIS target 55–60). https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23521699 [V-abs]
  n=22 women; 16 responded on 80 occasions during surgery; BIS flagged 47 (sensitivity 59%); BIS at response
  median 61 (range 37–80) vs 75 at post-operative eye opening; propofol effect-site concentration the same at
  both (2.0 vs 2.1 µg/ml). No recall of surgery.
- **Russell 2013b** (isoflurane, BIS 55–60). https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24047289 [V-abs]
  n=34; 11 responded on 32 occasions; BIS at response median 60 (range 36–83) vs 77 at eye opening. No recall.
- **Russell 2006, BJA** (Narcotrend-guided propofol). https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16443638 [V-abs]
  n=12, all responded (92 responses); 45% accompanied by a Narcotrend rise to a "conscious" stage; the rest
  showed no significant change.
- **Schneider et al. 2002.** https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11773816 [V-abs] n=20, propofol-alfentanil at
  BIS 50–60; 8/20 squeezed to command after intubation; BIS did not differ between responders and
  non-responders before or after; no recall.
- **Zand et al. 2014, BJA.** https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24531686 [V-abs] n=61 caesarean; IFT positive in
  41%, 46%, 23% at laryngoscopy, intubation, incision; BIS "could not reliably differentiate". No recall.
- **Evidence pointing the other way.** Kerssens et al. 2003, Anesthesiology
  https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12960540 [V-abs]: n=56, propofol-alfentanil at BIS 60–70, 1,082 commands;
  37 patients (66%) responded unequivocally; BIS was a "highly significant" predictor of response (P<0.0001);
  9 of the 37 recalled. Schneider et al. 2003 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12925469 [V-abs]: n=40, BIS 66 ± 17
  at non-response vs 79 ± 14 at response; prediction probability 0.68. Sanders 2017 group means above.
- **Linassi et al. 2018 meta-analysis, BJA.** https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29935574 [V-abs] 22 studies,
  1,131 patients, 393 (34.8%) IFT-positive; induction 19.7%, maintenance 31.2%; during maintenance lower with
  inhalational (0.18) than intravenous (0.48), and lower without brain-monitor guidance (0.19) than with (0.57).
  Low quality, high heterogeneity.
- **Pain signal versus surgical stimulation or EEG at that moment:** no study found that reports EEG at
  moments of pain signalling versus non-pain responses.

## Row 2 — Opioid versus hypnotic dose

- **Hypnotic dose and IFT response.** St Pierre et al. 2000 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/10818323 [V-abs]:
  n=30, fentanyl fixed at 2 µg/kg, etomidate 0.2/0.3/0.4 mg/kg gave IFT-positive rates 80%/70%/20%.
  Volatile exposure or continuous hypnotic before intubation lowered responding (Sanders 2017; Lennertz 2023).
- **Opioid dose and IFT response.** Lennertz 2023 [V-full]: opioid doses the same in responders and
  non-responders (see Row 1). Kart & Hanci 2018 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5991248/ [V-full]:
  n=90 caesarean, propofol alone vs + remifentanil 1 µg/kg vs + dexmedetomidine; IFT positivity "similar"
  (P>0.05; counts only in a figure). Schneider 2003: low vs high remifentanil arms, effect on responding not
  given in abstract.
- **Opioid dose and pain *signals* in responders:** no study found. Russell 1993
  https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8431332 [V-abs]: midazolam-alfentanil, n=32, 72% responded during
  surgery; "Twenty patients, asked specifically during surgery to indicate the presence or absence of pain,
  experienced pain at some time"; no spontaneous recall, 3 recalled on prompting. Pain occurred despite
  alfentanil; dose relation not reported.
- **Opioid changes responses to noxious stimulation, little effect on resting EEG.** Guignard et al. 2000
  https://europepmc.org/article/MED/10624998 [V-abs]: n=50, propofol fixed at 4 µg/ml, remifentanil 0/2/4/8/16
  ng/ml. BIS before laryngoscopy unaffected by remifentanil. Movers after intubation: 10, 9, 7, 1, 0 of 10. BIS,
  blood pressure and heart-rate rises after intubation fell with remifentanil dose. Movement here was gross
  movement, not response to command. Bouillon et al. 2004 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15166553 [V-abs]:
  n=20 volunteers; remifentanil alone did not abolish response to shaking/shouting or laryngoscopy; modest
  doses sharply cut the propofol needed for both; BIS and entropy more sensitive to propofol.
- **Real-time pain ratings: hypnotic versus analgesic.** Petersen-Felix et al. 1996
  https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8881619 [V-abs]: n=12; propofol and alfentanil reduced nociceptive and
  auditory evoked-potential amplitudes similarly, but "only alfentanil reduced the perceived pain"; propofol
  lowered pressure-pain tolerance (hyperalgesia). Frölich et al. 2005 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15673879
  [V-abs]: n=18; pain intensity 28/100 placebo, 35 mild, 40 moderate propofol sedation; unpleasantness 23, 29,
  33. Hofbauer et al. 2004 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/14739816 [V-abs]: n=15 PET; ratings rose at 0.5
  µg/ml with more thalamic and anterior cingulate response; at 3.5 µg/ml (unresponsive) thalamic and cingulate
  responses were absent and insular activation remained.
  All in responsive volunteers; no ratings possible at hypnotic doses.
- **Ketamine.** Sprenger et al. 2006 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16931688 [V-abs]: subanaesthetic
  S-ketamine, real-time ratings; unpleasantness fell 55%, intensity 23%, with dose-dependent falls in S2,
  insula and midcingulate activation. Experience at anaesthetic doses (Sarasso 2015): [R], not checked.

## Row 3 — Sedation with amnesia: pain at the time versus recalled

- **Boku et al. 2016, Anesth Prog.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5011957/ [V-full] n=246 dental
  patients, midazolam 0.03–0.08 mg/kg, then propofol infusion. Patients were asked at the time whether the
  injection hurt (sedation score 2–3, verbal); **102 said yes**. Asked again after the procedure, memory of
  that pain was abolished dose-dependently: ED95 0.061 mg/kg (30s) down to 0.033 mg/kg (60s). Sedation score
  did not predict amnesia (ROC area 0.31). How many of the 102 forgot is shown only in figures.
- IFT series: pain or command response at the time with 0% recall (Sanders 2017; Zand 2014; Schneider 2002),
  0.3% (Lennertz 2023), 3/32 on prompting (Russell 1993), 9/37 (Kerssens 2003).
- Recall rates in colonoscopy: 4% of 200 (Stait 2008, https://europepmc.org/article/MED/18853587 [V-abs]); 12%
  light vs 1% deep propofol (Allen 2015, n=200, https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26335904 [V-abs]). Neither
  recorded pain in real time.
- Gap: no endoscopy study found pairing observer- or patient-rated pain during the procedure with later recall.

## Row 4 — Nociceptive responses under deep anaesthesia

- **Lichtner et al. 2018, BJA.** https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29935584 [V-abs] n=10, deep propofol plus
  stepped remifentanil; noxious electrical stimuli. When movement, blood pressure and heart-rate responses were
  abolished, spinal reflex, somatosensory evoked potentials and fMRI brain responses were still "consistently
  observed"; very high opioid doses cut them only to 41% of maximum.
- **Lichtner et al. 2018, Neuroimage.** https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29421324 [V-abs] Intense noxious
  stimuli evoked spinal and cerebral responses during propofol burst suppression; rising propofol abolished
  insula and S2 responses and increased frontal responses.
- **Movement to noxious stimulus is largely spinal.** Rampil et al. 1993
  https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8466071 [V-abs]: rats, isoflurane MAC unchanged after precollicular
  decerebration (1.26 vs 1.30%). Antognini & Schwartz 1993 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8267200 [V-abs]:
  goats, anaesthetising the brain alone raised requirement from 1.2% to 2.9%.
- **Anand et al. 1987, Lancet.** https://europepmc.org/article/MED/2879174 [V-abs] Preterm babies, ductus
  ligation under nitrous oxide and curare, with (n=8) or without (n=8) fentanyl 10 µg/kg. Hormonal and
  metabolic stress responses and post-operative complications were greater without fentanyl. Anand & Hickey
  1992 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/1530752 [V-abs]: neonatal cardiac surgery, deaths 0/30 with high-dose
  sufentanil vs 4/15 with halothane-morphine.
- **Whether felt:** no study found that tests this. All measures are physiological and no report channel exists.

## Row 5 — Dream emotion versus brain measures

- **Sikka et al. 2019, J Neurosci.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6561691/ [V-full] n=17 (7 men), two
  nights, 126 REM awakenings, 115 dream reports. Frontal alpha asymmetry (F4 vs F3) in the REM period before
  waking predicted self-rated dream anger (R²=0.435, p=0.004). Evening waking asymmetry predicted it equally
  (R²=0.411, p=0.006); controlling for it, the REM relation was not significant (r=0.384, p=0.142), which the
  authors read as trait-like. Dream interest not predicted (p=0.074). Limits: ratings made after waking; two
  frontal electrodes; no measure of deep structures; one emotion.
- **Sterpenich et al. 2020, Hum Brain Mapp.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7267911/ [V-full]
  256-channel EEG, serial awakenings; within-subject fear vs no-fear dreams: N2, n=12 (14 vs 25 reports); REM,
  n=8 (12 vs 15 reports). Fear associated with source-localised insula activity, plus midcingulate in REM.
  Amygdala not identified. Authors note few observations and limits of EEG source localisation.
- **State level.** Maquet et al. 1996 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8774879 [V-abs]: n=7 PET, REM blood flow
  higher in pons, thalamus, amygdalae, anterior cingulate; lower in dorsolateral prefrontal. Not related to dream emotion.
- **Structural and lesion.** De Gennaro et al. 2011 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20740648 [V-abs]: n=34,
  lower left-amygdala microstructural integrity went with shorter dream reports and lower emotional load (home
  diaries). Blake et al. 2019 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30660955 [V-abs]: 8 patients with bilateral
  basolateral amygdala calcification, 23 reports vs 52 from 17 controls; patients' dreams more pleasant,
  shorter, simpler.
- **REM sleep behaviour disorder.** Bugalho et al. 2024 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39536526 [V-abs]:
  n=31, 873 enacted motor events scored for emotion on video; negative-emotion and violent events correlated
  with anxiety/depression scores. Real-time behavioural expression; no brain measure.
- **Direct correlation of amygdala activity with dream emotion:** none found.

## Row 6 — Disorders of consciousness

- **Laureys et al. 2002, Neuroimage.** https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12377148 [V-abs] 15 vegetative-state
  patients, 15 controls, PET with high-intensity median-nerve stimulation. Global metabolism 40% of normal.
  Midbrain, thalamus and primary somatosensory cortex activated "in each and every" patient; secondary
  somatosensory, insular, posterior parietal and anterior cingulate cortices in none; S1 functionally
  disconnected from higher areas.
- **Boly et al. 2008, Lancet Neurol.** https://europepmc.org/article/MED/18835749 [V-abs] 5 minimally conscious,
  15 vegetative, 15 controls. Minimally conscious patients activated thalamus, S1, S2/insula, frontoparietal
  and anterior cingulate like controls (no area less active), all more than vegetative patients. n=5.
- **Markl et al. 2013.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3607151/ [V-abs] fMRI, 30 unresponsive
  patients: 50% activated the sensory pain network, 30% the affective network.
- **Chatelle et al. 2014.** https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24065132 [V-abs] n=49, FDG-PET: Nociception Coma
  Scale-Revised score correlated with posterior anterior-cingulate metabolism only; no effect of diagnosis.
  Bonin et al. 2020 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31733361 [V-abs]: PET-confirmed unresponsive patients
  scored at most 4; patients scoring ≥5 had preserved left insula metabolism.
- **Perturbational complexity versus pain responses:** none found.

## Row 7 — Hypnotic analgesia and the hidden observer

- Knox, Morgan & Hilgard 1974 (https://europepmc.org/article/MED/4832190) and Hilgard et al. 1978
  (https://europepmc.org/article/MED/739088): titles confirmed, no abstracts available. [R]: highly
  hypnotisable subjects reporting little overt pain gave near-normal pain ratings in real time through a
  covert channel (key pressing or automatic writing).
- Spanos & Hewitt 1980 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/7205550 [V-abs]: 8 + 8 highly susceptible subjects;
  told the hidden part feels more pain, it reported more; told it feels less, it reported less.
- Rainville et al. 1997 https://europepmc.org/article/MED/9252330 [V-abs]: suggestion changed unpleasantness
  without intensity; anterior cingulate activity followed unpleasantness, S1 unchanged.

## Row 9 — Pleasure signalled in real time

- None found. Adjacent, recalled-after only: one "good dream" (Russell 2013b); 25.5% dreaming under
  colonoscopy sedation, dreamers more satisfied (Stait 2008).

## Summary

| Row | Best evidence | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| 1 IFT responders vs cortical measures | Lennertz 2023 (pain 18/37, no EEG); Sanders 2017 (monitor values higher in responders, wide overlap); Gaskell 2017 (3/6 responders with alpha-delta pattern); Russell 2013; Kerssens 2003 (BIS predicts response) | Moderate for responder-level EEG, mixed in direction; none for EEG at pain-signal moments |
| 2 Opioid vs hypnotic | Hypnotic dose alters IFT responding (St Pierre 2000); opioid dose does not (Lennertz 2023; Kart 2018); opioid alters real-time pain ratings and movement, propofol does not reduce ratings (Petersen-Felix 1996; Frölich 2005; Guignard 2000) | Moderate for responding and for awake ratings; none for pain signals in IFT responders by dose |
| 3 Sedation with amnesia | Boku 2016 (102 reported pain at the time; memory abolished dose-dependently); IFT recall rates | Moderate (one direct study; endoscopy data lacking) |
| 4 Nociception under deep anaesthesia | Lichtner 2018 (both); Rampil 1993; Anand 1987 | Strong that responses persist; none on whether felt |
| 5 Dream emotion vs brain | Sikka 2019 (n=17, frontal, trait-like); Sterpenich 2020 (n=8–12, insula/midcingulate); Blake 2019 lesion | Weak; no direct deep-structure correlation study |
| 6 Disorders of consciousness | Laureys 2002; Boly 2008; Chatelle 2014; Markl 2013 | Moderate (abstract-level; small minimally conscious sample) |
| 7 Hidden observer | Hilgard 1974/78 [R]; Spanos & Hewitt 1980 | Weak (contested, primary sources unread) |
| 9 Pleasure in real time | — | None found |
