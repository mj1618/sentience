# 20 — Evidence for "identity or something added" (scenario 20, Part 1b)

Evidence only; no scoring of ID / HO-L. Tags: **[V-full]** methods and results read in full text (supplements not read); **[V-abs]** abstract only (full text paywalled or behind a bot wall); **[R]** recalled, unchecked. Ids resolve at `https://europepmc.org/article/MED/<PMID>` or `https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/<PMCID>`.

Fixed definitions (scenario 20, Part 1b). **A**: the same value drives at least two distinct criterion-meeting uses other than the affect rating, one multistep or first-trial novel; or a sustained, decodable frontoparietal value code. Single-step devaluation sensitivity alone is not A. **F**: self-rated good/bad valence, anticipatory and consummatory; emotion naming does not count.

**General finding.** No study located measures both A (by this definition) and F in the same participants under a manipulation of either. Every entry below is missing at least one of the two.

## Row 1′ — A met, performance matched: does reported valence differ?

**No "relative blindsight" design for valence, value or pain was found** (Europe PMC searches: relative blindsight; matched performance + unpleasantness; hypnosis/placebo + choice/willingness to pay; metacognition of emotion/valence/pain).

*The perceptual template*
- **Lau & Passingham 2006, PNAS** (PMC1693736) **[V-full]**. fMRI n=14 (13 analysed); separate behavioural study n=8. Metacontrast masking at two stimulus-onset asynchronies. Discrimination 68.27% vs 70.22% correct (P=0.213); "seen" reports 49.17% vs 60.13% (P=0.007); the difference tracked left mid-dlPFC activity. Square/diamond shapes; no value, no affect. A: not assessed; F: not measured; performance matched: yes (perception only).
- **Beck et al. 2019, Cognition** (PMC6411924) **[V-full, partial]**. Minimum n=24. Confidence in *intensity* discrimination for contrast, warmth and nociceptive heat, accuracy staircased. No unpleasantness rating, no choice, no brain manipulation. A: not assessed; F: not measured; performance matched: yes.

*Pain affect altered by suggestion or context*
- **Rainville et al. 1997, Science** (PMID 9252330) **[V-abs]**. Hypnotic suggestion altered rated unpleasantness of noxious stimuli "without changing the perceived intensity"; anterior cingulate activity changed, somatosensory did not. Sample size not verified; no choice or avoidance measure mentioned. A: not assessed; F: changed by suggestion; performance matched: n.a. (intensity rating held, no behaviour task).
- **Winston et al. 2014, J Neurosci** (PMC4212059) **[V-full, partial]**. 24 recruited, 21 analysed. Incentive-compatible bids (from a 40p endowment) to avoid electric shocks at low/medium/high intensity; blocks contained only two levels. Bids to avoid medium pain were 2.5p lower in medium–high than in medium–low blocks (p=0.05; 6p in Vlaev et al. 2009). OFC encoded stimulus rank; pain-responsive regions were context-independent. Pain was rated only during calibration, **not by context**, so whether rated unpleasantness moved with the bid is unknown. A: not shown (one use: bidding); F: not measured per condition; performance matched: n.a.
- **Borckardt et al. 2011, Pain** (PMC3017430) — see row 3′; perceived control lowered unpleasantness ratings; no choice measure.

*Value-based choice with confidence or liking*
- **De Martino et al. 2013, Nat Neurosci** (PMC3786394) **[V-full, partial]**. 28 recruited, 20 analysed, fasted 4 h. Binary choices among snack items, each pair shown twice; confidence in each choice; post-scan incentive-compatible bids with bid confidence. Value difference explained 17.7% of confidence variance; 14.7% of choices reversed on repetition, with lower initial confidence. Right rostrolateral PFC tracked confidence but not value difference. Confidence concerns choice correctness, not valence; no liking rating. Two uses of the same value (choice, bid), both single-step. A: not shown (no multistep/novel use; value code was vmPFC, not frontoparietal); F: not measured; performance matched: no (accuracy rose with confidence).
- **Lebreton et al. 2015, Nat Neurosci** (PMID 26192748) **[V-abs]**. Two behavioural experiments and three fMRI data sets; confidence related quadratically to first-order ratings (age, value, probability) and the quadratic term was expressed in vmPFC without any confidence judgment being required. A: not assessed; F: pleasantness-type ratings present, not at floor; performance matched: n.a.
- **Patients with consistent preferences and absent liking**: none located (file 19 found none for depersonalisation, asymbolia or anhedonia). Preference formation in amnesia **[R]** concerns preferences without *recognition*, not without liking.

*Automatic valuation*
- **Lebreton et al. 2009, Neuron** (PMID 19914190) **[V-abs]**. In the scanner participants rated pleasantness (explicit task) or age (distractor task) of faces, houses, paintings; after scanning they chose between pairs. Regions reflecting both ratings and later choices did so in both tasks. Stimuli were fully visible and attended; pleasantness was rated for the same items. A: not shown (one later use: binary preference); F: present; performance matched: n.a. Sample size not in abstract.
- **Tusche, Bode & Haynes 2010, J Neurosci** (PMC6632699) **[V-full, partial]**. Between groups, male car enthusiasts: high attention n=17 (rated attractiveness of each car, 4-point), low attention n=15 (fixation task, 89% correct, cars task-irrelevant in the background). Afterwards both groups answered a **hypothetical** "Would you buy this car?" (No/Not sure/Yes), having been unaware a choice would follow. Decoding of later choice: low-attention medial PFC 76%, insula 82%/72%; high-attention medial frontal 82%, vmPFC 73% (chance 50%). No rating during unattended exposure; the low-attention group rated attractiveness afterwards. A: not shown (one hypothetical, single-step use); F: not measured at exposure, present afterwards; performance matched: n.a.
- **Grueschow et al. 2015, Neuron** (PMID 25640078) **[V-abs]**. Medial PFC value activity increased when value was choice-relevant and related to choice reliability; posterior cingulate represented value whether relevant or not and predicted attentional capture by irrelevant value. A: not assessed; F: not reported in abstract; performance matched: n.a.

None of the three automatic-valuation studies shows value in two criterion-meeting uses without felt valence: stimuli were visible, liking ratings were obtained, and the downstream use was one single-step choice.

## Row 2′ — Single-step goal-directed choice with feeling at floor or unmeasured

- **Pessiglione et al. 2008, Neuron** (PMC2572733) **[V-full, partial]**. n=20 (2 more discarded for describing cue parts). Masked abstract cues (33 or 50 ms) signalled +£1, £0 or −£1 for a "Go" response. Awareness: same/different discrimination of masked cues, 48% ± 3% correct, d′ = 0.08 ± 0.20, tested before and after. Go was chosen more after reward than punishment cues; plateau 63% ± 5% correct; ventral striatum tracked cue value. Afterwards, unmasked cues were rated most-to-least liked: reward cues rated higher than punishment cues (p<0.01). No devaluation or contingency-change test, so goal-directed status unverified. A: not shown; F: no rating during masked trials; differential liking present once cues were visible; performance matched: n.a.
- **Garofalo et al. 2020, Sci Rep** (PMC7368056) **[V-full]**. Exp 1 n=30, Exp 2 n=30, Exp 3 n=15. Pavlovian-instrumental transfer with masked cues. Cue liking (post − pre) rose for rewarded cues in all experiments. *Outcome-specific* transfer: present at 600 ms; **absent at 16 ms** (Exp 1, p=1, BF10=0.36); present at 33 ms (Exp 2, "perceptible"); in Exp 3 present on trials reported seen (t(14)=4.49) and absent on unseen trials (t(14)=−0.09, BF10=0.32). *General* transfer was present under masking in all three. Awareness in Exps 1–2 was assumed from duration and a pilot, not tested per participant. No devaluation. A: not shown; F: cue liking present (rated with visible cues); performance matched: no.
- **Seabrooke et al. 2017, JEP:ALC** (PMID 28627906) **[V-abs]**. Three experiments, visible cues. One outcome made to taste unpleasant; overall choice shifted away from it. Outcome-specific transfer was **not** reduced by devaluation (Exp 1); with cues signalling both outcomes equally, cue-elicited choice was devaluation-sensitive (Exps 2, 3). No awareness manipulation; n and ratings not in abstract. A: not shown; F: not verified; performance matched: n.a.
- **LeDoux & Daw 2018, Nat Rev Neurosci** (PMID 29593300) **[V-abs]**. Taxonomy with three goal-directed categories: direct action–outcome behaviours and actions based on "implicit or explicit forecasting of outcomes". **What empirical work they cite for implicit forecasting could not be checked** (paywalled).
- No human study was found showing devaluation-sensitive responding with rated pleasantness unchanged or at floor. The reverse (pleasantness falls, responding unchanged) is in file 19 (Tricomi 2009; Pool 2022). In animals F is unmeasurable.
- Winkielman 2005 and Lamb 1991 are in `research/04` and `research/18`; not re-gathered.

## Row 3′ — Bilateral lateral/polar prefrontal disruption verified by a metacognitive drop

**No study was found that verified a metacognitive drop after bilateral lateral or polar prefrontal disruption and also measured self-rated valence or value-guided choice.** The pieces exist only separately.

*Is the metacognitive drop itself established?*
- **Rounis et al. 2010, Cogn Neurosci** (PMID 24168333) **[V-abs]**. Bilateral dlPFC continuous theta-burst (cTBS; 300 pulses per side per Bor 2017) vs sham, within subjects, n=20 (per Bor 2017). Lower visibility ratings for correct trials, lower metacognitive sensitivity, discrimination unchanged. No affect.
- **Bor et al. 2017, PLoS One** (PMC5305100) **[V-full]**. Exp 1 between subjects: 90 recruited, 53 analysed (bilateral dlPFC n=12, vertex n=12, three other sites); confidence labels replaced visibility; active vertex control. No effect on meta-d′ − d′ (F(4,48)=0.06), type II d′ or phi; Bayes factors near or below 0.33. With excluded "unstable" participants restored, dlPFC vs vertex was significant (one-tailed p=0.037), driven by a rise in controls. Exp 2 within subjects: 27 recruited, 17 analysed; mean change +0.07 (Rounis: −0.35). No affect.
- **Ruby, Maniscalco & Peters 2018, Conscious Cogn** (PMC5964034) **[V-full, partial]**. Simulations only: argue Bor's design and ~30% exclusions lowered power. Bor et al. replied (PMC6204884, not read).
- **Lapate et al. 2020, Commun Biol** (PMC7347936) **[V-full, partial]**. 33 tested; cTBS to **left** mid-lateral PFC vs somatosensory control, within subjects. Metacognition of face *orientation* fell (type 2 AUC t(27)=−2.33, p=0.027; meta-d′ p=0.046; meta-d′ − d′ p=0.094); metacognition of face *emotion* did not (n=32; p≥0.49). d′ and visibility ratings unchanged. Unilateral.
- **Shekhar & Rahnev 2018** (PMID 29720553) **[V-abs]**: online TMS to dlPFC lowered confidence; to anterior PFC *increased* metacognitive ability. **Fleming et al. 2014, Brain** (PMID 25100039) **[V-abs]**: anterior PFC lesions (n=7) lowered perceptual metacognitive efficiency (meta-d′/d′ CI 0.28–0.64) with performance matched; memory metacognition spared. Neither measured affect.

*Lateral prefrontal disruption and rated feeling (no metacognitive check, except as noted)*
- **Lapate et al. 2017, Psychol Sci** (PMC5725229) **[V-full, partial]**. n=27; **same larger study as Lapate 2020** (both papers say so). cTBS to left lateral PFC vs somatosensory control. Mood before/after: no site effect on PANAS positive (F(1,26)=0.54) or negative affect (F=0.98), implicit affect, or state anxiety. Likeability of novel neutral faces (1–4) became biased by a preceding briefly shown fearful vs happy face after lateral PFC cTBS (interaction F(1,26)=7.24, p=.012), not after control; still detectable 3 days later (F(1,23)=4.97). PANAS is adjective-based mood, not rated valence of a stimulus. A: not assessed; F (mood): unchanged; evaluative ratings: changed; performance matched: n.a.
- **Gillihan et al. 2011, SCAN** (PMC3023090) **[V-full, partial]**. Lateral frontal lesions n=8 (stroke or resection; laterality not checked here), ventromedial n=7, controls n=15. Lateral group: dispositional positive affect not different from controls, negative affect higher (t(21)=2.34); **greater** self-rated sadness to a sad mood induction (t(20)=3.02, p=0.007); happiness induction and recovery normal. No metacognition or choice task. A: not assessed; F: present, not reduced; performance matched: n.a.
- **Hornak et al. 2003, Brain** (PMID 12805109) **[V-abs]**. Surgical excisions; dorsolateral/other-medial group "unimpaired" on subjective emotional state, social behaviour and emotion identification; bilateral orbitofrontal and unilateral anterior cingulate/medial BA9 lesions changed subjective emotional state. Group sizes and laterality of dorsolateral lesions not verified. A: not assessed; F: unchanged (questionnaire); performance matched: n.a.
- **Krummenacher et al. 2010, Pain** (PMID 19875233) **[V-abs]**. Low-frequency rTMS to left and right dlPFC, or sham-as-placebo, before expectation-induced placebo analgesia. Placebo raised heat pain threshold and tolerance; rTMS "did not affect pain experience" but "completely blocked placebo analgesia". Outcomes named are threshold and tolerance; unpleasantness ratings and n not verified.
- **Borckardt et al. 2011, Pain** (PMC3017430) **[V-full, partial]**. n=24, real vs sham between groups; **excitatory** 10 Hz rTMS to left prefrontal (F3), 4,000 pulses, during the task. No main effect of rTMS on intensity or unpleasantness. Perceived control lowered unpleasantness by 6.96 points under sham; this was abolished under real rTMS (interaction F(1,309)=6.55, p=.011); rated control lower (5.13 vs 7.21).
- **Taylor et al. 2012, Pain** (PMC3530383) **[V-full, partial]**. n=24; excitatory 10 Hz left dlPFC rTMS vs sham, naloxone vs saline. Real rTMS lowered rated intensity of a fixed heat stimulus from 6.92 to 4.42 (0–10); "identical results" for unpleasantness; naloxone abolished this. Taylor 2013 (PMID 23314221) and Brighina 2011 (PMC3072504) **[V-abs]**: same direction. Excitatory and unilateral, i.e. not disruption.

*Lateral prefrontal disruption and valuation or planning*
- **Camus et al. 2009, Eur J Neurosci** (PMID 19912330) **[V-abs]**. rTMS to right dlPFC during an economic valuation task with real foods lowered the values assigned relative to control. Protocol, n, and whether liking was rated separately from bids: not verified.
- **Smittenaar et al. 2013, Neuron** (PMC3893454) **[V-full, partial]**. n=25, within subjects; theta-burst to right dlPFC, left dlPFC, or vertex before a two-step task. Right dlPFC shifted the model-based/model-free balance toward model-free (p=0.01 vs vertex); left did not (p=0.63); model-free unaffected. Unilateral; no feeling rating; no metacognitive check. A: one multistep use reduced; F: not measured.
- **Smittenaar et al. 2014, PLoS One** (PMC3901733) **[V-full, partial]**. 23 recruited; tDCS over right dlPFC; no effect reported on either control system.

## Direct tests of LeDoux & Brown

- **LeDoux & Brown 2017, PNAS** (PMC5347624) **[V-full, partial]**. Theory paper. Main-text prediction: damage to first-order subcortical circuits "should mute but not eliminate feelings of fear"; others in SI Box 10 (not read).
- **Taschereau-Dumouchel et al. 2024, Phil Trans B** (PMC11444220) **[V-full, partial]**. Fear group n=30, no-fear group n=30. Fear-rating profiles over 30 animals were decodable from ventral visual cortex equally in both groups; groups differed in information synchronisation between visual areas and ventromedial/medial prefrontal cortex, not amygdala. Correlational; fear ratings (emotion naming), not valence; no choice task.
- **Taschereau-Dumouchel et al. 2018, PNAS** (PMC5879705) **[V-full, partial]**. n=17, double-blind decoded neurofeedback. Amygdala (t(16)=2.41) and skin-conductance responses to the targeted feared animal fell; participants could not identify the target (47%). Subjective-rating results not located in the sections read. Physiology is not A.
- **Cushing et al. 2024, Psychiatry Clin Neurosci** (PMC11488623) **[V-full, partial]**. Review by proponents; concedes prefrontal lesions "tend not to abolish self-reports of fear" and attributes this to redundancy and the need for extensive bilateral damage.
- Unconscious emotion with strict awareness checks: Winkielman 2005, Lamb 1991 — see `research/04`, `research/18`.
