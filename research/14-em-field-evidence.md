# 14 — Evidence for scenario 13 (EM-field theories vs neurons-only)

Evidence only; no scoring. Gathered 2026-10-03 via Europe PMC / PMC full text.
Tags: **[V-full]** methods/results read; **[V-abs]** abstract only; **[R]** recalled, not re-checked.
Units: 1 V/m = 1 mV/mm. PMC links: `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/<PMCID>/`.

## 0. What F's proponents actually claim

- **McFadden 2020** (Neurosci Conscious; PMC7507405) [V-full]. Consciousness is information physically integrated in the brain's EM field; the field acts back on voltage-gated channels (the cemi loop). Cites TMS and applied-field studies as confirmation that "appropriately structured" external fields influence firing and thought.
  - *Seizures:* widespread synchrony in a seizure is "empty of informational content that correlates with the outside world", so non-conscious. Same argument for absence seizures in McFadden 2022 (PMC9889563) [V-full].
  - *External fields:* TMS/MRI fields are, to the brain, incoherent signals, "perhaps capable of disrupting, but not forming coherent thoughts". Earlier answer (McFadden 2000, via Pockett's Scholarpedia): CSF acts as a Faraday cage [secondary].
  - *Split brain:* field perturbations fall off by an inverse-cube law, are "highly local", and need relays of synchronised neurons through the callosum; cutting it locks field information in each hemisphere.
  - *Anaesthesia:* listed as an unanswered question (2020). *Cerebellum:* not discussed in 2020; in 2022 he says its folding causes currents to cancel, citing Andersen 2020 for its "invisibility" to EEG/MEG (see row 4: that review argues the opposite).
  - McFadden 2002 and 2013 (J Conscious Stud) not retrieved [R].
- **Pockett**, Scholarpedia "Field theories of consciousness" 2013 (http://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Field_theories_of_consciousness) [V-full]; 2000 book and 2012 JCS paper not retrieved [R].
  - Experience is identical with certain spatial EM patterns; the proposed mark of a conscious pattern is a neutral region at cortical lamina 4. Areas lacking lamina 4 (motor cortex, hippocampus, cerebellum, subcortex) cannot generate conscious patterns.
  - She argues, against McFadden, that conscious patterns can *not* act causally on their own brain (dipole fields fall with distance cubed), while accepting that fields affect neurons in general.
  - *External fields:* radio is the wrong frequency; mains radiates negligibly; MRI fields lack spatial patterning, so the conscious pattern "rides" on them uncoupled. Only an externally applied inverse spatial pattern should cancel experience. Says MRI does not alter in-magnet EEG.
  - *Split brain:* two answers: an unsplittable system may be field-borne; and fields are too local to reach contralateral language areas.
  - Seizures / slow-wave sleep: no statement found in sources read.
  - Pockett & Holmes 2009 (PMID 19775914) [V-abs], intracranial grids, n=3: after generalised seizures, gamma power and phase synchrony were *higher* in the unconscious state; concludes neither is a correlate of consciousness.
- **Hunt & Jones 2023** (PMC10400444) [V-full]. Fields, not spikes, are the primary seat of integration; relies on the ephaptic literature below. Notes firing synchrony occurs without consciousness in anaesthesia and seizures. No quantitative treatment of external fields, split brain or cerebellar cancellation. Describes Chiang 2019 as 4-AP epileptiform waves; the paper used half-Ca/half-Mg slow activity (row 5).
- **Hales** (Hales & Ericson 2022, PMC9245352; Hales & Pockett 2014, PMC4264498) [V-full, skimmed]. All neural correlates are EM field phenomena; argument is foundational, with no specific predictions on rows 2–8 found. Hales 2014 (J Integr Neurosci) not retrieved.

Wording notes for the adjudicator: (a) both lead proponents predict *no* experiential effect of spatially unstructured external fields; (b) both invoke rapid fall-off for split brain; (c) Pockett excludes the cerebellum by lamination, not cancellation.

## 1. Field feedback (ephaptic)

- **Fröhlich & McCormick 2010**, Neuron (PMC3139922) [V-full]. Endogenous field during slow oscillation in anaesthetised ferret V1 in vivo: 2.29 ± 0.27 mV/mm, peak 3.89 (7 penetrations, 5 animals). In slices the field was smaller (0.87). Applied 2–4 mV/mm shortened Down states; sine fields of 1 mV/mm and above enhanced rhythmicity (0.5 mV/mm did not); 2 mV/mm shifted membrane potential 0.49 mV. Real-time positive/negative feedback fields altered network dynamics in vitro. A 2 mV/mm sine field also modulated the oscillation in vivo. Limits: slice-based, anaesthetised, slow oscillation only.
- **Anastassiou et al. 2011**, Nat Neurosci (PMID 21240273) [V-abs]. Rat cortical slices; field-induced somatic changes under 0.5 mV yet strong spike entrainment below 8 Hz. Krause 2019 summarises it as about 0.75 V/m with entrainment at 1 Hz but not 8 or 30 Hz [secondary].
- **Ozen et al. 2010**, J Neurosci (PMC2937280) [V-full]. Rats, anaesthetised (n=16) plus chronic sleeping/resting animals; about 1 mV/mm (measured post mortem) sufficed to phase-bias spiking; fraction entrained rose with intensity (6% to 69%).
- **Krause et al. 2019**, PNAS (PMC6431188) [V-full]. Two awake fixating macaques, 197 neurons (hippocampus, basal ganglia); measured field 0.2–0.35 V/m; spike timing entrained, firing rate unchanged (14/197 rate changes, chance level). Vieira 2020 (PMC7553316) [V-abs]: entrainment survived topical anaesthesia of skin.
- **Johnson et al. 2020**, Sci Adv (PMC7467690) [V-full, results]. Awake macaques, n=34 neurons; 8.9 / 17.6 / 26.5% entrained at median fields of about 0.4 / 0.8 / 1.2 mV/mm.
- **Han et al. 2018**, Neuron (PMC7513896) [V-abs]. Awake mice: neighbouring Purkinje cells synchronise at sub-millisecond scale through ephaptic coupling.
- **Pinotsis & Miller 2023**, Cereb Cortex (PMC10472500) [V-full]. Two macaques, working-memory delay, 32-channel LFP arrays in FEF/SEF. Measured: LFPs only. The "electric field" is computed from a bidomain model fitted to those LFPs; the feedback claim rests on Granger causality and Bayesian model comparison between two model-derived series. No field manipulation; authors note model fields are an upper bound.
- All the above are *applied* fields or modelling, except the slice feedback clamp and Han 2018. No study removes the endogenous field in an awake brain while leaving synapses intact.

## 2. External fields and experience

- **Field actually delivered.** Huang 2017, eLife (PMC5370189) [V-full]: 10 epilepsy patients, 1380 electrodes; maximum about 0.28 V/m per mA, about 0.8 V/m at 2 mA. Lafon 2017 (PMC5662600) [V-full]: 13 patients, median 0.02 V/m per mA, peak 0.16 V/m at 2.5 mA; no entrainment of sleep spindles. Vöröslakos 2018 (PMC5797140) [V-full]: rats and human cadavers; about 75% of scalp current shunted; at least 1 mV/mm needed for spiking effects; 4–6 mA estimated necessary in humans (the 4–6 mA figure is an estimate from cadaver and EEG data).
- **Confounds.** Asamoah 2019 (PMC6336776) [V-full]: tACS motor/tremor effects reproduced by skin-only stimulation and reduced by scalp anaesthesia; skin fields about 20 V/m per mA versus under 1 V/m in cortex. Phosphenes: Kar & Krekelberg 2012 (PMC3545027) [V-abs] and Laakso & Hirata 2013 [V-abs] attribute tACS phosphenes to retinal current; Schutter 2025 [V-abs] reviews this.
- **Perception tracking phase.** Fiene 2022 (PMID 34990876) [V-abs]: brightness of flicker varied with tACS phase under occipital but not periorbital montage. de Graaf 2020 (PMC7317496) [V-abs]: three experiments, no consistent phase effect on detection; post hoc reaction-time effect. None compares the experiential change against the measured firing change.
- **Cognition/mood.** Grover 2023 meta-analysis (PMC10860714) [V-abs]: 102 studies, modest tACS gains, larger offline. Horvath 2015 [V-abs]: no reliable single-session tDCS cognitive effect in 59 analyses.
- **Level of consciousness.** Thibaut 2014 [V-abs]: tDCS raised CRS-R in minimally conscious patients (n=30), not in vegetative state (n=25); transient. Voss 2014 [V-abs]: 25–40 Hz frontal tACS in REM increased lucidity (n=27, 250 µA [R]). Blanchette-Carrière 2020 [V-abs]: lucid dreams occurred equally under sham.
- **Strong fields.** MRI gradients: nerve-stimulation rheobase 2.2 V/m (Roemer 2021, PMC8651073 [V-full]); in-brain gradient field magnitudes not verified. Magnetophosphene threshold 8.14 mT at 20 Hz gives roughly 0.06–0.09 V/m in brain; the effect is attributed to retina (Hirata 2011 [V-abs]). Static-field vertigo is attributed to vestibular Lorentz forces [R]. RF fields (tens of V/m or more at 64–300 MHz) and TMS (about 100 V/m, suprathreshold) [R]. No reports found of altered experience from gradient or RF fields beyond nerve stimulation, phosphenes and vertigo.

## 3. Seizures and slow-wave sleep

- Endogenous fields: normal activity 1–10 mV/mm; epileptic activity about 20, up to 70 mV/mm (introduction of Subramanian 2025, PMC12013223, citing earlier work) [secondary]. Slow oscillation 2–4 mV/mm under anaesthesia (Fröhlich, above). Awake-state cortical field strength in mV/mm: no direct figure found.
- Scalp EEG amplitudes larger in slow-wave sleep and spike-wave seizures than waking [R].
- Proponent reply: information content, not amplitude (section 0). Pockett & Holmes 2009: more synchrony when unconscious.
- No study found that quantifies field information content independently of firing-pattern stereotypy.

## 4. Field geometry and cerebellum

- **Andersen, Jerbi & Dalal 2020**, NeuroImage (PMC7306153) [V-full]. Reviews the "closed field" claim and argues against it: Purkinje cells are aligned like pyramidal cells (open field); turtle cerebellum gave 1 pT at 17 mm from a 10 mm³ patch; a growing set of MEG/EEG studies localise cerebellar activity. Weak signals attributed to depth, local activation and sensor coverage.
- **Samuelsson 2020**, Hum Brain Mapp (PMC7244390) [V-abs]. Model from 9.4 T ex vivo human cerebellum: despite cancellation, cerebellar signal is on average only 30–60% weaker than cortical.
- Han 2018: strong local ephaptic effects in cerebellar cortex. Pockett lists cerebellum among sites where endogenous fields affect function.
- No structure identified with strong firing and near-zero local field. Stellate cells (cortical lamina 4) are closed-field per Pockett; no experiential test.

## 5. Activity crossing a cut

- **Chiang et al. 2019**, J Physiol (PMC6312416) [V-full]. Mouse (P10–30) longitudinal hippocampal slices, 400 µm thick, in vitro; slow periodic activity induced by half-Ca/half-Mg solution (not 4-AP). Speed 0.10 ± 0.01 m/s, unchanged after a complete cut (36 events, 3 slices); cut verified by separating and rejoining halves. Propagation stopped when the gap was 400 µm. Persisted with synaptic transmission blocked (low Ca). Model field about 5 mV/mm; applied fields under 5 mV/mm triggered or blocked waves (30 events, 2 slices).
- **Qiu et al. 2015**, J Neurosci (PMC4666910) [V-full]. 4-AP spikes; measured field 1.6–7.1, mean 3.5 mV/mm (65 spikes, 5 hippocampi); opposing field of 1–5 mV/mm slowed propagation.
- **Subramanian et al. 2022**, Exp Neurol (PMC10214533) [V-full]. In vivo analogue: 4 isoflurane-anaesthetised rats, 4-AP, knife transection of hippocampus. 54% of interictal spikes and 70% of seizure events crossed; theta crossed at 0.16 ± 0.12 m/s; a dielectric sheet in the cut blocked crossing. Cut width and completeness (histology) not confirmed in what was read; fields not reported in mV/mm.
- All positive results come from one laboratory (Durand), in densely packed hippocampus, in vitro or anaesthetised, mostly epileptiform. No awake or neocortical in vivo demonstration found.

## 6. Split brain

- No measurement or model of endogenous field strength across the interhemispheric fissure found.
- Indirect: ephaptic crossing failed at a 400 µm gap (Chiang 2019); the fissure contains CSF and falx, with medial surfaces millimetres apart [R].
- Proponents (section 0) both say the field is too local to unify hemispheres. Pockett additionally suggests residual unity might be field-borne but gives no data.

## 7. Same firing, different field

- **Xie et al. 2013**, Science (PMC3880190) [V-abs]. Mice: interstitial space +60% in natural sleep and anaesthesia. Confounded: brain state and firing change simultaneously.
- **Qiu 2015** osmolarity [V-full]: hypo-osmolar solution sped, hyper-osmolar slowed field-mediated propagation in slices; their model predicted little dependence on extracellular resistivity. Changes firing; no behavioural readout.
- **Skull defects.** Breach rhythm = larger scalp EEG over a defect [V-abs, case series]; Lafon 2017 models found craniotomy defects changed intracranial applied fields little. Syndrome of the trephined improves with cranioplasty [V-abs], attributed to pressure and CSF dynamics; confounded.
- Conductive or insulating material on cortex in awake surgery, hydrocephalus, hypothermia as field manipulations with an experiential readout: **none found**.

## 8. Anaesthesia

- **Lewis et al. 2012**, PNAS (PMC3523833) [V-full]. Three epilepsy patients, 198 units, propofol induction. Spike rate fell 81–92% at 35–85 s after loss of consciousness, then recovered: at 4 min, 33–117% of baseline across patients; 55% of units not different from baseline, 10% higher. Spiking confined to slow-oscillation phases that were asynchronous across sites more than 2 cm apart; local (<4 mm) structure preserved. Phase-locking *magnitude* between sites was conserved, with a slight increase.
- **Bastos et al. 2021**, eLife (PMC8079153) [V-abs]. Macaques, propofol: slow-frequency synchrony, decreased spiking, reduced coherence above 4 Hz; thalamic stimulation reversed these and roused the animals.
- **Akeju et al. 2016** (PMC4871620) [V-abs]. Ketamine, 12 patients: unconsciousness with gamma bursts (27–40 Hz) alternating with slow-delta.
- **Pal et al. 2020**, J Neurosci (PMC6961988) [V-full, methods/abstract]. Rats under continuous sevoflurane woken by prefrontal carbachol (n=10): high-gamma EEG connectivity stayed reduced during wakefulness; rats not woken showed reduced slow power and increased complexity. Behavioural wakefulness dissociated from these field measures.
- Pockett & Holmes 2009 (section 0): post-ictal unconsciousness with greater synchrony.
- Nothing found that separates loss of field coherence from loss of synaptic communication; every measure of the former is also a measure of the latter.
