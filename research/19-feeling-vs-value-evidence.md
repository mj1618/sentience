# 19 — Evidence for "feeling without value, or value without feeling" (scenario 19, Part 1b)

Evidence only; no scoring of H / K\*-HO. Tags: **[V-full]** methods and results read in full text ("partial" = relevant sections only; supplements not read); **[V-abs]** abstract only; **[R]** recalled, unchecked. Ids resolve at `europepmc.org/article/MED/<PMID>` or `/PMC/<PMCID>`. Requirements: **1** felt valence at floor by self-report, anticipatory and consummatory; **2** value signal intact on at least two of (a) OFC/vmPFC, (b) autonomic/Pavlovian, (c) incentive-compatible prompted valuation; **3** flexible action by the round 9 criteria (devaluation, contingency change, first choice among new options, effort against reward with option to decline). "Req 3: not measured" means no criterion-meeting task was run.

## Row 5 — Depersonalisation/derealisation disorder (DPD)

**No study of a criterion-meeting choice task in DPD was found.** Europe PMC searches combining depersonalisation with Iowa Gambling Task, decision making, reward, reinforcement, effort, devaluation and anhedonia returned no DPD study. What exists is rating, autonomic and imaging work with passive emotional stimuli, plus clinical descriptions of functioning.

- **Michal et al. 2013, PLoS One** (PMC3772934) **[V-full]**. 22 DPD patients vs 15 patient controls matched for depression and anxiety. Manipulated: 20 emotional/neutral sounds (IADS), normal listening vs mindful breathing. Measured: skin conductance responses (SCR), heart rate; valence and arousal ratings (9-point) in a separate session. Feeling: DPD patients rated unpleasant sounds as less unpleasant than patient controls (d=0.81) and than norms (d=0.83), but both groups clearly separated positive from negative sounds (valence main effect F(1,33)=132.5, η²=.80). SCR amplitudes were **higher** in DPD than in patient controls (group effect F(1,35)=8.44), contrary to the authors' prediction. No choice task. Req 1: not met (attenuated, not at floor; passive stimuli only); Req 2: not shown (one autonomic measure to sounds, no anticipatory or valuation measure); Req 3: not measured.
- **Sierra et al. 2002, Arch Gen Psychiatry** (PMID 12215083) **[V-abs]**. 15 DPD, 15 controls, 11 anxiety patients; SCR to 15 pictures (5 unpleasant, 5 pleasant, 5 neutral) and to a clap and a sigh. SCR to unpleasant pictures reduced in DPD (P=.01) with longer latency (3.01 s vs 2.5 and 2.1 s); latency to clap/sigh shorter than controls. Subjective ratings not reported in the abstract. Req 1: not measured (abstract); Req 2: not shown (the one measure taken was reduced); Req 3: not measured.
- **Medford et al. 2016, Front Psychol** (PMC4890597) **[V-full, partial]**. 14 DPD (illness duration 2 to >40 years, verbal IQ 103–127) and 25 controls viewed aversive vs neutral pictures in fMRI before lamotrigine; 10 rescanned (5 improved, 5 not). SCR fluctuations over the session lower in DPD at trend (U=119.5, one-tailed p=0.052); 4 of 14 patients showed no SCR fluctuation at all. No value or choice task. Req 1: not measured in the task (scale scores only); Req 2: not shown; Req 3: not measured.
- **Kusztor, Mulay & Tsuchiya 2026, registered preprint** **[V-abs]**. Non-clinical online sample N=723, 500 videos, 25 emotion categories: depersonalisation severity related **positively** to emotional intensity ratings; the numbing item of the scale showed no effect in 24 of 25 categories. Req 1: not met.
- **Real-life functioning. Michal et al. 2016, BMC Psychiatry** (PMC4924239) **[V-full, partial]**. 223 consecutive patients at a specialist clinic vs 1,129 depressed patients. DPD: 61.2% university-entrance diploma (vs 40.7%), 33.2% employed (vs 46.4%), 22.1% unemployed (vs 15.1%), 25.9% living with parents; self-rated work/school impairment 7.9/10 (vs 7.3); clinician GAF past week 49.7. Patients self-referred (selection bias noted by authors). No measure of goal pursuit as such; secondary evidence only.

## Row 8 — Instructed devaluation with anticipated affect measured

**No study was found that rated anticipated affect after a purely instructed devaluation and related rating change to choice change.** Instructed tasks use points and take no affect rating; satiation studies report ratings only as a group manipulation check.

- **Gillan et al. 2011, Am J Psychiatry** (PMC3533260) **[V-full, partial]**. 21 OCD patients, 30 controls. Fruit pictures earned points; devaluation by instruction ("one of them is now devalued and earns no points", marked with a cross), then a slips-of-action test. Patients made more slips and had poorer outcome knowledge. No pleasantness, liking or desire rating taken. Req 1: not measured; Req 2: not measured; Req 3: measured (devaluation), intact in controls, impaired in patients.
- **de Wit et al. 2018, JEP:General** (PMC6033090) **[V-full, partial]**. Exp 2: n=43, abstract currencies devalued by an instructed "stock market crash"; responding was sensitive to devaluation whether trained 1 or 3 days (6 participants misunderstood). Exp 1A: avoidance of wrist shock, devalued by visibly disconnecting one stimulator; the only rating was belief that shock would no longer occur (means 86 and 83 of 100), not affect. Req 1: not measured (instructed experiments); Req 3: measured, intact.
- **Tricomi, Balleine & O'Doherty 2009, Eur J Neurosci** (PMC2758609) **[V-full]**. n=32 (31 analysed: 16 trained 1 day, 15 trained 3 days). Free-operant button pressing for chocolate or corn chips; one food eaten "until it was no longer pleasant"; 3-min extinction test. Pleasantness ratings fell selectively for the devalued food in **both** groups (t(15)=−5.5; t(14)=−5.1; both P<.001). Response rate fell for the devalued outcome only in the 1-day group (t(15)=3.6, P<.01); 3-day group unchanged (t(14)=0.2); interaction F(1,29)=7.9. So in the overtrained group ratings changed without a change in responding. Individual-level relation between rating change and response change not reported. Req 1: not met (feeling present and changed); Req 3: measured.
- **Pool et al. 2022, Learn Mem** (PMC8686594) **[V-full, partial]**. Preregistered four-site replication of Tricomi, n=306. Hunger and liking fell after satiation at every site. Training duration had no reliable effect. A mixture model classed 212 participants as outcome-insensitive and 94 as outcome-sensitive, in similar proportions in both training groups — i.e. about two-thirds did not adjust responding despite the group-level fall in liking. No analysis relating individual liking change to behaviour found in the sections read. Req 1: not met; Req 3: measured.
- **Valentin, Dickinson & O'Doherty 2007, J Neurosci** (PMID 17428979) **[V-abs; full text not retrievable]**. n=19; choice between actions for tomato juice, chocolate milk, orange juice; one devalued by feeding to satiety; choice test in extinction; orbitofrontal activity differed for devalued vs valued action. Pleasantness ratings and any rating–choice relation not verified.
- **Hogarth et al. 2015, Psychopharmacology** (PMC4534490) **[V-full, partial]**. 48 smokers, 44 analysed. Key presses earned tobacco or chocolate points; tobacco devalued by smoking to satiety (puff-by-puff pleasantness and desire ratings taken); then negative (n=20) or positive (n=24) mood induction; choice test in extinction. Satiety lowered tobacco choice after positive mood; negative mood abolished this. Change in self-rated mood correlated with change in tobacco choice (r=0.43, p=.004). The high- and low-mood-change negative subgroups did not differ in puff pleasantness or post-satiety desire. Desire was not re-rated after the mood induction. Req 1: not met; Req 3: measured.
- **Morris et al. 2015, Biol Psychiatry** (PMID 25062683) **[V-abs]**. 18 schizophrenia, 18 controls; sensitivity to experienced change in outcome value "did not differ", yet patients failed to use it to guide choice. Rating method not given in abstract.

## Rows 1, 3, 4, 9

**Row 1, asymbolia.** No report with any measure of valuation (requirement 2) or of prompted flexible avoidance was found; Berthier et al. 1988 (n=6; see file 18) remains the main series and has neither. Req 1: partly described clinically; Req 2: not measured; Req 3: not measured.

**Row 3, opioids.** No study of avoidance or escape choice under opioid analgesia at matched sensed intensity was found.

- **Korb et al. 2020, eLife** (PMC7553773) **[V-full, partial]**. N=131 healthy volunteers, between-subject: amisulpride 400 mg (42), naltrexone 50 mg (44), placebo (45). Each trial: wanting rating, hand-grip force (which set the probability of the announced reward), delivery of sweet milk or touch, liking rating; facial EMG. Neither drug changed wanting or liking ratings. Both drugs lowered force for food (group means 75.0 and 73.5 vs 80.2 for placebo; Reward Type × Drug F(2,128.4)=4.71, p=.01), not for touch. Force here scales an instructed response; there was no option to decline. Req 1: not met (ratings unchanged); Req 2: not measured; Req 3: partial (graded effort for reward, no choice).
- **Eikemo et al. 2017, Neuropsychopharmacology** (PMC5520785) **[V-abs]**. 30 men, crossover: morphine 10 mg, naltrexone 50 mg, placebo; two-alternative task with unequal reward probabilities. Morphine increased bias to the high-probability option; morphine raised and naltrexone lowered evidence-accumulation efficiency; "neither drug affected … subjective state". Req 1: not met; Req 3: not a criterion task.
- **Chelnokova et al. 2014, Mol Psychiatry** (PMID 24514570) **[R; no abstract indexed]**: morphine and naltrexone shifted both liking ratings and key-press effort to view attractive faces.

**Row 4, blunting drugs.**

- **Langley et al. 2023, Neuropsychopharmacology** (PMC9938113) **[V-full, main text]**. Double-blind, semi-randomised, healthy volunteers: escitalopram 20 mg (n=32) vs placebo (n=34) for at least 21 days (mean 26). Tested once, on drug. Changed: reinforcement sensitivity lower on escitalopram in probabilistic reversal learning (mean difference −2.77, 90% HDI −5.29 to −0.40) and in the two-step task (Reward × Group −0.34, p<.01). Unchanged: reward and punishment learning rates, stimulus stickiness; model-based control (Reward × Transition × Group p=.29); Cambridge Gambling Task, ultimatum game, moral judgement, emotion recognition, and all "cold" tests. Self-report: the only questionnaire difference was sexual function (orgasm dimension, d=0.68); no group difference on other questionnaires. Emotional blunting was not shown by self-report; the link to blunting is the authors' interpretation. Req 1: not met; Req 2: not measured; Req 3: not a criterion task (two-step model-based index intact).
- **Durso, Luttrell & Way 2015, Psychol Sci** (PMC4515109) **[V-full, partial]**. Two studies (82 and 85 recruited), acetaminophen 1,000 mg vs placebo; ratings of 40 pictures. Evaluation extremity 1.96 vs 2.25 on a 0–5 distance scale (t(151)=3.40, p=.001); extreme pictures 3.01 vs 3.46. No behaviour measured. Req 1: not met (small reduction); Req 3: not measured.
- **Keaveney, Peters & Way 2020, SCAN** (PMC7511878) **[V-full, partial]**. Balloon risk task, imaginary money. Study 1 (n=142): more pumps on acetaminophen (d=0.38, P=.023). Study 2: replicated. Study 3 (n=215): no difference (t(213)=0.20, P=.84). Pooled (n=546) d=0.19, P=.024. Not a criterion task. **DeWall et al. 2010** (PMID 20548058) **[V-abs]**: three weeks of acetaminophen lowered daily hurt-feelings reports; no choice task. No preregistered direct replication of Durso 2015 or DeWall 2010 was located.
- **Alexithymia.** Zhang et al. 2017 (PMC5704455) **[V-full, partial]**: 42 high vs 44 low scorers (students); Iowa Gambling Task net score lower in the high group, Game of Dice Task (explicit probabilities) unimpaired. Kano et al. 2011 (PMID 21862828) **[V-abs]**: 10 vs 13 men, poorer IGT learning. Alexithymia scales measure difficulty identifying feelings, not valence at floor. Req 1: not met; Req 3: IGT is a learning task.

**Row 9, bodily feedback.**

- **Heims et al. 2004, Neuropsychologia** (PMID 15381028) **[V-abs]**. Pure autonomic failure patients (n not in abstract) unimpaired on the IGT, emotion recognition, theory of mind; worse only on an emotional attribution test. Req 1: not shown; Req 3: IGT intact.
- **North & O'Carroll 2001, Neuropsychologia** (PMID 11254934) **[V-abs]**. Complete C6 tetraplegia vs matched controls (n not in abstract): no difference in IGT deck selection or net outcome. Feeling not assessed in this paper; O'Carroll et al. 2003 (PMID 12554827) **[V-abs]**, 20 C6 patients vs 20 controls, no difference in alexithymia; Deady et al. 2010 (PMID 20677079) **[V-abs]**, 24 patients, no reduction in emotional awareness. Req 1: not met.

## Other "feels nothing" conditions

- **Auto-activation deficit. Schmidt et al. 2008, Brain** (PMID 18344560) **[V-abs; full text not retrieved]**. 13 patients with bilateral striato-pallidal lesions vs 13 unmedicated Parkinson's patients. Grip force to instruction: no difference. SCR to monetary incentives: no difference from the Parkinson's group. Grip force for money: patients "failed to distinguish between monetary incentives". The abstract mentions no hedonic or affect ratings; whether any were taken is unverified. The comparison group was patients, not healthy controls. Req 1: not measured; Req 2: one of the required two (autonomic), relative to a patient group; Req 3: lost on graded effort for reward (not a choice).
- **Cotard delusion. Davies & Coltheart 2022** (PMID 36112925) **[V-abs]**: case literature shows such patients often report anxiety, fear, guilt, distress. No choice-task study found. Req 1: not met.
- No study of severe anhedonic depression showing ratings at floor together with an intact criterion task was located.

## Reverse dissociations

- **Value-related signal without attention to value.** Tusche, Bode & Haynes 2010 (PMID 20534850) **[V-abs]**: insula and medial prefrontal patterns predicted later stated willingness to buy cars equally well when attention was diverted. Lebreton et al. 2009 (PMID 19914190) **[V-abs]**: value regions tracked later preference while participants judged picture age. Stimuli were fully visible; neither asked whether anything was felt. Masked-cue value signals (Pessiglione 2007, 2008) and their failed replications are in file 18. Req 1: not measured.
- **Action measure shifts while ratings do not:** Korb 2020. **Ratings shift while action does not:** Tricomi 2009 3-day group; Pool 2022 outcome-insensitive majority.
- **Feeling reported with candidate substrate destroyed. Feinstein et al. 2016, Brain Struct Funct** (PMC4734900) **[V-full, partial]**. One patient with bilateral insula, anterior cingulate and amygdala damage: cold-pressor unpleasantness ratings reached 10/10 on three of four trials, hand withdrawn at mean 87 s (29 controls: 185 s), heart rate and skin conductance rose. Orbitofrontal status and valuation tasks not checked here.
- No case of reported feeling with a demonstrated absent value signal on a valuation task was found.
