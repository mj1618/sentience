## 2. How solid is the "later report" column?

- **REM / NREM.** NREM mentation recall averages ~50% across studies (Nielsen 2000 [V-abs],
  https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11515145). REM ~80% is [R]. Siclari [14] cites "up to 70%"
  for NREM and a "small but consistent minority" of REM awakenings with no experience.
  Ruby 2020 [39]: "no report" cannot be distinguished from a forgotten dream.
- **Propofol / gas — "usually none" is contested.** Leslie 2007 [V-abs]: dreaming reported by
  22% of 300 surgical patients on emergence (mixed agents); authors suggest dreams occur during
  recovery. Leslie 2009 [V-abs]: 27% propofol, 28% desflurane (n=300). Radek 2018 [V-abs]:
  healthy volunteers roused during propofol infusion; 74% of propofol interviews contained
  experiences from the unresponsive period (propofol n=24; lighter than surgical depth; only
  10/24 arousable). Sarasso 2015 [15]: no experience reported after propofol or xenon (n=6 each).
  Sanders 2017 [V-abs]: isolated-forearm response after intubation in 4.6% (12/260); none had
  explicit recall afterwards (n=253).
- **Ketamine.** Sarasso [15]: participants reported "long, vivid dreams" (n=6). Population
  incidence not verified. Lee 2013 [22] collected no experience reports.
- **Absence seizure.** No study of post-ictal experience report was found. Guo 2016 [28]:
  task performance is spared in some seizures. "None" rests on behaviour, not report.
- **CMD.** Owen 2006 (n=1) and Monti 2010 (5 of 54; 4 of 23 VS) are [R] (Europe PMC returned
  only a comment for Monti). Claassen 2019 [V-abs]: EEG command-following in 16/104 (15%) acute
  unresponsive patients. Bodien 2024 [V-abs]: 60/241 (25%). Experience is inferred from
  command-following; later report exists for few patients.
- **Sleepwalking — "usually none" is contested.** Cataldi 2024 [27, V-full]: of 57 interviewed
  episodes, 56% conscious experience, 25% experience without recalled content, 19% none.
  Oudiette 2009 [V-abs]: 71% of 38 patients recalled ≥1 dreamlike mentation over a lifetime.

## 3. Notes per marker

### A. Metabolism
- [1] Maquet 1990, Brain Res, FDG-PET [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/2350676 — only
  4 SWS and 4 REM scans met steady-state. SWS −43.8 ± 14.1% vs wake; REM +4.3 ± 7.4% (ns).
- [2] Buchsbaum 1989 [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/2796606 — n=36; NREM (stage
  2–3) −23% whole brain; REM "tended" higher than waking.
- [3] Långsjö 2004 [V-abs] MED/15114201 — n=9, **sub-anaesthetic** ketamine: rGMR up in most
  regions (thalamus +14.6%, frontal +13.6%, parietal +13.1%); subjects responsive.
- [4] Långsjö 2005 [V-abs] MED/16052107 — n=8, **anaesthetic** S-ketamine: blood flow +36.4%
  but "no effect on whole brain CMRO2 or GMR". The "ketamine raises metabolism" claim holds for
  flow and for sub-anaesthetic dose, not for whole-brain metabolism at anaesthetic dose.
- [5] Alkire 1995 [V-abs] MED/7856898 — n=6; whole-brain GMR 29 → 13 µmol/100 g/min (≈ −55%);
  cortex −58%, subcortical −48%.
- [6] Engel 1985 [V-abs] MED/3919636 — n=4; 2.5–3.5× global increase during frequent absences
  vs hyperventilation control. FDG integrates over ~30+ min, mixing ictal and interictal time.
- [7] Theodore 1985 [V-abs] MED/3921871 — increase in 2 patients with spike-wave; **decrease**
  in one in absence status. fMRI [30]: small increases then prolonged decreases.
- [8] Stender 2015 [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25294128 — VS n=14: cortical
  metabolism 42% of normal; MCS n=21: 55%.
- [9] Stender 2016, Curr Biol [V-abs] MED/27238279 — n=131; "42% of normal cortical activity"
  as minimal requirement; metabolic rate matched current or imminent awareness in 94%.
  Stender 2014 Lancet [V-abs, abstract truncated in retrieval]: 13 of 41 behaviourally
  unresponsive patients had imaging compatible with minimal consciousness [completion R].
  None of these reports metabolism in fMRI command-followers specifically.

### B. Gamma
- [10] Llinás & Ribary 1993 [V-abs] MED/8446632 — MEG, n=5; coherent 40 Hz in wake and REM,
  "very reduced" in delta sleep.
- [11] Cantero 2004 [V-abs] MED/15219599 — intracranial; local and long-range gamma coherence
  higher in wake than in either slow-wave **or REM** sleep. Conflicts with [10].
- [12] Murphy 2011, Sleep [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21358845 — n=8, 256-ch;
  propofol increased gamma (25–40 Hz) power, persisting through loss of consciousness, with
  increased gamma connectivity between anterior and posterior cingulate. Scalp gamma is
  vulnerable to muscle artefact.
- [13] Imas 2005 (two papers, rats) [V-abs] MED/15851880, MED/16019145 — volatile agents
  preserved/enhanced flash-induced gamma in V1 (n=15) but reduced frontal-posterior transfer
  entropy at gamma (n=6; abstract truncated on direction detail).
- Ketamine: gamma (25–35 Hz) relative power rose (F=68.86), EMG correlation checked [22,
  V-full]; also [24]. Within NREM, 20–50 Hz power was higher before dream reports [14].
- Absence seizures: only a rat model and scalp HFO case series surfaced; no usable human data.

### C. Field strength / coherence
No source in this round measured "field strength" as such; cells use EEG amplitude/power.
- Waking and REM low-voltage; N3 slow waves ≥75 µV; 3 Hz spike-wave typically larger still
  and bilaterally synchronous — all [R] (textbook); synchrony also stated in [6].
- [14]: posterior 1–4 Hz power in prediction trials 10.17 ± 2.5 (dream) vs 103.47 ± 38.16
  (no experience) [V-full].
- [28] Guo 2016 [V-abs] MED/27839650: frontal EEG power 50.4 vs 24.8 for seizures with
  impaired vs spared behaviour.
- [27]: parasomnia episodes with experience were preceded by high-amplitude anterior slow
  waves (abstract) alongside lower posterior delta.

### D. P3b / frontal ignition
- [18] Pitts 2014 [V-abs] MED/25339922 — perceived but task-irrelevant stimuli gave a
  mid-latency posterior negativity (~200–240 ms) and no P3b; P3b appeared with task relevance.
- [19] Cohen 2020 [V-abs] MED/32409620 — visible stimuli: "no P3b whatsoever" without report.
- [20] Sergent 2021 [V-abs] MED/33608533 — task-free listening still shows late (≥250–300 ms)
  bifurcating activity predicting sampled reports, "except for frontal executive components".
  Counter-evidence to a clean "late activity is only report".
- [21] Strauss 2015 [V-abs] MED/25737555 — global-regularity P300 "vanished during sleep"
  (N1, N2, REM). [21b] Cote & Campbell 1999 [V-abs] MED/10454269 — n=8, parieto-central P300
  at 321 ms in REM to loud rare tones. The two differ in paradigm.
- [25] Faugeras 2011 [V-abs] MED/21593438 — 2 of 22 VS patients showed the global effect; both
  showed clinical consciousness within 3–4 days. Bekinschtein 2009: effect can be absent in
  conscious subjects unaware of the regularity.
- Not found: P3b under ketamine or propofol unresponsiveness (Reinsel 1995 is conscious
  sedation), in absence seizures, sleepwalking, or dreamers vs non-dreamers.

### E. Posterior hot zone
- [14] Siclari et al. 2017, Nat Neurosci [V-full, author manuscript PMC5462120]. 256-channel
  EEG, serial awakenings, source-level power in last 20 s. Exp 1: n=32, 233 awakenings
  (subjects selected for having both report types in NREM). Exp 2: n=7 trained, 815 awakenings.
  REM contrast: 10 subjects. Dream report (and report without recalled content, n=20) vs no
  experience: lower 1–4 Hz power in parieto-occipital cortex, in NREM and REM. Exp 3 (n=7,
  NREM only, 84 awakenings): algorithm-initiated awakenings 33/36 correct for "dream" (91.6%),
  21/26 for "no experience" (80.7%), 87% overall. The Fig. 5 caption gives different trial
  counts (55 and 27); unresolved. High-frequency differences extended into frontal cortex.
- [40] Wong et al. 2020, Neurosci Conscious [V-full, PMC7362719] — 9 participants, 54 one-min
  NREM recordings (27 dream, 27 none), 25-channel EEG, blinded unsupervised classifier on
  spectral power: best 59% (p=0.44). Not a like-for-like replication (fewer channels, no
  source modelling, unsupervised, small n).
- Wong et al. 2025, Nat Commun, DREAM database [V-abs] MED/40804039 — 505 participants, 2643
  awakenings; reports "can be predicted" from EEG features in REM and NREM; abstract does not
  say whether the posterior-delta finding specifically replicates.
- [39] Ruby 2020 commentary [V-full, PMC7662438] — forgotten dreams counted as no experience;
  timing of the dream relative to the analysed EEG unknown; prior serial-awakening studies
  uncited.
- [27] Cataldi 2024, Nat Commun [V-full, PMC11082195] — 22 patients, 75 rated episodes, 57
  interviewed; experience vs none: lower delta and higher beta (26–34 Hz) in posterior cortex
  in the 20 s before movement. Same lab and method as [14]. Episodes were mostly confusional
  arousals; **no patient left the bed**. 11 no-experience episodes.
- Indirect only: [29] propofol lowers flow in precuneus/cuneus/posterior cingulate; [23]
  Vlisides 2018 [V-abs] sub-anaesthetic ketamine lowers precuneus/TPJ alpha.

### F. PCI
- Casali 2013 [V-abs] MED/23946194 — introduced PCI; wake, sleep, midazolam/xenon/propofol,
  patients.
- [15] Sarasso 2015, Curr Biol [V-abs] MED/26752078 — 18 volunteers; low complexity under
  propofol and xenon with no experience reported; wake-like complexity under ketamine with
  vivid dream reports.
- [16] Casarotto 2016, Ann Neurol [V-full, PMC5132045] — benchmark n=150. PCImax ranges:
  NREM 0.15–0.31 (n=18, chosen as giving no report on awakening); midazolam 0.23–0.31 (6);
  xenon 0.11–0.31 (6); propofol 0.23–0.31 (6); REM with dream report 0.36–0.56 (8); ketamine
  0.36–0.52 (6); wake 0.39–0.70 (102). Cutoff 0.31 gave 100% sensitivity/specificity **in the
  sample it was derived from**; 13 of 461 single waking measurements fell in the unconscious
  range (hence use of the per-subject maximum). VS: 13/43 no response, 21/43 low, 9/43 high.
- Critiques/limits: same research group throughout; small n per unresponsive condition;
  "no report" conditions were selected on report; the high-PCI VS patients were not shown to
  be the command-followers; no PCI in N+, absence, or sleepwalking.

### G. Brainstem / thalamus
- [31] Maquet 1996, Nature [V-abs] MED/8774879 — n=7 (dreams recalled): REM flow positively
  related to pontine tegmentum, left thalamus, amygdala, anterior cingulate.
- [32] Braun 1997 [V-abs] MED/9236630 — n=37: brainstem, thalamus, basal forebrain profoundly
  deactivated in slow-wave sleep, reactivated in REM.
- [33] Alkire 2000 [V-abs] MED/10993665 — n=11, two volatile agents: thalamus and midbrain
  reticular formation suppressed. [29] Fiset 1999 [V-abs] MED/10377359 — n=5, propofol: medial
  thalamus flow down, covarying with midbrain.
- [30] Bai 2010 [V-abs] MED/20427649 — 9 children, 88 absence seizures: thalamic fMRI
  increase after onset, cortical decreases lasting >20 s after.
- [26] Bassetti 2000, Lancet [V-abs] MED/10981896 — **one patient**; "activation of
  thalamocingulate pathways and persisting deactivation of other thalamocortical arousal
  systems". Regional detail [R].
- VS: [8] thalamus and brainstem metabolism reduced equally in VS and MCS; Laureys 2000
  [V-abs] thalamo-prefrontal connectivity altered in VS. "Brainstem arousal preserved in VS"
  (sleep–wake cycles) is [R].

### H. Front–back communication
- [22] Lee 2013, Anesthesiology [V-full, PMC4346246] — ketamine 2 mg/kg, n=30 surgical
  patients; frontal→parietal symbolic transfer entropy 0.0074 ± 0.003 → 0.0055 ± 0.0027;
  feedforward preserved. Propofol (n=9), sevoflurane (n=9) reanalysed: same pattern.
  **No dream/experience reports collected.** Authors note two Granger-causality studies that
  found no decrease under propofol; short-timescale connectivity rebounded under ketamine.
- [36] Bonhomme 2016 [V-abs] MED/27496657 — ketamine to unresponsiveness; 8 of 14 analysable;
  medial-prefrontal default-network connectivity fell (0.20 → 0.07).
- [24] Vlisides 2017 [V-abs] MED/28486269 — n=10; anterior–posterior theta phase-locking
  *increased* under anaesthetic ketamine (abstract truncated before directed-alpha result).
- [37] Boveroux 2010 [V-abs] MED/20885292 — n=19, propofol: frontoparietal cortico-cortical
  and thalamo-cortical connectivity fell; sensory networks preserved.
- REM: [31] dorsolateral prefrontal, parietal, posterior cingulate/precuneus flow negatively
  related to REM; [34] Massimini 2010 TMS spread wake-like. [35] Massimini 2005: NREM TMS
  response stays local.
- [38] Boly 2011, Science [V-abs] MED/21566197 — VS: only frontal→temporal backward
  connectivity impaired; King et al. 2011 comment disputes data quality and statistics.
- [14]: the dream/no-dream contrast did not require frontoparietal differences in low-frequency
  power.

## 4. Not verified this round
Sarasso 2015, Casali 2013, Stender 2016, Claassen 2019, Bodien 2024, all PET papers:
abstract only. Owen 2006, Monti 2010, REM recall ~80%, raw EEG amplitudes, Bassetti regional
detail: recalled. Ketamine dream incidence in general practice; any experience-report study of
absence seizures; P3b under anaesthesia: not found.
