# 04 — Felt valence as the interface to the planner: literature check

Hypothesis under test: felt valence is the interface by which bodily needs reach the flexible, goal-directed system; Pavlovian/habit systems run on valence that need not be felt.

Tags: **[V]** primary text or abstract fetched and read; **[S]** search snippet only; **[R]** recalled, unchecked. Compiled 2026-10-03. Several publisher/PMC pages were bot-blocked; where so, PubMed abstracts were used and effect sizes are absent.

## 1. Incentive learning

- **Balleine 1992** (JEP:ABP 18:236–250), 5 experiments. Rats trained on low deprivation, shifted to high, tested in extinction: the shift "increased performance only if the animals had been exposed to the reinforcer in the high-deprivation state". Reverse shift (high→low): performance "only sensitive to this shift if animals were previously exposed to the reinforcer while in the low-deprivation state". Holds for pellets and maltodextrin. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1619392/ — Abstract gives no effect sizes; none verified.
- Same dependence reported for an upshift in thirst (Lopez, Balleine & Dickinson 1992). [S] https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03197955
- Goal-directed control needs two learning processes (action–outcome contingency; incentive value); incentive learning for food "involves the insular cortex", contingency learning the prelimbic cortex. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9704982/
- **Exceptions / boundary conditions**
  - *Chains* (Balleine, Garner, Gonzalez & Dickinson 1995): responses proximal to reward and consummatory responses are "immediately sensitive" to deprivation shifts (Pavlovian incentive process); distal procurement responses change only after incentive learning. [S] https://produccioncientifica.ugr.es/documentos/65187ccedac9c450d398a05c
  - *Extended training* (Dickinson et al. 1995, Anim Learn Behav 23:197–206): after overtraining, extinction performance was unaffected by re-exposure in the non-deprived state. [S; abstract not retrieved] https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03199935
  - *Sodium appetite*: Robinson & Berridge cite reports that depletion directly increases pressing on a lever distinctively paired with NaCl, but note it "combines Pavlovian and instrumental associations". [V, as their citation] (URL in §3)
  - *Sensory-specific satiety* devaluation (Balleine & Dickinson 1998): title verified only; the satiety treatment is itself consummatory contact, so it does not test "no re-experience". [S] https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03199161
  - Ondansetron (anti-emetic) blocking incentive learning after taste aversion. [R]

## 2. Hedonic Interface Theory (Dickinson & Balleine, "Hedonics: the cognitive–motivational interface", in *Pleasures of the Brain*, 2009/2010)

Chapter could not be fetched (403); claims below are from secondary sources and earlier D&B quotes.

- Core: "primary motivational states, such as hunger, do not determine the value of an instrumental goal directly; rather, animals have to learn about the value of a commodity in a particular motivational state through direct experience with it in that state" (D&B 1994); "assignment of incentive value is based on learning about one's own hedonic or affective reactions to the goal" (D&B 1995). [V, quoted in] https://philosophical-issues-in-behavioural-science.butterfill.com/docs/lecture_08/interface_problem_experience/
- HIT proper: two "psychologies" (S–R/reflex machine; intentional belief–desire). Conscious hedonic experience is what grounds cognitive goal values in the reflex machine's biological responses; once assigned, values can drive action without concurrent affect. [S] https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281198773_Hedonics_The_cognitive-motivational_interface
- Strongest reading (Butlin 2023, as quoted by Campero): "conscious experiences of pleasure and displeasure are necessary for information concerning a range of bodily states to influence goal-directed action selection"; goal-directed control "distinguishes conscious creatures from mere 'beast machines'"; outside rats, "little conclusive evidence for other animal branches". [V] https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16696
- **Critiques.** No dedicated published refutation located. Problems assembled here: (a) the data show *contact with the outcome in the new state* is required, not that the contact was *felt* — consciousness is an added interpretation; (b) Berridge-school evidence for unconscious "liking" (§4, §5); (c) instructed devaluation in humans (§8) shows a second, non-hedonic route into the planner; (d) loose-coupling efficiency objection (why make cognition wait for experience?), answered by D&B-style "learned preferences can override misleading drives". [V] (Butterfill URL above)

## 3. Robinson & Berridge 2013, "Instant transformation of learned repulsion into motivational 'wanting'" (Curr Biol 23:282–289)

All [V] from full text: https://robinsonlab.research.wesleyan.edu/files/2014/01/Robinson-2013-Current-Biology-2.pdf ; abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23375893/

- Design: Pavlovian only. Lever+sound CS_Salt predicted intra-oral infusion of 9% (1.5 M) NaCl (triple seawater; elicited gapes); a second lever predicted 17% sucrose; third lever predicted nothing. ≥50 pairings; rats learned to turn away from CS_Salt. Then DOCA + furosemide induced a never-before-experienced sodium appetite; test next day with CS **in extinction** (no NaCl yet tasted in the new state). Group size not stated in pages read; df imply about 12.
- Results: first CS_Salt presentation instantly approached, sniffed, nibbled (F[1,8]=29.35, p=0.001); lever depressed ">1,000% more than on any previous day" (z=−2.52, p=0.012). CS_Sucrose and control lever unchanged (F[1,10]=0.52, p=0.49). Reverted to repulsion after repletion (z=−2.55, p=0.011). Home-cage 3% NaCl intake up 775% (20.67 vs 2.67 ml).
- Crucially, CS_Salt also evoked positive orofacial "liking" reactions in >80% of rats *before* any retasting (t[11]=3.21, p=0.008). On later retasting, hedonic reactions to 1.5 M NaCl rose 40-fold; disgust reactions more than halved.
- Fos (CS + depletion vs control): NAc rostral medial shell ~1700%, ventral pallidum >600%, VTA >450%, infralimbic >550%.
- **Reconciliation with incentive learning** (authors' own): they contrast their result with reports that rats "failed to cognitively infer a higher value for salt or to instrumentally pursue actions that would obtain NaCl" in a novel appetite — "failures that correspond to model-based reinforcement computations when models lack any experience-gained knowledge about the new salt value", switched only by retasting. Pavlovian incentive salience is recomputed online from CS–sensory-identity memory × current state (Zhang et al. 2009 κ gain factor); instrumental act–outcome values are cached from experience. So: Pavlovian = instant, instrumental = needs re-experience.

## 4. Unreported affect in humans

- **Winkielman, Berridge & Wilbarger 2005** (PSPB 31:121–135). [V] full text https://pages.ucsd.edu/~pwinkiel/winkielman-berridge-wilbarger_PSPB_2005.pdf
  - Study 1, N=39; 8 primes of 16 ms, masked 400 ms; forced-choice prime detection 51.6% (chance). Thirst × prime interaction on pouring (B=6.52, t(34)=3.02, p<.01) and drinking (B=6.02, t(34)=2.59, p<.01). At +1 SD thirst: poured 37/58/79 ml and drank 12/29/46 ml after angry/neutral/happy. No effect at low thirst. Mood and arousal: no prime effect (thirsty trend prime-incongruent, p=.35); mood–pouring r=.03.
  - Study 2, N=29: willingness to pay 38 vs 10 cents (happy vs angry) at high thirst, t(13)=2.25, p=.05; only when rated immediately; omnibus prime × thirst only marginal (F(2,28)=3.43, p=.07). 20-item PANAS: no effect. Authors concede "negative results are never conclusive".
  - **Replication:** no direct replication, successful or failed, found. Samples are small; the wider subliminal-priming literature has replicated poorly. [S] https://replicationindex.com/category/subliminal-priming/ Treat as unreplicated.
- **Berridge & Winkielman 2003** (Cogn Emot 17:181–211): unconscious "liking" mediated by subcortical systems (NAc); conscious pleasure arises when awareness systems interact with core processes. [V, author abstract page] https://pages.ucsd.edu/~pwinkiel/abs_unconscious-emotion-CE-2003.htm
- **Affective blindsight.** Tamietto et al. 2009 (PNAS): two cortically blind patients; unseen facial/bodily expressions evoked faster facial EMG and greater pupil dilation than seen ones. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19805044/ Small-N (a handful of patients, repeatedly tested); Pessoa & Adolphs 2010 dispute the colliculo-pulvinar-amygdala account. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20959860/ Shows unseen *stimulus*, not unfelt *affect*. de Gelder 1999 original: [R].
- **Lamb et al. 1991** (JPET 259:1165–73). n=5 post-addicts; FR30(FR100:S) — 3,000 presses per i.m. injection; each dose for a week. Placebo did not maintain responding; 3.75 mg morphine did in 4/5; yet subjects "did not report subjective effects different from placebo" at that dose. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1762068/ No replication located.
- Fischman & Foltin 1992: cocaine self-administration and self-report "generally" correlate but "clearly diverge" in places. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1638911/ The specific low-dose (4 mg) cocaine claim: [R], not found.

## 5. Brainstem "liking"

- Grill & Norgren 1978: chronic decerebrate rats show the same ingestive and rejection orofacial sequences as intact rats; discriminative taste responses are "complete within, or caudal to, the midbrain". Authors frame it as ingestive motor integration; no claim about feeling. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/630410/
- Steiner 1973 (Symp Oral Sens Percept 4:254–78): citation verified [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4612820/ ; content (anencephalic neonates show sweet-relaxation and bitter-gape; Steiner called it a brainstem "gustofacial reflex") only [S] https://www.jped.com.br/en-a-method-for-assessment-facial-articulo-S0021755716302777
- Berridge uses scare-quoted 'liking' for an objective core process that may be unconscious [S] https://pages.ucsd.edu/~pwinkiel/abs_unconscious-emotion-CE-2003.htm ; Merker 2007 argues the opposite, that the upper brainstem supports consciousness [S] https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/C9B1B393176EF250D4AFBB3054A04E31 . The data cannot adjudicate.

## 6. Devaluation sensitivity across taxa

|---|---|---|

Net: unambiguous *instrumental* devaluation is verified only for rats and humans; insect cases are Pavlovian or ambiguous.

## 7. Pain asymbolia, cingulotomy, morphine

- Berthier, Starkstein & Leiguarda 1988: 6 patients, insula damaged in all. Intact pain recognition but "lack of withdrawal and absent or inadequate emotional responses" to pain over the whole body and to threatening gestures; 5/6 no reaction to verbal menace; "seemed unable to learn appropriate escape or protective responses". **Both** automatic defence and learned avoidance are lost. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3415199/
- Schilder & Stengel: patients smile, offer limbs for more stimulation. [S] https://philosophersmag.com/pain/
- Cingulotomy: pain still present but "not particularly bothersome"; withdrawal preserved. Low-dose morphine in rats: reduced aversiveness with unchanged paw-withdrawal threshold. Both [S] https://academic.oup.com/book/55095/chapter/423296089

Asymbolia therefore does *not* show the clean "reflexes spared, planning lost" split the hypothesis predicts; cingulotomy/morphine fit better but are snippet-level.

## 8. Instructed devaluation in humans

- Standard method: participants are simply told an outcome now loses points; test in (nominal) extinction. Healthy adults withhold responses for devalued outcomes immediately, with no experience of the devalued outcome. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6033090/
- Gillan et al. 2011: 21 OCD vs 30 controls; patients learned from feedback normally but had impaired outcome knowledge and more slips of action. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21572165/
- de Wit et al. 2018: five failures to induce habits by overtraining (incl. instructed slips-of-action; Exp 1 N=74); they suggest instructed devaluation is especially dependent on goal-directed processing. Tricomi 2009 overtraining effect (N=31) did not replicate. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29975092/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19490086/
- Vázquez-Millán et al. 2026 (preregistered): most participants devaluation-sensitive; "habit-like" responding came mainly from those in whom devaluation failed. [V] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42414812/
- Caveat (inference): outcomes are points/coins whose value is already propositional. No study found testing whether *instruction about a bodily state* ("you are now sated") substitutes for experienced satiety.

## Bearing on the interface hypothesis

**For.** (i) Balleine 1992: the planner does not read drive state directly; it needs consummatory contact in the new state — bidirectional, two reinforcers, extended to thirst. (ii) The Pavlovian/instrumental split is exactly as predicted: Robinson & Berridge's CS flips instantly; consummatory and reward-proximal responses shift immediately; distal instrumental actions do not. (iii) Incentive learning localises to insula, which is also the invariant lesion in pain asymbolia. (iv) Cingulotomy/morphine: affect removed, reflex spared [S].

**Against.** (i) "Re-experience" is operationalised as contact; nothing shows it must be *felt*. Decerebrate and anencephalic hedonic reactions, and conditioned 'liking' to a CS before any retasting, show hedonic reactions are produced by machinery nobody has shown to be conscious. (ii) Humans update goal-directed action from instruction alone — felt valence is not the only door into the planner. (iii) Winkielman 2005 and Lamb 1991: valence steers voluntary consumption and effortful drug-seeking without reported feeling. (iv) Asymbolia abolishes withdrawal *and* avoidance learning together. (v) Comparative: instrumental devaluation is verified only in rats and humans, so "goal-directed ⇒ sentient" has almost no phylogenetic test.

**Single most damaging counter-finding.** Lamb et al. 1991: 3,000 lever presses per injection, sustained for a week at 3.75 mg morphine in 4/5 subjects, placebo not sustained, with no reportable subjective effect — an outcome-contingent instrumental action tracking drug value without felt valence. Limits: n=5, unreplicated, report-based, and never devaluation-tested, so it may be habit/reinforcement rather than goal-directed control. A devaluation test at a sub-subjective dose would be decisive either way. Runner-up: instructed devaluation, robust and replicated, but it concerns symbolic rather than bodily value.
