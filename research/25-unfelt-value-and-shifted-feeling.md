# Research brief 25 — Unfelt value, behaviour without feeling change, feeling shifted with value constant

For scenario 31 (round 16), amended version. Covers **prediction 1** (masked/unseen cues and
simple control), **evidence type (ii)** (behaviour changes, reported feeling does not) and
**evidence type (iii)** (feeling shifted while value information is held constant).
Gathered 2026-10-04. No scoring or adjudication here; this is the evidence only.

**Access codes** used on every source:
- **FT** = full text read (methods and results).
- **AB** = abstract only (PubMed / Europe PMC / repository record).
- **SN** = search-engine snippet or secondary summary only. Treat as unverified detail.
- **MEM** = a detail commonly attributed to the source that I could not confirm from any
  page I could open. Flagged, not to be relied on.

**Two senses of "unfelt"** (from the scenario file): (a) fails an awareness test for the
cue/outcome; (b) reports no difference in feeling. Each entry says which was measured.

| Source | Access | Content |
|---|---|---|

### 1.8 Does unseen value drive PLANNING (devaluation, model-based two-step, inference)?

- Höhmann et al. 2025 (above; AB; n = 75, preregistered): masked stimuli in an outcome-revaluation
  test produced neither habitual nor goal-directed responding (chance accuracy).
- Jurchiş et al. 2026 (above; AB; n = 187/160, registered): unconscious structural knowledge
  supported adaptive responding only via a conscious value judgment.
- **No study located** using a two-step (model-based) task with masked cues or outcomes, and none
  testing sensory preconditioning/inference with cues passing an adequate awareness test.
  Searches returned nothing on point. Flag: gap, not a null.
- Per the amended scenario, failures here are predicted by N as well (no access) and "score for
  nobody"; listed for completeness.

## Part 2 — Evidence type (ii): behaviour changes, reported feeling does not

### 2.1 Winkielman, Berridge & Wilbarger 2005, *PSPB* 31:121 — **full text read**
https://pages.ucsd.edu/~pwinkiel/winkielman-berridge-wilbarger_PSPB_2005.pdf

- **Study 1**: n = 39 undergraduates. Eight 16-ms happy/neutral/angry faces, each backward-masked
  by a 400-ms neutral face, inside a gender-classification task. Then (counterbalanced) pour and
  drink a Kool-Aid drink, and rate feelings.
- **Feeling measure (sense b)**: two single items rated "right now, at this very moment" — mood
  (−5 unpleasant to +5 pleasant) and arousal (−5 to +5), taken immediately after the primes for
  half of participants. Graded, immediate, but only two items.
- **Cue awareness (sense a)**: 16-trial two-alternative forced-choice recognition; 51.6%,
  t(38) = 1, p = .30. Group-level test; no post-hoc selection of unaware participants;
  forced-choice score unrelated to priming effects (ts < 1).
- **Behaviour**: no main effect of prime; a **prime × thirst interaction**. Pouring B = 6.52,
  t(34) = 3.02, p < .01; at +1 SD thirst, poured 37 ml (angry) / 58 (neutral) / 79 (happy),
  B = 21.31, t(34) = 3.53. Drinking interaction B = 6.02, t(34) = 2.59; at +1 SD thirst, drank
  12 / 29 / 46 ml. At mean thirst: pouring marginal (p < .08), drinking n.s. At −1 SD thirst: n.s.
- **Feelings**: "No main effects or interaction of priming and thirst occurred". Among thirsty
  participants mood trended in the prime-*incongruent* direction (2.34 angry, 2.02 neutral, 1.70
  happy; p = .35). Mood did not correlate with pouring (r = .03) or drinking (r = .05).
- Only the first priming block showed effects; the second (reversed valence) block showed none.
- **Study 2**: n = 29. Fixed sip; ratings. PANAS (20 items, 1–5) as the feeling measure;
  forced-choice 54% (n.s.). Willingness to pay: prime × order F(1,28) = 4.41, p < .05 — 37 vs
  19 cents (happy vs angry) only when the drink was rated *before* the mood questionnaire;
  33 vs 34 cents when mood was rated first. Prime × thirst on ratings marginal (p = .07); authors
  note "statistical power of Study 2 was low". "Wanting more" at high thirst: B = 1.02,
  t(28) = 2.16. No effect on liking or sweetness ratings. PANAS global index 1.18 (happy) vs 1.14
  (angry), SE .08; no priming effect on any composite; one marginal single item at low thirst.
- Authors' own caveat: "negative results are never conclusive, so it remains possible that a mood
  change occurred but was not detected." No power analysis for the null on feeling.
- **Replication status**: I found **no independent direct replication** (successful or failed) of
  the drinking/pouring study. The earlier Winkielman, Zajonc & Schwarz 1997 result is cited in
  the paper as precedent. Schimmack's blog (above) reports unpublished failures for the broader
  subliminal-affective-priming paradigm but not this study. Flag: unreplicated, small n, key
  effects are moderator interactions.

### 2.5 Items asked for but not established

- **Affective blindsight** (Tamietto et al. 2009 *PNAS*, PMC2764895; SN): unseen emotional faces
  and bodies in two cortically blind patients produced faster facial EMG reactions and pupil
  responses than seen ones. Feeling reports not verified from the snippet. Contrast Anders 2004
  (§1.3), where patients *did* report a changed feeling to an unseen CS.
- **Hunger/thirst states influencing choice without reported feeling**: nothing located that
  measured the absence of the felt state with a graded report. In Winkielman 2005 thirst was
  itself self-rated (0–11) and was consciously reported. Flag: gap.
- **Implicit affect measures** (e.g. IPANAT, AMP): not gathered in depth. The replicationindex
  post criticises the evidential base of the Affect Misattribution Paradigm; no numbers verified.

Plain statement: the classic weather/mood → life-satisfaction effect at its original size **has not

outcome here is a *judgment*, not a choice between options with known values.

### 3.7 Hypnotic modulation of pain unpleasantness

Rainville, Duncan, Price, Carrier & Bushnell 1997, *Science* 277:968 (AB;
https://doi.org/10.1126/science.277.5328.968). n = 8 highly hypnotisable volunteers (SN for n).
Suggestions selectively raised/lowered rated **unpleasantness** of constant noxious heat "without
changing the perceived intensity"; anterior cingulate activity tracked unpleasantness, S1 did not.
No behavioural (withdrawal, avoidance, choice) measure in the abstract. Effect on subsequent
behaviour: **not located**.

### 3.8 Pharmacological mood manipulation and decisions

Only the Crockett et al. 2008 tryptophan-depletion result (§2.4) was gathered, and it runs the
other way (behaviour shifts, mood report does not). No study located in which a drug shifted
reported mood with value information held constant and a decision task as outcome, at
meta-analytic or preregistered level. Flag: gap.

## Measurement notes for the adjudicator (descriptive only)

- Almost every Part 1 study measures sense (a), cue awareness. Exceptions that measured a
  feeling or a conscious value judgment: Anders 2004 (feeling reported to unseen CS),
  Jurchiş 2026 (conscious judgment of predictive value), Winkielman 2005 (mood null).
- Positive Part 1 results resting on **post-hoc selection of unaware participants**:
  Moran 2021 (original criterion), the Hogarth PIT aware/unaware splits, and most studies in the
  Mertens & Engelhard corpus. Pessiglione 2008 used a group-level chance test and discarded
  two participants who could describe the cues. Raio 2012 used trial-by-trial report.
- Feeling measures in Part 2: Winkielman Study 1 used two single-item scales, Study 2 the
  20-item PANAS; Lamb 1991 used liking scales, ARCI-MBG and drug identification with n = 5.
  None reported a power analysis for the null on feeling.
- In Part 3, large preregistered or very-large-N work exists only for mood → life-satisfaction
  judgment (null/small) and trait-emotion → risk (mixed). The induced-affect meta-analyses
  (Ferrer 2020; Bartholomeyczik 2022) are not preregistered-replication based.
