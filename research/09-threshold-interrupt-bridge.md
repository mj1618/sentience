## A — "Report threshold only"

*One valuation system; "felt" means the signal exceeded a metacognitive criterion. Unfelt valence is weak felt valence.*

### What the classic "unfelt valence" studies actually measured

- **Winkielman, Berridge & Wilbarger 2005.** Study 1 n = 39, Study 2 n = 29. Prime awareness was a 16-trial forced-choice test: 51.6% correct, t(38) = 1, p = .30 (Study 1); 54%, n.s. (Study 2). "No feeling changes" is a null on mood ratings. [V, full text] https://pages.ucsd.edu/~pwinkiel/winkielman-berridge-wilbarger_PSPB_2005.pdf
  - Two nulls from short tests in small samples. 54% on 16 trials is not evidence of d' = 0, and the feeling measure was never given a bias-free test.
- **Lamb et al. 1991.** Five post-addicts. 3.75 mg morphine maintained lever-pressing in 4 of 5; placebo did not. Subjective reports at that dose "not different from placebo"; intermediate doses gave "inconsistent effects"; only 30 mg reliably gave liking. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/1762068
  - The graded, inconsistent middle doses are what a criterion account predicts. But n = 5, and the null is on yes/no and scale reports, which are criterion-dependent.
- **Affective blindsight (Pegna et al. 2005).** One patient with bilateral V1 destruction guessed facial emotion; right amygdala active. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15592466
  - Single case, and directly exposed to Phillips's critique below.

### Evidence that supports A (dissociations dissolve under better measurement)

- **Peters & Lau 2015.** Bias-free 2-interval confidence procedure; "no evidence for unconscious perception"; behaviour matched a Bayesian ideal observer; subjective and objective thresholds "effectively the same". Perception, not affect. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26433023
- **Phillips 2021.** Blindsight is "qualitatively degraded but nonetheless conscious vision, unacknowledged due to conservative response biases". An argument from SDT models and existing data, not a new experiment. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32757572
- **Newell & Shanks 2014.** Unconscious-influence claims rest on "inadequate procedures for assessing awareness" and unconsidered artefacts. [S] https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1419217/
- **Shanks 2017.** Selecting post hoc the participants or trials with chance awareness produces spurious "unconscious" effects through regression to the mean, shown by simulation and re-analysis. His table of affected fields includes unconscious affective priming and pain perception. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5486877/
- **Lähteenmäki et al. 2015.** Five experiments, n = 178, trial-by-trial 4-point Perceptual Awareness Scale. With no reported awareness, affective categorisation was at chance and affective priming was zero; with even partial awareness both appeared. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25559654
- **Pessoa et al. 2006.** With SDT-characterised awareness, no differential amygdala response to undetected 33 ms fearful faces. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15930371
- **Hedger et al. 2016** (meta-analysis, 2696 observers). Evidence for threat processing without awareness is "weak"; undermined by lax awareness measures and uncontrolled response bias. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27123863
- **Mertens & Engelhard 2020** (k = 41). "No convincing evidence for contingency unaware fear conditioning"; higher study quality predicted fewer positive findings; publication bias detected. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31747553

### Graded awareness

- PAS tracks performance more closely than confidence or wagering; perception is argued to be graded rather than all-or-none. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20133167 and https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16725347
- Lähteenmäki's data are the only graded-awareness result here that concerns affect, and they concern awareness of the *stimulus*. I found no PAS-style scale applied to the *feeling itself*.

### Evidence against A

- **Sandberg et al. 2010.** Above-chance performance when participants reported seeing nothing, on every scale, though only where awareness and performance also correlated. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20133167
- **Peters, Ro & Lau 2016.** Criterion effects are not merely contamination: "many real effects necessarily depend on criterion". If criterion placement is itself a substantive variable, A's "only a threshold" loses content. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27499928
- **Lamb 1991** remains a dose-ordered dissociation that no one has overturned, only left unreplicated with modern measures.
- **Scope gap.** Nearly all the pro-A work shows that *stimulus* awareness was underestimated. A's claim is about the *valence signal* being weakly felt. These are different thresholds, and the second is essentially untested.

### Verdict: **survives**, by default rather than by test

No SDT-controlled demonstration of affect driving behaviour at d' = 0 was found, and the best-controlled studies find affective effects vanish with awareness. But A as stated risks unfalsifiability: any dissociation can be relabelled "below criterion".

**Settling observation.** Run the Peters & Lau bias-free two-interval procedure on feeling itself: one interval carries a low-dose reinforcer or masked affective prime, the other a placebo. If choice or consumption discriminates the intervals while bets on "which interval felt different" stay at chance with adequate power, A is refuted. If betting tracks behaviour, A holds.

## B — "Feeling is an interrupt"

*Felt intensity tracks urgency-to-switch, not value at stake.*

### Evidence for

- **Simon 1967.** Emotion as an interrupt mechanism for a serial processor. [R; record has no abstract] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/5341441
- **Eccleston & Crombez 1999.** Pain "interrupts and demands attention"; it is "selected for action ... to urge escape"; interruption depends on threat value and on competing demands. Chronic pain is recast as "chronic interruption". [S] https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/pain-demands-attention-a-cognitive-affective-model-of-the-interru/
- **Task load lowers pain.** Bantick et al. 2002: pain ratings "significantly reduced" under the harder Stroop condition. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11844731
- **Buhle & Wager 2010.** Calibrated heat plus working-memory task: less pain during the task, and more heat degraded performance; pain fully mediated the effect, even trial-by-trial at fixed heat. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20129735
- **Goal-dependence.** Whether pain captures attention depends on the focal goal, not only on the pain. Review. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/19896002
- **Control.** Wiech et al. 2006: self-controlled noxious stimuli, physically identical to externally controlled ones, gave "less pain and anxiety". [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/17079679
- **Urges track act-now signals, not deficit.**
  - Thirst neurons in mice shut off on oral cues within seconds, anticipating future fluid balance before blood changes. Neural, not felt, but the authors link it to "the rapid satiation of thirst". [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27487211
  - Urinary urgency is most often cued by "on the way to the bathroom" and "arrival at home", in people with and without overactive bladder (n = 306, self-report). [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24805178
  - Air hunger "alerts us to a failure to meet an urgent homeostatic need"; lung expansion reduces it. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33577128
  - Scratching reduces itch, and its pleasure broadly parallels the itch reduction (n = 18). [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/22242789
- **Positive affect.** Carver 2003 treats pleasure as a "coasting" signal that frees attention for other goals. This fits B, but it is theory, not data. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29715724

### Evidence against

- **Chronic pain does not yield to load.** Meta-analysis in chronic pain patients: distraction vs control, k = 8, Hedges' g = 0.10, n.s. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29126746
  - An interrupt that keeps firing with no action available, and is not gated by competing tasks, is what B predicts should *not* happen. Eccleston & Crombez's "chronic interruption" names the anomaly without explaining it.
- **Distraction fails for some.** People with a large attentional bias to pain cues, or higher initial pain, benefit less (n = 53). [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/22989933
- **Controllability is ambiguous.** Salomons et al. 2004 found reduced ACC, insula and SII activation for perceived-controllable pain; a search snippet says ratings did not differ, but the abstract does not state this. [V for imaging; S for ratings] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15306654
- **Not discriminating.** Buhle & Wager interpret their own result as shared, capacity-limited resources. Load effects are predicted equally by "pain needs attention to be felt" with no interrupt function at all.
- **Scratch pleasure outlasts itch.** At the ankle, pleasure stayed elevated while itch fell. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/22242789
- **Not verified.** Whether pain drops once protective action has started; whether calm contentment or absorbing pleasures have any interrupt profile; the Beecher battlefield observation [R] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21017778.

### Verdict: **weakened**

B fits acute pain and bodily urges well, and the urge data (cue-triggered urgency, anticipatory thirst shut-off) favour "urgency to act" over "magnitude of harm". It does not account for chronic pain's resistance to load, and its treatment of positive feeling is a theoretical gloss. The strongest pro-B findings are also predicted by a plain attentional-capacity account.

**Settling observation.** Hold nociceptive input, threat value and task load constant, and vary only whether a switch could still help: the protective response is already irrevocably completed versus still pending. B predicts lower felt intensity in the completed condition. A capacity account predicts no difference.

## C — "Feeling bridges time"

*A sustained felt state keeps an event live until consequences arrive.*

### Evidence for

- **Clark & Squire 1998.** Amnesic patients acquired delay but not trace eyeblink conditioning (500–1000 ms gap). In normal volunteers awareness "was a prerequisite for successful trace conditioning" and unrelated to delay. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/9525860
- **Manns, Clark & Squire 2000.** Contingency knowledge and differential trace conditioning developed "approximately in parallel". [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11040257
- **Bekinschtein et al. 2009.** Patients with disorders of consciousness learned trace conditioning; learning correlated with cortical atrophy and predicted recovery; "none of these effects" under propofol. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/19767746
  - Numbers from news coverage: 22 patients, 16 healthy controls, 12 anaesthetised; 15 patients showed learning. [V2] https://www.the-scientist.com/can-unresponsive-brains-learn-43879
  - The inference is circular for C: the authors assume trace conditioning indexes awareness and conclude the patients may be conscious.

### Evidence against

- **The trace/delay split does not replicate.**
  - Lovibond et al. 2011, using Clark & Squire's own procedure: differential conditioning only in aware participants "with both trace and delay procedures". [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21586313
  - Knuttinen et al. 2001: acquisition correlated with awareness in both trace and delay. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11508714
  - Weidemann et al. 2016 (n = 80, delay): unaware participants showed no differential conditioning, F(1,14) = 1.42, p = .254; instruction raised both awareness and conditioning. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4831030
  - Lovibond & Shanks 2002: awareness appears necessary for conditioning generally. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11868231
  - So awareness tracks *knowing the contingency*, not *bridging a gap*. And "awareness" here is propositional knowledge, never a sustained feeling.
- **Trace conditioning without a forebrain.** Decerebrate guinea pigs acquired adaptively timed responses at a 500 ms trace interval, more slowly and less stably. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12713647
- **Trace conditioning in insects.**
  - Drosophila bridge a gap of seconds between odour and shock, reaching the same asymptote as delay conditioning. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21593308
  - The mechanism is a mushroom-body "sensory buffer" converging with dopamine. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/22123966
  - Honeybees likewise; the authors do call it "less reflexive" than delay conditioning. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21593307
  - C. elegans and Aplysia: not found this session.
- **Hours-long bridge under anaesthesia.** Rats: saccharin, then lithium 2 h later. Pentobarbital anaesthesia during the second half of the gap (1–2 h) did not prevent the taste aversion; it blocked learning only around the taste itself. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/2542844
  - This is the nearest thing to a direct test: the organism was unconscious across the gap and the credit was still assigned.
- **Feeling-free mechanisms.**
  - Dopamine potentiates a spine only within 0.3–2 s after glutamatergic input: a molecular eligibility trace. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25258080
  - Dopamine as prediction error (Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997) [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/9054347, or as retrospective causal association (Jeong et al. 2022) [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/36480599. Both bridge delays computationally.

### Verdict: **refuted** as a necessity claim; unsupported as a sufficiency claim

Delay-bridging is achieved by synaptic tags, insect mushroom bodies, a decerebrate brainstem, and an anaesthetised rat across two hours. The one human finding that tied the gap specifically to awareness has failed replication, and what it measured was contingency knowledge. A narrower version (felt states help with long, arbitrary, one-shot delays in humans) is untested rather than disproved.

**Settling observation.** In humans, abolish or occupy the felt state during the gap only (sedation, or a demanding filler task with affect ratings confirming the feeling lapsed) and test whether long-delay credit assignment survives. The rat taste-aversion result says it will.
