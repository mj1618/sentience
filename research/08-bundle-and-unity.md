## Conjecture 1 — "Sentience is not one thing"

### 1a. Graded anaesthesia: one threshold or several?

- Guedel's ether scheme is itself staged: stage I analgesia with consciousness impaired but not lost, stage II delirium, stage III surgical anaesthesia, stage IV medullary paralysis. [S] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557596/
- Modern pharmacology treats anaesthesia as separable endpoints. Amnesia, unconsciousness and immobility are described as mediated by different neural systems; immobility to noxious stimulation is largely a spinal-cord action, sedation and amnesia are brain actions. [S] https://bjan-sba.org/article/doi/10.1590/S0034-70942005000100013
- MAC (no movement to incision) and MAC-awake (no response to command) are distinct concentrations; memory is usually lost around MAC-awake. Opioids lower MAC but barely change MAC-awake, so drug classes move the endpoints independently. [S] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15448514
- Ketamine: in 37 healthy volunteers (0.4 mg/kg bolus then 0.4 mg/kg/h, fMRI, thermal pain) the authors conclude that analgesic and dissociative effects are independent and mediated by distinct neural pathways. Preprint, not peer reviewed. [V] https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.25.666594.full.pdf
- A search snippet stated that subanaesthetic ketamine cut pain unpleasantness more than intensity, but the source paper could not be identified, so no figure is given here. [S, unattributed]
- **Not found:** any study ordering loss of hunger, thirst, air hunger and itch against analgesia and loss of responsiveness for any agent. The "pain present but not bothersome" report for nitrous oxide and opioids is standard clinical lore [R] and was not verified.

Reading: the measured endpoints dissociate, but they are behavioural (movement, command-following, recall). They do not show that the *feelings* switch off piecemeal.

### 1b. Selective losses

| Feeling lost | Condition | What is spared | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | SCN9A (Nav1.7) loss of function | Touch, warm/cold, proprioception, tickle, pressure; histamine flare normal. Anosmia accompanies it. | [S] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17167479/ |
| Pain affect only | Pain asymbolia, 6 patients, insula damaged in all | Location and intensity recognised | [S] https://stafforini.com/works/berthier-1988-asymbolia-pain-sensorylimbic/ |
| Air hunger | Congenital central hypoventilation, 5 patients | No respiratory discomfort on CO2 inhalation or prolonged breath-hold, yet shortness-of-breath sensations on heavy exercise | [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8210759 |
| Thirst | Adipsic hypernatraemia with autoantibodies to subfornical organ / OVLT | No thirst despite severe hypernatraemia; no hypothalamic lesion on MRI | [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8029419 |
| Externally triggered fear | Bilateral amygdala damage (S.M. and two others) | 35% CO2 evoked fear and panic attacks in all three | [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23377128 |
| Sweet "liking" | Posterior ventral pallidum lesion (rat) | Replaced by disgust reactions | [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4425246/ |

Notes:

- Whether subjective itch is intact in Nav1.7 CIP was **not established**. A normal histamine flare is an axon reflex, not a report of itch; a search summary says Nav1.7-null mice scratch less. [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7012836
- The asymbolia review literature notes the original insula finding has not been replicated. [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4734900
- The CCHS result is the sharpest dissociation found: air hunger from CO2 is absent while exertional breathlessness remains, so even "breathlessness" splits into two.
- Fear likewise splits by trigger route (exteroceptive via amygdala, interoceptive not).
- Selective disgust loss in Huntington's disease and hunger loss after lateral hypothalamic lesions: [R], unchecked.

### 1c. Is there a common final pathway for valence?

For a shared mechanism:

- Berridge and Kringelbach report that diverse pleasures (food, sex, drugs, music, social) produce "strikingly similar patterns of brain activity", and that mu, delta and kappa opioid stimulation and endocannabinoids in the same nucleus accumbens hotspot all amplify sweetness "liking". The posterior ventral pallidum is the one site where a lesion removes positive "liking" and substitutes disgust. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4425246/
- Levy and Glimcher's meta-analysis of thirteen fMRI studies finds a vmPFC/OFC subregion encoding subjective value of different reward types on a common scale. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/22766486

Against a single substrate that a lesion can remove:

- Dopamine is not it: near-complete destruction of nigrostriatal and mesolimbic dopamine neurons leaves "liking" reactions to sweetness intact. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4425246/
- Insula plus ACC is not it: patient B, with complete bilateral insula destruction and bilateral damage to the affective anterior cingulate, retained pain, pleasure, itch, tickle, temperature, hunger, thirst, bladder and bowel feelings, and emotional feelings. The authors place basic feeling subcortically. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3657385
- vmPFC common currency is a valuation-for-choice signal from imaging; no evidence was found that vmPFC lesions abolish felt valence.
- **No report was found of any lesion or drug that abolishes all valence while leaving consciousness.** PAG lesions (akinetic mutism) and high-dose opioids were not checked. [R]

### 1d. Natural kind or constructed?

Barrett (2006) argues that anger, sadness, fear lack specific causal mechanisms and so are not natural kinds. Panksepp replied that her evidence is correlational and that causal (stimulation, lesion) evidence supports discrete subcortical systems. [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2597798/

Panksepp posits several distinct affect systems (the bundle position); Barrett posits one general "core affect" dressed by concepts [R] (the unity position at the level of valence). Unresolved.

### Conjecture 1 summary

- **Evidence for:** separable anaesthetic endpoints; clean single-feeling deletions (pain, CO2 air hunger, thirst, external fear); air hunger and fear each fragment further by input route; no lesion found that removes all valence; insula/ACC destruction removes none.
- **Evidence against:** overlapping hedonic hotspots with shared opioid and endocannabinoid amplification across pleasures; a single site (posterior ventral pallidum) necessary for positive "liking"; a common value scale in vmPFC/OFC; Damasio's own interpretation of patient B is a common *subcortical* feeling substrate, not no substrate.
- **Verdict: weakened.** "Separate triggers and separate input channels" survives well. "No common origin" does not: the selective losses are all upstream (receptor, sensor, relay), which is exactly what a shared downstream valence stage would also predict. The data cannot yet separate "many feelings" from "many inputs to one valuer".
- **Settling observation:** a graded-dose within-subject study (propofol or opioid) tracking ratings of pain unpleasantness, air hunger (CO2), thirst, itch and a pleasant stimulus at each step. One shared concentration at which all valence vanishes while intensity reports persist supports a common stage; reliably different thresholds per feeling support the bundle.

## Conjecture 2 — "One feeler per bottleneck"

### 2a. Split-brain

- Patient P.S. (LeDoux, Wilson, Gazzaniga 1977): the right hemisphere spelled "automobile race" as its job choice while the left repeatedly said "draftsman"; the right gave its mood as "good", the left "silly". On a first like/dislike scaling test many words drew "quite different evaluations" from the two hemispheres, the right consistently more negative; on a retest about a month later the ratings were "strikingly consistent". The authors conclude conscious processes "can be doubled". [V] https://people.psych.ucsb.edu/gazzaniga/michael/PDF/A%20divided%20mind.%20Observations%20on%20the%20conscious%20properties%20of%20separated%20hemispheres%20(1977).pdf
- Pinto et al. 2017: patients with complete callosotomy could not compare stimuli across hemifields, but showed full awareness of presence and well-above-chance report of location, orientation and identity across the whole visual field regardless of response type (left hand, right hand, verbal). The press summary gives two patients. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28122878
- de Haan et al. 2020: "the current consensus is that the body of evidence is insufficient to answer this question"; "perception appears to be more split, while responding remains largely unified"; spared subcortical connections are the proposed route for transfer. Whether each hemisphere decides independently is listed as open. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7305066/
- Emotional spill-over: in Gazzaniga's account, an aversive film shown to the right hemisphere left the speaking hemisphere reporting it felt scared and jumpy without knowing why. Secondary source only; primary not located. [S] https://www.discovermagazine.com/mind/the-last-of-the-split-brain-patients

Reading: cortical content divides; mood, arousal and response control mostly do not. That is what Conjecture 2 predicts if the arbitration bottleneck is subcortical and uncut. P.S.'s divergent preferences are the counter-case, but they were unstable across a month and concern stated evaluations, not felt hedonic state.

### 2b. Craniopagus twins (Hogan)

Reports say one twin responds when the other is tickled, that each can taste and see what the other does, and that a pacifier in one mouth calmed the other. Sources are documentaries and journalism plus philosophy papers; no peer-reviewed sensory testing was found. [V, secondary] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krista_and_Tatiana_Hogan

If accurate, this is shared *input* with two brainstems and two motor systems; whether there are two feelers of one sensation could not be established.

### 2c. Octopus

- Isolated arms withdraw within about a second from pinch and acetic acid, so the reflex needs no brain. Conference report. [S] https://openresearch.surrey.ac.uk/esploro/outputs/conferencePresentation/Preliminary-evidence-for-a-nociceptive-reflex/99511421802346
- Arm injury produces neural hyperexcitability in injured *and uninjured* arms for at least 24 h; the authors state this does not demonstrate pain-like states. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24239646
- Crook 2021: octopuses avoided a context paired with acetic-acid injection, preferred a context paired with relief, showed no preference without pain; injection-site grooming occurred in all injected animals and was abolished by local anaesthetic. [V] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33733076 (the full-text claim of central recordings was not read)
- Single-arm maze learning requires the brain to use information from one arm; the authors conclude arms are under "considerable central control". [S] https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/use-of-peripheral-sensory-information-for-central-nervous-control/

Reading: the reflex is per-arm, but everything that marks valence (place learning, directed grooming, whole-body sensitisation) is whole-animal. No evidence of per-arm learned avoidance was found.

### 2d. Merker and Morsella

- Merker 2007 proposes a system from the midbrain roof to the basal diencephalon that relates target selection, action selection and motivation, converting parallel forebrain processing into the "limited-capacity, sequential mode" needed for coherent behaviour; hydranencephalic children and decorticate mammals are the evidence. [S] https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/merker-s-midbrain-centered-reality-space (secondary; primary not fetched)
- Morsella 2005: phenomenal states allow otherwise encapsulated response systems to jointly influence skeletal muscle (PRISM); conflicts that do not involve skeletal muscle, such as the pupillary reflex, are unconscious. [S] https://acmelab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2005_the_function_of_phenomenal_states.pdf

Both are theoretical syntheses resting on case observation and the conscious/unconscious conflict contrast; no decisive experiment was found.

### 2e. Dissociative identity, hemispherectomy

- DID: under PET, the trauma-related identity state showed strong psychophysiological reactions to a trauma script and the neutral state did not; simulating controls did not reproduce the pattern. [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3387157 This is state-dependent affect in one body with one action bottleneck, and it does not show simultaneous feelers.
- Hemispherectomy: one paper on network stability after hemispherectomy was located by title only. [S] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32550146 Preserved unified consciousness after removal of a hemisphere is [R].

### Conjecture 2 summary

- **Evidence for:** after callosotomy, perception splits but responding and mood largely do not, matching an intact subcortical arbiter; octopus valence-marking behaviour is central while reflexes are peripheral; Hogan reports suggest shared sensation without merged agents; Merker and Morsella independently locate consciousness at action arbitration.
- **Evidence against:** P.S.'s hemispheres stated different goals and, on one test, different like/dislike ratings, with no extra bottleneck created; the consensus review says the unity question is unanswered; Hogan evidence is anecdotal; the bottleneck is nowhere defined well enough to count (cortex, basal ganglia and midbrain all arbitrate).
- **Verdict: survives, untested.** Nothing found contradicts it; nothing found tests it against the rival that feeling follows information integration rather than arbitration.
- **Settling observation:** in a complete-callosotomy patient, condition an aversive outcome to a stimulus seen only by one hemisphere, then offer that stimulus as a choice to the other hemisphere with a non-verbal response. Avoidance and autonomic response by the naive hemisphere mean one feeler (subcortical valence, shared); indifference with hemisphere-specific avoidance means two.
