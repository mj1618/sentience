## Answers from the tables

**(a) Minimal set abolishing all feeling.** No structure in the table abolishes all feeling while leaving demonstrable wakefulness. The only lesion that removes everything is bilateral upper pontine–midbrain tegmentum (including parabrachial and neighbouring nuclei), and it does so by producing coma. Every lesion above that level — insula, cingulate, amygdala, orbitofrontal, SI, even these together in Patient B and Roger — leaves reported feeling. The nearest thing to "awake without feeling" is pallidal anhedonia/auto-activation deficit and PAG-region akinetic mutism; the first is partial and rests on [R], and in the second feeling was never assessed. So the lesion record does **not** separate loss of feeling from loss of wakefulness. That is consistent with the two being one mechanism, and equally with a testing gap: feeling is measured by report, and report needs arousal.

**(b) Candidate generators (stimulation evokes strong valence, lesion abolishes it).**
- *Hypothalamus*: stimulation gives panic; damage abolishes thirst by self-report. This is the cleanest double result, for specific feelings, in single cases.
- *PAG / upper brainstem*: stimulation gives the most intense negative affect on the map; lesions give mutism or coma. The necessity half is untested for feeling, so it remains a candidate, not a demonstration.
- *Accumbens–pallidum*: stimulation gives euphoria and smiling; pallidal lesion gives anhedonia [R]. Candidate for positive valence.
- Disqualified: amygdala (stimulation gives fear, but CO2 panic survives its loss — a trigger), posterior insula (stimulation gives pain, but pain survives its loss), mid-cingulate.

**(c) Shared necessary structure for pain-type and pleasure-type feeling.** None demonstrated apart from the tegmental arousal core. The two families dissociate: SM lacks externally triggered fear and keeps other feelings; pallidal damage removes pleasure without reported loss of pain [R]; hypothalamic damage removes thirst alone. The verified evidence fits several valence-specific subcortical systems sharing one enabling core, with the "pleasure" half depending on recalled material.

**(d) Cortex-first versus brainstem/diencephalon-first.** The map favours subcortex-first for raw valence: no cortical lesion abolishes feeling; convexity cortex is nearly silent to stimulation (pain 1.4%, two sites only); the strongest evoked affects come from PAG, hypothalamus, accumbens and the subthalamic region.
- *Strongest objection to the brainstem-first reading*: in every verified case of preserved feeling, some cortex survived (SI/SII, parietal, lateral prefrontal in Patient B and Roger). The only cortex-free cases, hydranencephaly and decorticate animals, supply behaviour and no report. And subcortical stimulation in a human with intact cortex may only trigger a cortical experience.
- *Strongest objection to the cortex-first reading*: it must retreat to "any remaining cortex will do" once the insula, cingulate and orbitofrontal cortex are shown dispensable, and the remaining candidates (SI, lateral cortex) do not evoke affect when stimulated; it also has to treat the organised affective behaviour of decorticate children and rats as unfelt without independent evidence.

**(e) Conservation.**
- *Lamprey*: habenula circuitry controlling dopamine and serotonin is conserved [V-abs] ([Stephenson-Jones 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3271889/)); the dopamine system, with pallial, basal-ganglia and tectal connections, is conserved [V-abs] ([Pérez-Fernández 2014](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24942187)); basal ganglia organisation is conserved across vertebrates [V-abs] ([Frost-Nylén 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11097981/)). Hypothalamus, tectum, reticular formation and a PAG homologue are present [R]. No six-layered neocortex [R].
- *Fish*: habenula–interpeduncular pathway conserved and involved in fear and anxiety [V-abs] ([Okamoto 2012](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21567982)); hypothalamus, tectum, griseum centrale, parabrachial-like nucleus, pallium without neocortex [R].
- *Insects*: Barron & Klein, following Merker, take the integrated vertebrate midbrain (tectum, hypothalamus, PAG, substantia nigra, striatum, reticular formation) as sufficient, and propose the central complex as the tectum analogue, with mushroom bodies and protocerebrum; they deny it to nematodes [V-full] ([2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4983823/)). The analogy is about spatial and action-selection integration, not valence generation; an insect analogue of PAG or hypothalamus is not proposed there.

So the candidate generators in (b) all predate neocortex and are present in the earliest vertebrates; the structures shown dispensable are the phylogenetically newer ones.

## What rests on recalled or unverified material

- Pallidal anhedonia and auto-activation deficit (records located, no abstracts): carries the "pleasure" half of (b) and (c).
- Bejjani 1999 details; Heath's pleasure reports; Hohmann's n and gradient.
- Nashold's exact patient wording. The terror and "impending death" description is verified only through a secondary review.
- Critics of hydranencephaly and decorticate evidence: commentaries not retrieved.
- Lobotomy and cingulotomy "pain present but not bothersome"; thalamic pain syndrome; human PAG lesions beyond the mutism case; fish and lamprey PAG/hypothalamus homologues.
- Meletti's fear proportion comes from a review, not the primary paper; Penfield's own frequency of evoked emotion was not found.
- Abstract-only items, where an extrapolation could hide: Mazzola, Shewmon, Aleman & Merker, Lanteaume, Rao, Wilent, Schlaepfer, Lenz, Koenigs, the coma-mapping papers, and the spinal and autonomic-failure studies.
