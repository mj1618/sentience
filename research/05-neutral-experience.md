# 05 — Neutral experience: is there experience that is neither valenced feeling nor thought?

Brief compiled 2026-10-03. Tags: **[V]** primary text/abstract fetched and read; **[S]** search snippet only; **[R]** recalled, not checked. Fetched pages were read through a summarising tool, so quoted wording under [V] should be re-checked before being cited onward. The only source read in full first-hand is Laplane & Dubois 2001.

Problem: if consciousness = sentience (felt valence) + thought, then plainly seeing a grey wall, or a wakeful "empty mind", must be either (a) faintly valenced, (b) evidence for a third ingredient, or (c) itself constructed from evaluative response.

## 1. Is all experience valenced?

**Core affect as ever-present.** Barrett & Bliss-Moreau (2009) argue affect is a psychologically irreducible primitive; they cite Wundt that people are likely "never in a state entirely free from feeling", and claim "Without affect, there is visual sensation, but no sight", and that background core affect "is perceived as a property of the world" rather than as one's reaction [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2884406/. Note that this concedes a residual "visual sensation" without affect. Russell's core-affect circumplex places a neutral point at the centre of a continuous space, so "neutral" is a location, not an absence [R].

**Micro-valence.** Lebrecht, Bar, Barrett & Tarr (2012) is a hypothesis/review paper, not a new experiment. Claim: most objects carry a subtle valence that "ranges in magnitude but is always present", computed as part of object perception. The evidence cited is prior work: curved objects preferred over sharp ones (Bar & Neta 2006), and consistent within/between-observer valence ratings of everyday objects such as telephones (Lebrecht & Tarr 2010) [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3328080. What was measured is therefore forced preference/rating consistency. That shows valence *can be extracted* on demand, not that it is *felt* in passive viewing.

**Vedanā.** Abhidharma lists vedanā (pleasant / unpleasant / neither) as a universal mental factor accompanying every moment of consciousness: one of seven universals in Theravāda, five in Mahāyāna [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedan%C4%81. As a phenomenological claim it cuts both ways: hedonic tone is always present as a *dimension*, but a third, neutral value is explicitly recognised.

**Value as the function of experience.** Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry (2022) propose "phenomenal worthiness": experience has intrinsic value and acts as a "mental currency" allowing comparison of disparate experiences [S abstract] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9036654. They state that every act of perception "is automatically associated with some value ... regardless of whether the value itself is close to zero" [V, same URL]. This is position (a) stated openly, and it concedes near-zero cases.

**Carruthers.** "Valence and Value" (2018) treats valence as a nonconceptual representation of seeming goodness/badness on a single continuum, and as the central component of *affective* states [S] https://stafforini.com/works/carruthers-2018-valence-value/. I found no claim by Carruthers that all conscious experience is valenced.

**Affect-first theories.** Solms (*The Hidden Spring*) locates the source of consciousness in the upper brainstem and treats it as fundamentally affective, citing hydranencephaly. Seth's review objects that inferring consciousness in such children is "a leap", and that recovery from coma and vegetative state is best tracked by cortical networks [V] https://neurobanter.com/2021/02/18/mixed-feelings-about-a-hard-problem-review-of-the-hidden-spring/. Damasio and Seth make related interoceptive claims [R].

**Counter-position: neutral affect is real.** Gasper, Spencer & Hu (2019): people report feeling neutral, neutral ratings are not highly correlated with positive/negative affect, and a third "neutral" factor emerges across five samples. In one cited study 7.5% of American and 22% of Japanese students reported no emotion over the preceding 3 hours [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6856204/. They frame neutrality as a *felt affective state* ("indifferent"), so neutral is still a value of the valence variable.

## 2. Does perception depend on affect/interoception?

**Park et al. 2014** (Nat Neurosci 17:612–618). MEG: the amplitude of neural responses locked to heartbeats *before* stimulus onset predicted detection of a faint grating, in right posterior inferior parietal lobule and ventral anterior cingulate. Neither measured bodily parameters nor overall cortical excitability accounted for it [V abstract] https://hub.tmu.edu.tw/en/publications/spontaneous-fluctuations-in-neural-responses-to-heartbeats-predic/. **The effect-size numbers (N, hit-rate change, sensitivity vs criterion) could not be verified**: the Nature page is paywalled and no open source gave them. The authors read this as support for a "neural subjective frame", defined as the biological mechanisms "defining the subject as a biological entity", an anchoring point for first-person statements [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3965163. Caution: this is interoceptive *self-anchoring*, not valence. It supports "body signals gate awareness", not "affect gates awareness".

**Depersonalisation/derealisation (DPDR).** Patients have blunted affect and blunted autonomic responding: skin conductance responses to unpleasant pictures were reduced and their latency prolonged (3.01 s vs 2.5 s controls and 2.1 s anxiety group; P = .02; Sierra et al. 2002) [S] https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/autonomic-response-in-depersonalization-disorder/. They complain the world looks flat, two-dimensional, lifeless, foggy [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12640545/. Factor analysis of the Cambridge Depersonalisation Scale (138 patients) separates "Emotional Numbing" from "Alienation from Surroundings" among four factors [S] https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Unpacking_the_depersonalization_syndrome_an_exploratory_factor_analysis_on_the_Cambridge_Depersonalization_Scale/23420366.

The key test: Kusztor, Mulay, Yamada, Hohwy & Tsuchiya (2025, *Neuroscience of Consciousness*, registered report). Participants rated the realness of images at varied saturation/contrast (N = 100 per condition) and adjusted images to match everyday experience (N = 75 and 50). DP/DR symptom scores did not predict either measure: BF01 ≈ 1359 and 131 for ratings, ≈ 15.8 and 14.6 for adjustment. The authors conclude that reported loss of vividness does "not reflect genuine perceptual changes" but a (meta-)cognitive interpretation. Limits: online, **non-clinical** sample with few high scorers (mean CDS-state 31 on a 0–220 scale) [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12640545/.

So: blunting affect removes the *feeling of reality*, apparently without removing sensory quality. This is the closest thing to a natural dissociation, and it points away from (c).

**SSRI blunting.** Price, Cole & Goodwin (2009): patients describe emotions as dulled, numbed, flattened, positive and negative alike [S] https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/emotional-sideeffects-of-selective-serotonin-reuptakeinhibitors-qualitative-study/88C72E9EA0961CDE777C2FDCDBCE1CA9. I found no report of altered sensory vividness. Alexithymia, anhedonia and Cotard were not checked.

## 3. Arousal/wakefulness as an ingredient

Standard clinical model: consciousness has two components, arousal (brainstem populations projecting to thalamus and cortex) and awareness. Coma lacks both; the vegetative state is "wakefulness without awareness"; akinetic mutism follows mesial frontal injury [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disorder_of_consciousness. Intralaminar/central thalamus as the arousal relay, and Schiff et al. 2007 central thalamic stimulation, were not verified [R]. Hence arousal without experience exists (VS). The open question is arousal *with* experience but without content.

**Minimal phenomenal experience.** Metzinger (2020) proposes that "pure awareness" reports refer to a predictive model of **tonic alertness**, an "unpartitioned epistemic space" [V abstract] https://philosophymindscience.org/index.php/phimisci/article/view/8960, implemented by the ARAS [S, same search]. This is a candidate third ingredient: experience of wakefulness itself.

**Is MPE valenced?** Gamma & Metzinger (2021): 1,403 usable responses, 92 items, a 12-factor solution explaining 44% of variance. One factor is "Peace, Bliss and Silence" (top loadings as extracted: at peace 0.72, at ease 0.72, blissfulness 0.45). But the authors state that "MPE *as such* is not an emotional state", though it can "trigger" mostly positive affect, and may "co-exist with a mostly subtle ... form of bliss" [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8279394. So: empirically, pure-awareness episodes are usually reported with mild positive tone; theoretically, Metzinger treats the tone as an accompaniment, not a constituent. Self-selected meditators and retrospective report limit this. Metzinger 2024 (*The Elephant and the Blind*) was not checked for a neutral-valence analysis.

## 4. Auto-activation deficit (Laplane & Dubois 2001) — read in full

[V] https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/2001-laplane.pdf (Mov Disord 16:810–814).

- Report: the mind is "empty, a total blank"; no thoughts, no projection into the future; reported "in almost the same terms by numerous patients". Not all patients report it.
- Perception and cognition are intact: they answer questions appropriately, WAIS/Raven are generally normal, and one played high-level bridge when prompted. The paper does not explicitly ask what they *perceive* while empty; it is an inference that perception continues (one patient waits "for a light" with an unlit cigarette).
- Affect: "flattened", "blunted"; no anxiety, fear or desire; **not bored**; they react appropriately to good or bad news but return quickly to a "neutral" state and "appear to have no feelings when left alone". Depression can nevertheless coexist.
- Lesions: bilateral pallidum/lenticular nuclei, caudate, bilateral thalamus; "striato-thalamo-prefrontal loops".
- Authors' reading: "conscious awareness without any content, i.e., consciousness per se."

This is the strongest clinical candidate for awake experience with thought *and* affect both near zero. Weakness: retrospective report by patients whose spontaneous reporting is the thing impaired.

## 5. Blindsight and Humphrey

Humphrey's monkey Helen (visual cortex removed) relearned visually guided behaviour, yet seemed "strangely unsure of herself"; human blindsight shows perception dissociated from conscious sensation [V] https://nextbigideaclub.com/magazine/sentience-invention-consciousness-bookbite/40738/. His theory, *not verified against the book*: sensation began as an evaluative bodily response to surface stimulation ("sentition"); the response was internalised (privatised) so the command signal loops back on itself, and this recurrent loop (an attractor, the "ipsundrum") is what we experience as qualia [R]. On this view the red of red *is* a monitored evaluative response, which is position (c). He restricts sentience to mammals and birds [V] https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/AvubGwD2xkCD4tGtd/only-mammals-and-birds-are-sentient-according-to.

Critiques:
- Play/sensation-seeking as the marker fails: octopus and bumblebee play are documented; the theory bundles self-modelling, reinforcement and qualia without showing they are inseparable; it may dissolve rather than solve the hard problem [V, same URL; forum-level critique, not peer-reviewed].
- Blindsight may not be perception without experience at all: Phillips argues it is "qualitatively degraded conscious vision" masked by conservative response criteria [S] https://www.ianbphillips.com/files/papers/blindsight.pdf.
- From §2: if sensation were internalised evaluation, blunting evaluation should degrade sensory quality. The DPDR null result says it does not.

## 6. Direct attempts to find zero-valence experience

None found that target it as such. Nearest, all above: Kusztor et al. 2025 (vividness vs DP/DR symptoms, null), Gasper et al. 2019 (neutral affect as a separable self-report factor), Gamma & Metzinger 2021 (affective items in pure-awareness reports). No study was found that manipulates affect and measures phenomenal vividness psychophysically in a clinical group. This is a gap.

## Bearing on sentience + thought

**(a) Neutral perception is faint valence.** Supported by assertion and by rating studies (micro-valence; Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry), but the evidence shows valence is *extractable*, not that it *constitutes* the seeing. The authors concede values "close to zero", and Barrett concedes "visual sensation" without affect. (a) is unfalsifiable unless it predicts that sensory quality scales with valence magnitude, and the DPDR data suggest it does not.

**(b) A third ingredient.** Best supported. (i) DPDR: affect and felt reality drop, sensory matching is unchanged. (ii) AAD: thought and affect both near zero, yet patients are awake and report the state. (iii) MPE: modelled as tonic alertness, with bliss an accompaniment. (iv) Clinical neurology already separates arousal from content. Candidates for the third term are arousal/tonic alertness and perceptual phenomenality; these may be two things, not one.

**(c) Perception built from evaluative response (Humphrey).** Weakest empirically: it rests on an evolutionary narrative, a contested reading of blindsight, and is strained by DPDR.

Caveat favouring the owner's equation: Park 2014 and the "subjective frame" suggest perception needs a bodily *subject* signal. If "sentience" is widened from valence to interoceptive self-presence, (b)'s third ingredient may collapse into a redefined first one.

**Discriminating observations.**
1. Psychophysics in *clinical* DPDR or under SSRI/opioid blunting: do contrast, saturation and vividness matches shift with affect? (a)/(c) predict yes, (b) predicts no.
2. Experience sampling of AAD patients during the "blank" state with forced-choice probes ("do you see the room?", "pleasant/unpleasant/neither?"). Seeing + "neither" supports (b).
3. Dose-response: does micro-valence magnitude predict detection threshold or vividness for neutral objects once salience is controlled? Needed for (a).
4. Does heartbeat-evoked response amplitude track valence or only detection? If only detection, the bodily signal is a subject-frame, not affect.
5. MPE with real-time valence probes: are any episodes reliably reported as flatly neutral?
