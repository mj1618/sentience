# Research 27 — Anaesthesia, systems level (scenario 32, accounts N, B, T, D; predictions 5 and 6)

Neutral evidence brief. No scoring, no verdict. Gathered 2026-10-04.

## How to read this

**Three things kept apart.** R = responsiveness (movement, righting, command-following).
M = memory (recall afterwards). E = experience (anything it was like, by report). Every
entry says which of these it measured.

**Standing caveat on animal work.** Every rodent and macaque result below measures
**responsiveness** (loss/return of righting reflex, arousal score, EEG pattern). None is
paired with a marker validated against human report unless explicitly stated. The righting
reflex survives decerebration in rats and its return does not predict recovery of
cognitive performance (Vincent et al. 2021, Front Syst Neurosci, rats, full text via
summariser: "RORR is intact in decerebrated rats"; RORR latency not correlated with
5-choice task performance).

**Source-status tags.**
- [A] abstract read in full (Europe PMC record).
- [FT] open-access full text read through a fetch-and-summarise tool (a small model
  extracted the answers; I did not read the raw text myself, so numbers carry that risk).
- [FTx] full-text XML pulled and sentences read directly.
- [S] search-engine snippet only.
- [M] from my own memory, not verified this session.

**Replication status** is stated per item: "same group" means all supporting papers share
a laboratory.

## 1. Experience during unresponsiveness (the report baseline every marker must meet)

### 1a. Connected consciousness on the isolated forearm (R to command present, M absent)

| Study | Status | n, species | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sanders et al. 2017, Anesthesiology (ConsCIOUS-1) | [A] | 260 surgical patients, 6 sites | IFT response after intubation 12/260 = **4.6%**; 5/12 signalled pain by a second squeeze; explicit recall 0/253. Responders younger (39 vs 51 y); depth-monitor values higher in responders (54 vs 42 before intubation) but "wide range". |
| Lennertz et al. 2023, BJA (ConsCIOUS-2) | [FT] | 338 completed (344 enrolled), age 18-40, 10 hospitals | Response **37/338 = 11%** (95% CI 8-15); women 31/232 = 13%, men 6/106 = 6%; 18/37 (49%) endorsed pain; definite explicit recall 0.3%, possible 1.5%. Continuous anaesthetic between induction and laryngoscopy lowered odds (adjusted OR 0.43). No EEG predictor analysed in this paper. |
| Gaskell et al. 2017, BJA | [A] | 90 patients (subset of ConsCIOUS-1), frontal EEG | 6/90 responded; **3 responded while showing the frontal alpha-delta pattern** "widely held to indicate anaesthetic-induced unconsciousness"; spectra of responders "similar to those of non-responders (P>0.05)"; "none of the derived depth-of-anaesthesia indices could robustly discriminate" responders. Pre-stated falsification criterion (one responder with alpha-delta). |
| Pandit 2014, Conscious Cogn ("dysanaesthesia") | [A] | review/hypothesis | Proposes a state of awareness without distress or recall; cites Brice-questionnaire recall ~1:500 vs spontaneous report ~1:15,000. Hypothesis paper, no new data. |
| NAP5 (Pandit et al. 2014, BJA/Anaesthesia) | [A] | UK national audit | Spontaneously reported accidental awareness **~1:19,600** (95% CI 1:16,700-23,450); with neuromuscular block ~1:8,200; without ~1:135,900; caesarean section ~1:670. This is M (recalled and volunteered), so a floor, not a rate of E. |

What these measure: command-following (R) with the arm spared from paralysis, taken as
evidence of connected E at that moment; M is almost always absent. Replication:
ConsCIOUS-1 and -2 are the same consortium, different cohorts, consistent direction
(4.6% all ages; 11% young adults). The gap between 1:19,600 (recall) and 5-11%
(in-the-moment response) is three to four orders of magnitude.

### 1b. Disconnected experience (dreaming) reported on immediate awakening (R absent, E reported)

| Study | Status | n, design | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Noreika et al. 2011, Brain Cogn | [A] | healthy volunteers; dexmedetomidine, propofol, sevoflurane, xenon sessions (session counts not in abstract) | Experience recalled "in almost 60% of sessions" after loss of responsiveness. With dexmedetomidine, experiences associated with shallower depth-monitor values. Per-drug rates not verified. |
| Radek et al. 2018, BJA | [A] | 47 healthy men: dexmedetomidine 23, propofol 24; woken **during constant infusion** | 93 interviews; **84%** contained experiences from the unresponsive period (dexmedetomidine **90%**, propofol **74%**); dreaming in 86% of those reports; awareness of surroundings "rare and linked to brief arousals". Arousable: dexmedetomidine 18/23; propofol 10/24. Authors: "unresponsiveness does not induce unconsciousness or necessarily even disconnectedness." |
| Scheinin et al. 2021, J Neurosci | [A]+[FT] | healthy men; exp 1 PET n=39 (propofol 19, dexmedetomidine 20 [FT]); exp 2 sleep n=37 | **~80%** of arousable subjects reported experiences at constant dose; sleep: N1 58%, N2 66%, N3 83% [FT]. Connected awareness reported by 1 subject per drug [FT]. |
| Leslie et al. 2007, Anesthesiology | [A] | 300 surgical patients | Dreaming reported by **22%** on emergence; BIS during maintenance identical in dreamers and non-dreamers (37 vs 38, P=0.68). Authors infer dreaming occurs during recovery. |
| Leslie et al. 2009, Anesthesiology | [A] | 300 patients randomised propofol vs desflurane | Dream recall **27% vs 28%**; associated with REM-like EEG just before waking. |
| Matus et al. 2021 | [A] | 124 endoscopy patients, propofol | Dreaming 43%. |
| Sikka, Hu, Heifets 2026, BJA scoping review | [A] | 157 studies, 87,866 participants | Experimental settings "often 40%-80%"; large clinical trials "typically 3%-8%"; ketamine clinical 0-95%, experimental 44-100%; propofol clinical 0-64%, experimental 0-77%. Dream recall "not associated with intraoperative awareness". |
| Sarasso et al. 2015 (below) | [A] | 18 volunteers | No experience reported after propofol or xenon; "long, vivid dreams" after ketamine. |

Caveats that cut both ways:
- Reports are retrospective. A "no experience" report cannot distinguish absent E from
  absent M (Colombo 2019 words it "absent/inaccessible to recall"). Scheinin 2021 states
  that neither responsiveness nor report "can indisputably verify a person's actual state".
- Timing: Leslie attributes surgical-patient dreams to the emergence period, not the
  maintenance period. The constant-infusion designs (Radek, Scheinin, Casey, Bajwa) are the
  only ones that pin the report to the drugged, unresponsive interval, and those are at
  light sedation-level doses in volunteers, not surgical anaesthesia.
- Conflict to note: Sarasso found no reports after propofol (n≈6, single awakening after
  deep, Ramsay-6 anaesthesia); Radek found 74% (n=24, lighter dose, awakening during
  infusion). The two are different depths and different interview timings.

## 2. Network markers against report (prediction N.5; also B.5)

The question for N.5 is narrow: does a network measure separate **reported experience from
no report at matched unresponsiveness**, ideally within one drug. Across-drug separation
(ketamine vs propofol) confounds experience with drug.

### 2a. Perturbational Complexity Index (TMS-EEG)

| Study | Status | n | Result | Bears on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Casali et al. 2013, Sci Transl Med | [A] | healthy subjects in wake, dreaming, NREM, midazolam, xenon, propofol; plus brain-injured patients. Per-drug n not in abstract ([M]: 6 per agent, unverified) | PCI "reliably discriminated the level of consciousness in single individuals". | State separation; report only for wake/REM. |
| Sarasso et al. 2015, Curr Biol | [A] | 18 volunteers total, propofol/xenon/ketamine ([M]: 6 per drug, unverified; a re-analysis by Colombo 2019 used 5 per drug) | All unresponsive (Ramsay 6 per Colombo 2019). Propofol: low-amplitude local slow wave, low complexity, no report. Xenon: high-amplitude global slow wave, low complexity, no report. Ketamine: "wakefulness-like, complex" response, vivid dream reports. | **Across-drug** separation of report from no-report at matched unresponsiveness. Not within-drug. Same group as PCI's developers. |
| Casarotto et al. 2016, Ann Neurol | [A] | benchmark 150 (healthy + communicative brain-injured); then 38 MCS, 43 vegetative | Cutoff gave 100% sensitivity and specificity in the benchmark; 94.7% sensitivity for MCS; **9/43 vegetative patients had high PCI** overlapping the conscious distribution. | Arousal present (eyes open) with low PCI in 34/43; arousal present with high PCI and no behaviour in 9/43. Whether those 9 have E is unknown by construction. |
| Rosanova et al. 2018, Nat Commun | [A] | 16 vegetative patients, 20 awake controls, 8 sleepers | Sleep-like cortical OFF-periods after TMS in vegetative patients despite wakeful arousal. | Brainstem arousal intact, cortical complexity low. |
| Nieminen et al. 2016, Sci Rep | [A] | NREM sleep, serial awakenings (n not in abstract; [M] 6) | **Within-state**: no-experience awakenings showed a larger negative deflection and shorter phase-locked response than dream awakenings; amplitude inversely correlated with report length. | Within-state separation, in sleep, not anaesthesia; small n; same group. |
| Bajwa et al. 2025, Sci Rep | [A]+[FT] | 20 volunteers, propofol to deep sedation (median 2.0 µg/ml, RASS −4; arousable only by trapezius pinch and shouting) | 52 awakenings: 24 experience, **5 no experience**, 23 unarousable/incoherent. PCIst: wake 53.6; sedation-with-experience 21.7; sedation-no-experience 27.3 (direction opposite to N.5). **No significant difference** within sedation: PCIst F=0.536, p=0.590; LZc F=0.727, p=0.489 [FT]. | **The only within-drug, matched-unresponsiveness PCI test against report that I found.** Null. Power is weak (5 no-experience reports, each from a different subject); independent group (Oslo). |
| Farnes et al. 2020, PLoS One | [A] | 10 volunteers, sub-anaesthetic ketamine (responsive) | PCI unchanged from wake; spontaneous LZc/ACE/SCE increased and correlated with altered-experience ratings. Authors: PCI indexes capacity, spontaneous diversity indexes content. | Content vs level distinction (relevant to B.5 wording). |

Not found: any study measuring PCI in ketamine-anaesthetised subjects who reported **no**
experience, or PCI under deep surgical ketamine doses in humans. [M] In rats, ketamine PCI
is reported to be dose-dependent (Arena et al.), unverified this session.

### 2b. Spectral slope / spectral exponent

- Colombo et al. 2019, NeuroImage [A]: same subjects as Sarasso, **n=5 per drug**.
  Broad-band exponent steepened in every subject under propofol and xenon (no report), and
  stayed wake-like under ketamine (reports). "Highly correlated to PCI". Across-drug only.
- Lendner et al. 2020, eLife [A]: four datasets, intracranial and scalp. Slope separates
  wake from propofol, NREM **and REM sleep**; "discriminates wakefulness from REM sleep".
  Authors label it "a marker of arousal level". REM is the state with the highest dream
  report rate, so on this dataset slope tracks arousal state and not reported experience.
  Colombo and Lendner used different frequency ranges (1-40 Hz vs 30-45 Hz [M]); they are
  not a like-for-like contradiction, but an account may not switch range between agents
  under the scenario's rule.
- Maschke et al. 2023 preprint [FT]: re-analysis, 5 per drug; ketamine stays near
  criticality, propofol/xenon depart. Same dataset again (three papers, one sample of 15-18).

### 2c. Frontoparietal feedback connectivity

- Lee, Mashour et al. 2013, Anesthesiology [A]: surgical patients, ketamine n=30,
  propofol n=9, sevoflurane n=9. Frontal-to-parietal symbolic transfer entropy fell after
  loss of responsiveness with **all three, ketamine included** (0.0074 → 0.0055).
  Experience reports were not collected. If ketamine subjects were dreaming (as elsewhere
  reported at 44-100%), then this measure fell while experience persisted; the paper does
  not test that.
- Vlisides et al. 2017, Anesthesiology [A]: 10 volunteers; anterior-to-posterior alpha
  connectivity "maximally depressed" with anaesthetic ketamine, dose-dependent.
- Sanders et al. 2018, BJA [A]: 8 subjects, TMS-EEG with dynamic causal modelling; propofol's
  "primary effect" was impaired **feedforward**, not feedback, connectivity.
- Kallionpää et al. 2020, BJA [A]: 47 men, dexmedetomidine 23 / propofol 24, constant
  infusion. Local prefrontal-frontal alpha connectivity tracked responsiveness and reversed
  on arousal at fixed dose; **anterior-posterior alpha connectivity "did not differentiate"**
  loss from return of responsiveness. (Tracks R; experience not the contrast.)
- Pal et al. 2020, J Neurosci [A]+[FT]: rats; see 3c. Wake-like behaviour restored under
  sevoflurane with frontoparietal gamma connectivity still at anaesthetised levels.

### 2d. Alpha anteriorisation / frontal alpha-delta

- Gaskell 2017 (above): 3 command-following patients had the pattern.
- Blain-Moraes et al. 2015, Anesthesiology [A]: 10 volunteers (7 analysed), sevoflurane: no
  consistent anteriorisation at concentrations producing unresponsiveness.
- Frohlich, Toker, Monti 2021, Brain [A]: review of conscious states with high-amplitude
  delta (Angelman syndrome, some epilepsies, responsiveness under propofol, dreaming).

### 2e. Studies that contrasted experience vs no experience, or connected vs disconnected, at matched unresponsiveness

| Study | Status | n | Contrast | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scheinin et al. 2021, J Neurosci | [A]+[FT] | 39 (anaesthesia), 37 (sleep) | Responsive-connected vs unresponsive-disconnected, within subject, constant dose (PET) | State difference localised to **thalamus, anterior/posterior cingulate, angular gyri**, "independent from the pharmacologic agent, drug concentration, and direction of change". Drug effect was separate: "widespread depression of brain activity across cortical areas"; "only minimal and inconsistent differences on the cortical surface" between states [FT]. **Did not compare unresponsive-with-experience vs unresponsive-without** [FT]; most disconnected subjects were dreaming, so the contrast is connectedness/responsiveness, not presence of E. |
| Casey et al. 2022, BJA | [A]+[FT] | dexmedetomidine 20, propofol 6, sleep 15; 330 / 88 / 188 usable awakenings [FT] | Disconnected consciousness vs unconsciousness (report of no experience) in the preceding 20 s | Pre-registered primary measure, occipital delta power: AUC **0.605** (dexmedetomidine), **0.512** (sleep/propofol, i.e. chance). | Darracq et al. 2018, Sci Rep | [A] | TMS-EEG across sleep and anaesthesia (n not in abstract) | Disconnected-conscious (REM, ketamine) vs unconscious (propofol, NREM) vs wake | Evoked alpha reduced in disconnected consciousness; evoked low-gamma reduced in unconscious states; "confirmed" with NREM serial awakenings. Partly across-state. |

| Aamodt et al. 2021, 2022 | [A] | sleep, serial awakenings | Dream vs no dream within NREM2 | Lempel-Ziv complexity fell with sleep depth but **did not differ** between dream and no-dream; 2022 failed to reproduce their own 2021 content correlation. |
| Bajwa et al. 2025 | above | 20 | propofol, experience vs none | PCIst and LZc: **no difference**. |

Summary of what exists, without judgement: across-drug evidence (one cohort of 15-18,
analysed three ways) shows global complexity and slope following report. Within-drug
evidence at matched unresponsiveness comprises one null for PCI/LZc under propofol (5
no-experience reports), one weak-to-chance result for a pre-registered spectral measure
(Casey), nulls for LZc within NREM2, and positive within-state findings for regional
low-frequency power (Siclari, Nieminen) in sleep. The regional findings point at posterior
cortex (Siclari), cingulate (Casey), and thalamus plus cingulate plus angular gyrus
(Scheinin), which are not the same regions and are not a global integration measure.

## 3. Brainstem sufficiency and necessity (prediction B.6; N.6; T)

### 3a. Mesopontine tegmental anaesthesia area (MPTA), rat. All endpoints are R (righting, atonia, pinch response) plus EEG synchronisation.

| Study | Status | n | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Devor & Zalkind 2001, Pain | [A] | rats (n not in abstract) | Bilateral microinjection of "minute quantities" of pentobarbital at one mesopontine site gave loss of righting, atonia, no response to noxious stimuli, slow-wave EEG. Authors: "presumably, loss of consciousness". |
| Sukhotinsky et al. 2007, Eur J Neurosci | [A] | rats | Other GABA-A agonists effective; attenuated by local bicuculline; tracing shows projections to intralaminar thalamus, basal forebrain, zona incerta, frontal cortex, spinal cord. |
| Abulafia et al. 2009, J Neurosci | [A] | rats | Cortical Fos suppressed "throughout the cortex" by either MPTA or systemic route; subcortical patterns differed between routes. |
| Minert & Devor 2016, Exp Neurol | [A] | rats | Cell-selective MPTA lesion did not cause coma; raised systemic pentobarbital dose "in a manner proportional to the extent of the lesion"; off-target and sham lesions ineffective. **Exact percentage not verified** (abstract gives none). A 2023 review by the same authors says the dose "had to be increased to nearly lethal levels" before loss of righting [FTx]. |
| Minert, Yatziv, Devor 2017, J Neurosci | [A]+[FT] | 53 rats, 91 muscimol microinjections; pentobarbital 7 trials in 6 rats; propofol 12 trials in 9 rats | Exposure of **≤1,900 neurons** sufficient to sustain the state; **0.5 mm off-target ineffective**. Design measured prolongation of an already-induced anaesthetic state ("bonus" minutes), with return of pacing movements as the emergence marker. Two sites (Harvard/BIDMC and Hebrew University) but the same investigators. |
| Minert, Baron, Devor 2020, Anesthesiology | [A] | 5-7 lesioned vs 18-21 control rats per drug | Dose for deepest score rose to **147 ± 50%** of baseline for etomidate (P=0.0007) and **136 ± 58%** for propofol (P=0.010). **No change for alfaxalone (94%), ketamine (90%), medetomidine (112%).** Authors: MPTA is the target of "these, but not necessarily all, GABAergic anesthetics". |
| Lanir-Azaria et al. 2018, Behav Brain Res | [A] | rats | MPTA lesions increased wake time; did not abolish sleep. |
| Baron et al. 2022, Exp Neurol; Baron et al. 2024, Pain | [A] | rats | Chemogenetic **excitation** of MPTA "effector" neurons gives immobility and EEG change; pentobarbital and propofol effective at concentrations "present in the brain after systemic dosing"; target proposed as δ-subunit GABA-A receptors; benzodiazepines and zolpidem ineffective there. |
| Avigdor et al. 2021 (cited in Baron & Devor 2023) | [FTx] | rats | EEG after MPTA microinjection alternates NREM-like and **REM-like (wake-like)** epochs, "differs markedly" from the uniform delta of systemic anaesthesia. |
| Yatziv et al. 2021, Exp Brain Res | [A] | mice | Translation to mouse "has been challenging"; "tentatively located" the homologue using 20 nL muscimol. |

**Independent replication.**
- Voss, Young, Barnard, Sleigh 2005, Anaesth Intensive Care [A], 15 rats, pilot:
  thiopentone into MPTA produced an "anaesthesia-like" state (atonia, loss of righting) in
  **20%** of animals; **propofol produced it in none** (antinociception only). Devor's
  group acknowledge this failure and "have no obvious explanation" [FTx].
- Namjoshi et al. 2009, J Neurophysiol: cited by Devor as replication [FTx]; it concerns
  pentobarbital in MPTA suppressing spinothalamic neurons. Not read; includes a Devor
  co-author on related work (Soja). Independence unverified.
- I found no independent replication of the lesion result, no test with volatile agents by
  microdelivery, and no primate or human data. The scenario's "single unreplicated study"
  cap does not strictly apply (many papers) but all positive lesion and hot-spot data come
  from one laboratory.

Limits stated by the authors themselves: lesioning or silencing the MPTA (ibotenic acid,
lidocaine, TTX) does not cause unconsciousness; anaesthesia is still reachable at higher
dose without it; ketamine, neurosteroid and α2-agonist sensitivity are unaffected; the
effect needs excitation of effector neurons via disinhibition [FTx].

### 3b. Hypothalamic "anaesthesia-activated neurons" and the sleep-circuit hijack hypothesis

For:
- Moore et al. 2012, Curr Biol [A], mice: isoflurane and halothane depolarise
  sleep-active VLPO neurons directly; VLPO lesion gives "acute resistance" to isoflurane
  (righting).
- Jiang-Xie et al. 2019, Neuron [A], mice: supraoptic-region neuroendocrine cells activated
  by multiple anaesthetic classes; activating them promotes slow-wave sleep and prolongs
  anaesthesia; ablating or inhibiting them shortens anaesthesia duration and reduces sleep.
  Single lab; I found no independent replication.
- MPTA lesions also reduce sleep (Lanir-Azaria 2018).

Against:
- Eikermann et al. 2011, Brain Res [A], rats, VLPO ablated in 25, sham 8: VLPO "not
  required" for isoflurane anaesthesia; lesioned animals were **more** sensitive, tracking
  accumulated sleep loss (r≈0.5-0.6).
- Vanini et al. 2020, Curr Biol [A], mice: chemogenetic activation of preoptic GABAergic
  or glutamatergic neurons changed sleep-wake architecture but did **not** alter isoflurane
  induction or recovery time. Authors: correlative overlap "might not necessarily have
  strong causal significance".
- Moore (acute resistance) and Eikermann (chronic hypersensitivity) differ in lesion
  timing; the two are usually reconciled that way, which leaves the necessity claim
  unsettled.

All endpoints: R (righting) and EEG.

What was restored in every animal study: R. In macaques, cortical integration measures
came back together with arousal (2 animals; one-fifth of stimulation blocks). In rats (Pal
2020) wake-like behaviour came back without cortical gamma connectivity. In humans,
physostigmine restored command-following at fixed propofol with thalamic and precuneus
flow increases; experience reports during the unresponsive interval were not the endpoint.
No study restored arousal and then tested for E with a report-validated marker such as PCI.
No study I found restored cortical integration without arousal and asked about E.

Sites that reversed: brainstem (VTA, parabrachial), thalamus (central medial, central
lateral, centromedian), basal forebrain, and **prefrontal cortex**. Reversal is therefore
not specific to brainstem nuclei.

### 3d. Which arousal relay is necessary? (bears on T)

- Fuller et al. 2011, J Comp Neurol [A], rats: "extensive thalamic lesions had little
  effect" on EEG or behavioural wakefulness; basal forebrain lesions or
  parabrachial-precoeruleus lesions gave unresponsiveness with monotonous <1 Hz EEG.
- Hindman et al. 2018, Ann Neurol [A], human thalamic strokes: "All patients with severely
  impaired arousal (coma, stupor) had lesion extension into the midbrain and/or pontine
  tegmentum, whereas purely thalamic lesions did not severely impair arousal."
- Against that: thalamic stimulation or nicotine reverses anaesthesia (Alkire 2007,
  Redinbaugh 2020, Bastos 2021), and Scheinin 2021 and Xie 2011 find the thalamus among the
  state-tracking regions in humans.
- These measure arousal/R. A thalamus not needed for wakefulness is a different claim from
  a thalamus not needed for E.

## 4. Cortex- or thalamus-restricted delivery and dissociations (N.6, D, T)

### 4a. Layer-5 dendritic decoupling (D)

Suzuki & Larkum 2020, Cell [A]+[S], mice (n not verified): optogenetic depolarisation of
distal apical dendrites of layer-5 pyramidal cells drove somatic spiking in awake animals
and was "blocked by anesthesia" under three agents (isoflurane, urethane,
ketamine/xylazine [S]). Blocking metabotropic glutamate and cholinergic receptors, or
inactivating higher-order thalamus, "had the same effect".
- Measures: a cellular coupling variable against anaesthetised/awake state. No R, M or E
  endpoint was manipulated through the dendrite; the causal direction from decoupling to
  loss of responsiveness was not tested in this paper.
- Ketamine was given with xylazine (an α2 agonist), so it is not a test of ketamine alone,
  the one agent with high dream-report rates.
- Thalamic dependence and cholinergic dependence mean the finding is compatible with an
  upstream arousal cause (B, T) as well as with a cortical endpoint (N, D); the abstract
  itself links it to both cortico-cortical and thalamo-cortical loops.
- Replication: single lab for the optogenetic coupling result. Consistent correlational
  data: Redinbaugh 2020 (deep layers most state-sensitive, 2 macaques); Hönigsperger et al.
  2022 preprint [A] (mice, deep-layer firing and connectivity fall with PCI under
  sevoflurane). I did not find an independent replication of the coupling experiment, nor
  any human data. Full text not read; flagged.

### 4b. Regional delivery

- Devor group's whole-brain microinjection survey: pentobarbital in a 3-D grid across rat
  brain; the MPTA was the only site producing the state [FTx]. Cortical and thalamic sites
  therefore did not, at those volumes. One lab; small volumes into large structures are a
  weak test of a distributed-cortex hypothesis (not their wording; a design limitation).
- Antognini & Schwartz 1993, Anesthesiology [A], 6 goats: isoflurane delivered
  preferentially to the brain required **2.9%** vs 1.2% whole-body to prevent movement to
  pain. Rampil et al. 1993 [A], rats, 7 decerebrated vs 7 controls: MAC unchanged by
  precollicular decerebration (1.26% vs 1.30%). Both show that **immobility (R) is largely
  spinal** and says nothing about E.
- Wada test (intracarotid amobarbital, one hemisphere): the other hemisphere stays awake
  and reporting [S, general knowledge]. Unilateral only; no bilateral cortical delivery
  with brainstem spared was found.
- Alemà et al. 1966, J Neurosurg, "Functional inactivation of the human brain stem related
  to the level of consciousness. Intravertebral injection of barbiturate": **exists (PMID
  5906509) but has no abstract and I could not read it.** This is the nearest thing to a
  direct human test of brainstem-restricted delivery. Its result is unverified here. A
  related cat study (Magni, Moruzzi, Rossi, Zanchetti 1959) reported EEG arousal after
  barbiturate inactivation of the *lower* brainstem [S].
- Pal 2018 / Huels 2021 (above): prefrontal cortex manipulation alone shifts anaesthetic
  state in rats (R).

### 4c. Arousal without cortex, cortex without arousal

- Vegetative state: sleep-wake cycles and eye opening (brainstem arousal intact), no
  behavioural sign of E, low PCI in 34/43 and sleep-like cortical OFF-periods (Casarotto
  2016; Rosanova 2018). 9/43 had high PCI. Absence of report is the definition of the
  condition, so E is unknown in both subgroups.
- Demertzi et al. 2019, Sci Adv [A]: fMRI coordination pattern separated minimally conscious
  from unresponsive patients; anaesthesia shifted patterns toward the unresponsive profile.
- Coma from brainstem lesions: small tegmental lesions cause coma, large cortical lesions
  rarely do (Fischer et al. 2016, cited in Baron & Devor 2023 [FTx]; Hindman 2018 [A]).
  Coma is loss of arousal, R, M and presumably E together; it does not separate them.
- Congenital absence of cortex: Shewmon et al. 1999 [A], 4 children aged 5-17 with "total
  or near-total absence of cerebral cortex" showing discriminative responding, social
  interaction, affect, associative learning. Merker 2007 [A], review plus observation.
  These are behavioural (R) inferences to E, without report.
- Hydranencephaly and anaesthesia: one case report (BMC Anesthesiol 2020 [FT]): infant with
  brainstem and thalamus, cortex replaced by fluid; given standard sevoflurane (8%
  induction, 2% maintenance), propofol, dexmedetomidine across three procedures; no EEG, no
  dose-requirement comparison. It shows such children are anaesthetised with ordinary
  agents; it does not quantify sensitivity. I found no systematic data, and nothing on
  anencephaly.
- Decorticate/decerebrate animals: righting and MAC persist (Rampil 1993; Vincent 2021).

## 5. Direct human evidence on the B.5 / N.5 fork

Asked for: (i) brainstem arousal suppressed and experience absent regardless of cortical
complexity; (ii) cortical complexity high with no experience; (iii) cortical complexity low
with experience.

- (i) **Not found.** No human study independently measures brainstem arousal-system
  activity alongside cortical complexity and report. Dexmedetomidine acts at the locus
  coeruleus and yields the highest experience-report rate (90% of interviews, Radek 2018);
  it is arousable sedation, so "suppressed" is arguable.
- (ii) Candidates, none decisive: high-PCI vegetative patients (9/43; E unknown). Bajwa
  2025: no-experience awakenings had numerically higher PCIst (27.3) than experience
  awakenings (21.7), not significant, n=5. Pal 2020: complexity rose with parietal carbachol
  without behaviour (rat, R only). REM-like EEG during MPTA-induced state (rat, Avigdor
  2021): activated cortex with no righting; E unknown.
- (iii) Candidates: 74-90% experience reports from propofol/dexmedetomidine unresponsiveness
  (Radek 2018) and ~80% (Scheinin 2021) at doses where EEG complexity and PCI are reduced
  relative to wake (Bajwa: PCIst 21.7 vs 53.6 awake, with experience reported). NREM dream
  reports at N3 83% (Scheinin 2021 [FT]). Command-following with frontal alpha-delta
  (Gaskell 2017). These involve a reduced, not floor-level, measure; the PCI threshold used
  to classify "unconscious" in Casarotto 2016 was not applied in Bajwa.
- Bearing on "content vs presence": Scheinin 2021 reports widespread cortical suppression
  as a drug effect and thalamus/cingulate/angular change as the state effect. Their state
  contrast is connected-and-responsive vs disconnected-and-unresponsive, with experience
  present on both sides in most subjects. Casey 2022 is the only anaesthesia study with an
  explicit "no experience" class and neural contrast; its validated discrimination was AUC
  0.62.

## 6. Evidence sorted by prediction (description only)

**N.5** (a network measure separates reported experience from no-report at matched
unresponsiveness)
- Consistent with: Sarasso 2015 / Colombo 2019 / Maschke 2023 (one cohort, across drugs);
  Nieminen 2016 and Siclari 2017 (within NREM sleep); Casey 2022 secondary signatures
  (validation AUC 0.62-0.74); Darracq 2018.
- Against or null: Bajwa 2025 (PCIst and LZc null within propofol, low power); Aamodt
  2021/2022 (LZc null within NREM2); Casey 2022 primary measure at chance outside
  dexmedetomidine; Lee 2013 (feedback connectivity falls under ketamine too, where dreams
  are commonly reported; reports not collected); Lendner 2020 (slope separates REM from
  wake); Gaskell 2017 (frontal EEG does not separate command-followers).
- Measure-switching note: the supporting within-state results use regional low-frequency
  power or evoked-response shape, not the global complexity index used across drugs.

**B.5** (with brainstem arousal engaged, experience absent regardless of cortical
complexity; cortical measures index content and connectedness only)
- Consistent with: Farnes 2020 (spontaneous diversity tracks content, PCI unchanged);
  Scheinin 2021 (cortical surface changes are drug effects; state tracks thalamus and
  midline); Casey 2022 (broad cortical change = disconnection; presence/absence = focal
  midline); Pal 2020 (behavioural state dissociates from cortical connectivity and
  complexity, rat, R only); Bajwa 2025 null.
- Against: the across-drug ketamine result (complexity follows report); Scheinin and Casey
  locate the state signal in thalamus and cingulate cortex, not brainstem (PET resolution
  for small brainstem nuclei is limited; neither paper reports a brainstem state effect);
  high experience rates under dexmedetomidine and propofol with arousal systems
  pharmacologically depressed; no direct human test of the "regardless of cortical
  complexity" clause exists.

**N.6** (restoring arousal without cortico-thalamic integration does not restore experience;
cortex/thalamus-restricted delivery can abolish it)
- Consistent with: Redinbaugh 2020 and Bastos 2021 (arousal and cortical integration return
  together under thalamic stimulation; R only); Kenny 2016 (EEG activation without
  righting); Xie 2011 (human reversal tracks thalamus and precuneus).
- Against: Pal 2020 (wake-like behaviour without restored gamma connectivity; R only);
  Devor survey (no cortical or thalamic microinjection site produced the state, one lab);
  Fuller 2011 and Hindman 2018 (thalamic damage alone does not abolish arousal).
- Untested: experience after arousal restoration; bilateral cortex-restricted delivery.

**B.6** (microdelivery to a brainstem site gives the full state at doses ineffective
elsewhere; lesion markedly raises systemic dose; arousal-nucleus stimulation reverses with
drug present)
- Consistent with: MPTA microinjection (≤1,900 neurons, 0.5 mm specificity); lesion raises
  etomidate/propofol dose to 147% / 136% (and pentobarbital, magnitude unverified); VTA,
  parabrachial, methylphenidate reversal.
- Against or limiting: lesion effect is 36-47% for the two verified agents and absent for
  ketamine, alfaxalone, medetomidine; volatile agents untested at MPTA; Voss 2005
  independent pilot: 20% with thiopentone, 0% with propofol; mouse translation
  "challenging"; MPTA-induced EEG is REM/NREM-alternating, unlike systemic anaesthesia;
  reversal also works from thalamus, basal forebrain and prefrontal cortex; sleep-nucleus
  manipulations give mixed results (Vanini 2020, Eikermann 2011 vs Moore 2012, Jiang-Xie
  2019); Luo 2018 parabrachial activation did not alter induction.
- All R. No report-validated marker was used in any B.6 study.

**T** (thalamic switch)
- For: Alkire 2007; Redinbaugh 2020; Bastos 2021; Luppi 2026; Xie 2011; Scheinin 2021;
  Suzuki 2020 (higher-order thalamic inactivation decouples layer-5 dendrites).
- Against: Fuller 2011; Hindman 2018; Devor survey (no thalamic microinjection site).

**D** (layer-5 dendritic decoupling)
- For: Suzuki & Larkum 2020 (three agents); laminar correlates in macaque and mouse.
- Limits: one lab; mice; no behavioural or report endpoint tied to the dendritic variable;
  ketamine tested only with xylazine; depends on thalamic and cholinergic input.
