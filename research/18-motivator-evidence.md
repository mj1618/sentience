# 18 — Evidence for "Is felt valence the motivator?" (scenario 18)

Evidence only; no scoring of H/W/K. Tags: **[V-full]** methods/results read in full text; **[V-abs]** abstract only; **[R]** recalled, not checked this round. "EPMC" = europepmc.org. Behaviour type is labelled: *invigoration* (how hard/fast a pre-instructed response is made), *habitual*, or *flexible choice* (selecting between actions on the basis of outcome).

## Row 1 — Subliminal reward and effort

- **Pessiglione et al. 2007, Science** (PMC2631941) **[V-full, main text; n is in the supplement: 18, R]**. Manipulated: masked coin image (£1 vs 1p) shown 17, 50 or 100 ms before each trial. Measured: hand-grip force (payoff proportional to force), skin conductance, fMRI. Behaviour = *invigoration* of one fixed, instructed response; no choice between actions. Result: force higher for £1 at 100, 50 and 17 ms (all p<0.01); skin conductance and ventral pallidum differed at 100 and 50 ms, not 17 ms. Awareness: separate in-scanner forced choice afterwards ("seen/guess £1/1p"); analysis "restricted to all situations where subjects guess at chance level", force effect still p<0.01. Authors themselves attribute the subcortical locus to "the simple and repetitive nature of the task, rendering strategic control unnecessary". No effect size beyond p-values in the main text. Caveat (Bijleveld 2014, below): only the 17 ms condition was at chance for identity.
- **Bijleveld et al. 2014, Hum Brain Mapp** (PMC4265283) **[V-full]**. n=23. Masked Euro vs cent (17 ms vs 300 ms) before a mental-rotation trial; pay scaled with speed. Behaviour = accuracy/speed on an instructed task (*invigoration/effort*, not choice). Reward main effect on accuracy F(1,22)=6.3, p=.020, concentrated on hardest (180°) trials; no Reward×Presentation interaction (F<1). Awareness: post-task 2AFC identification, 17 ms accuracy 50.7% (p=.75), n=22; no correlation between identification and effect. fMRI: ventral striatum and motor areas engaged by supraliminal but **not** subliminal coins.
- **Bijleveld, Custers & Aarts 2010, Cognition** (PMID 20089247) **[V-abs]**. Subliminal high reward made people faster with equal accuracy; only supraliminal reward changed the speed–accuracy *strategy*. 2012 JEP:G (PMID 22468672) **[V-abs]**: unconscious integration of reward and effort demand "rudimentary"; conscious reward allowed strategic effort conservation.
- **Berkovitch et al. 2022, Schizophr Bull** (PMC9434445) **[V-full]**. Conceptual replication of the grip task; 31 patients, 32 controls; 33 ms masked coins. First block was unconscious-only (to exclude carry-over of a consciously formed mapping): force higher for big incentive, T(62)=1.7, p=.048 **one-tailed**. Awareness: 128-trial 2AFC post-test, group d′=0.11, **significantly above zero** (p=.048); regression intercept at d′=0 still significant one-tailed (p=.024); slope with d′ p=.070.
- **Hinze et al. 2021, J Neurophysiol** (PMC8715050) **[V-full, partial]**. n=38; masked reward before a repeated-saccade task. Reward raised saccade frequency/velocity, strongest when visible; at 17 ms identification was "not above chance" but trending (p=.07); in the subgroup individually at chance only saccade frequency was affected.
- **Garofalo et al. 2020, Sci Rep** (PMC7368056) **[V-abs]**. Pavlovian-to-instrumental transfer: subliminal cues produced *general* transfer only; *outcome-specific* transfer (choice toward the particular reward) required supraliminal cues.
- **Critiques.** Skora 2021 (below) summarises the standard objection: a non-significant separate awareness test does not show absence of awareness (Vadillo et al. 2016; Shanks 2017, PMC5486877 **[V-full, skimmed]**; Rothkirch, Shanks & Hesselmann 2022 **[V-abs]**: post hoc selection of "unaware" trials/participants produces regression-to-the-mean artefacts). Pessiglione 2007's "restricted to chance-level situations" analysis is a post hoc selection of this type. Shanks 2026 BBS commentary **[V-abs]**: "a century of research has failed to build a compelling body of replicable evidence that behavior can be controlled unconsciously". No preregistered direct replication of the 2007 grip study was found.

## Row 2 — Subliminal instrumental learning

- **Pessiglione et al. 2008, Neuron** (PMC2572733) **[V-full]**. n=20 analysed (2 more scanned and **excluded** because they could describe cue fragments, were above chance, and "won unusually large amounts"). Masked abstract cues (33 or 50 ms, set per person); Go/NoGo choice where the "risky" response paid +£1/−£1/£0 depending on the unseen cue; 3 sessions × 120 trials. Behaviour = *choice* between two responses, learned by reinforcement (but a single binary press/withhold). Result: mean payoff £7.5 ± 1.8 (p<0.001, one-tailed); correct responding plateaued at 63% ± 5%; unmasked cues later rated in line with value. Awareness: same/different discrimination of two masked cues, before and after; group 48% ± 3%, d′=0.08 ± 0.20; correlation of awareness score with payoff r=0.26 (n.s.). Subjects were told to "follow their intuition"; authors note that with visible cues learning "would have been optimized in one trial or two".
- **Skora, Yeomans, Crombag & Scott 2021, Cognition** (preprint osf.io/6rq9d, marked "Accepted – Cognition") **[V-full]**. Exp 1 (masking; n=46 recruited, 37 analysed): individually set thresholds and a trial-by-trial awareness check (confident correct symmetry judgment = aware; 11.75% of trials excluded; 7 participants aware on >25% excluded). Unaware trials d′=0.02, B=0.10 (evidence for null); aware trials d′=0.72, B=420. Exp 2 (continuous flash suppression, delay conditioning, taste reward; n=39, unaware sample n=19): d′=−0.10, B=0.07; aware trials d′=0.99.
- **Skora et al. 2023, Cortex (registered replication)** (PMID 36621202) **[V-abs]**. Exp 1 direct replication reproduced apparent learning under the original awareness test but "could not establish that the full sample was unaware"; Exp 2 with trial-wise checks: evidence for absence. **Skora, Scott & Jocham 2024** **[V-abs]**: contingencies learned consciously were not acted on when cues were masked. **Höhmann, Jocham & Skora 2025** **[V-abs]** (preregistered, N=75): masked stimuli triggered neither goal-directed nor *habitual* responses after extensive training.
- **Jurchiș et al. 2026, Cortex registered report** **[V-abs]** (N=187/160): unconsciously learned grammar regularities supported adaptive Go/NoGo only when they yielded conscious judgments of predictive value.
- **Mertens & Engelhard 2020, Neurosci Biobehav Rev** (PMID 31747553) **[V-abs]**. k=41 studies; higher methodological quality predicted lower odds of concluding unaware fear conditioning; funnel asymmetry and p-curve indicate publication bias; "no convincing evidence".

## Row 3 — Sub-subjective drug doses

- **Lamb et al. 1991, JPET** (PMID 1762068) **[V-abs; full text paywalled]**. n=5 volunteers with IV heroin histories. Second-order schedule FR30(FR100:S): every 100 lever presses gave a brief light; the 30th completion (3,000 presses) gave an i.m. injection; one session per weekday, each dose for a week. Placebo did **not** maintain responding; 3.75 mg morphine maintained it in 4/5; 7.5/15/30 mg in 5/5. Subjective measures taken concurrently: drug liking, ARCI Morphine-Benzedrine Group scale, drug detection and identification. Lowest dose: reports not different from placebo; intermediate doses inconsistent; 30 mg produced liking and "dope" identifications. Not verified: session-by-session acquisition, whether subjects could say which weeks were active, response rates. Behaviour = sustained operant responding on one lever with a conditioned reinforcer every 100 presses; **no test of goal-directedness (devaluation, contingency change, or choice) reported in the abstract**.
- **Fischman 1989, NIDA Res Monogr** (PMID 2512494) **[R; no abstract]**: low-dose i.v. cocaine reported as chosen over saline without distinguishable subjective effects. Not verified.
- **Comer, Collins & Fischman 1997** (PMID 9832953) **[V-abs]**: n=5; heroin vs money ($10–40) choice on progressive ratio; self-administration shifted with the money alternative while subjective ratings did not — responding sensitive to alternatives (*flexible choice*), but at felt doses.
- **Hogarth 2020, Neuropsychopharmacology** (PMC7265389) **[V-full, partial]**: review; in human drug users 8 of 11 outcome-devaluation tests found drug seeking goal-directed, and the 3 "habit" results coincided with impaired explicit contingency knowledge. Concerns ordinary (felt) drug use, not sub-subjective doses.
- No replication of Lamb 1991 found.

## Row 4 — Pain without unpleasantness

- **Berthier, Starkstein & Leiguarda 1988, Ann Neurol** (PMID 3415199) **[V-abs]**. n=6 (5 ischaemic, 1 traumatic haematoma; left hemisphere 4, right 2); insula damaged in all 6. No primary sensory deficit. Lost: withdrawal and appropriate emotional response to painful stimuli over the whole body and to threatening gestures; 5/6 also failed to react to verbal menace; "appeared unconcerned" and "seemed unable to learn appropriate escape or protective responses". Associated: neglect, body-schema disorders. Recalled, unverified **[R]**: patients recognised the stimulus as pain; some smiled/laughed. Note that withdrawal (a reflex-level response) was lost too, and that responses to *visual* threat were lost.
- **Prefrontal lobotomy/cingulotomy for pain** — secondary source Gerrans 2024 (PMC10860504) **[V-full of the review; primary sources not read]**: patients said intensity was unchanged and were untroubled, yet "automatic responses to a noxious stimulus were 'intact and often exaggerated'". Foltz & White 1962 and morphine "hurts but doesn't bother me" **[R]**. No quantitative study of protective behaviour or injury after cingulotomy/morphine was found.
- **Rainville et al. 1997, Science** (PMID 9252330) **[V-abs]**; **Rainville et al. 1999, Pain** (PMID 10467921) **[V-abs]**: hypnotic suggestion altered unpleasantness ratings largely independent of intensity (hand in 45–47.5 °C water, 1 min); unpleasantness, not intensity, correlated with heart-rate increase. Behaviour (withdrawal, tolerance, avoidance) was **not** measured.

## Row 5 — Congenital insensitivity to pain (CIP)

- **Cox et al. 2006, Nature** (PMC7212082) **[V-full]**. SCN9A loss of function; 6 affected individuals in 3 families. All bit lips/tongue in first 4 years; all had frequent cuts and bruises; most had fractures or osteomyelitis found late. "Normal intelligence" per parents, teachers, physicians. Older individuals "realized what actions should elicit pain (including acting as if in pain after football tackles)". Index case did street theatre (knives through arms, walking on coals) and died before age 14 jumping off a roof. Authors: such patients "fail to learn pain-avoiding (severe risk-avoiding) behaviours".
- **Zhang & Haga 2014** (PMID 24953503) **[V-abs]**. CIPA (NTRK1), 91 Japanese patients: fractures 65%, Charcot joints 29%, bone/joint infection 24%; "most fractures occurred from 1 to 7 years of age" (i.e. fracture rate falls later; other complications no age trend). CIPA often includes intellectual disability, so it is a weaker test of "normal intelligence".
- **Mifsud et al. 2019** (PMC6699213) **[V-full, partial]**. Whole-population series, Malta: 9 adults (mean age 33.7), mental status listed "normal" in those checked; 127 limb operations and 7 major lower-limb amputations between them.
- **Learning from instruction.** Rodríguez-Blanque 2024 systematic review (PMC11204641) **[V-full, partial]**: management rests on "education and preventive measures"; no controlled data on how far instruction reduces injury. Nagasako et al. 2003, Pain **[not obtained; no abstract]**.
- **Other negative feelings.** McDermott et al. 2019, Neuron (PMC6424805) **[V-full, partial]**: SCN9A participant rated capsaicin pain and unpleasantness 0. Danziger et al. 2006 **[V-abs]**: CIP patients had normal semantic knowledge of others' pain but lower aversive responses to injury videos. Whether SCN9A patients have normal fear/sadness: not established here.

## Row 6 — Anhedonia vs apathy

- **Husain & Roiser 2018** (PMID 29946157) **[V-abs]**: frames both as disorders of effort-based decision-making for reward and suggests "some shared mechanisms". Full text not read.
- **Gard et al. 2007, Schizophr Res** (PMC1986826) **[V-full]**. Study 1: 15 patients, 12 controls, experience sampling 7×/day for 7 days: in-the-moment enjoyment not different; anticipated pleasure lower (p=.06 overall; p<.01 for goal-directed activities); patients in goal-directed activity 17% vs 35.7% of samples. Study 2 (t df=99): TEPS consummatory not different, anticipatory lower (p<.01); anticipatory, not consummatory, pleasure correlated with BAS drive. All self-report; all patients medicated.
- **Kring & Barch 2014** (PMC4020953) **[V-full, partial]**: review; in-the-moment "liking" relatively intact in schizophrenia by self-report and striatal response to received reward; deficits in anticipation, effort computation, goal maintenance.
- **Treadway et al. 2009** (PMC2720457) **[V-abs]**: n=61 students; trait anhedonia inversely related to choosing high-effort options. **Treadway et al. 2012** (PMC3730492) **[V-abs]**: MDD patients chose high-effort/high-reward options less. Here anhedonia and reduced effort **co-occur**.
- **Chong et al. 2015, Cortex** (PMC4533227) **[V-full]**. 26 Parkinson's patients (depression and apathy excluded) ON vs OFF dopaminergic medication, counterbalanced: accept/reject offers of apples for grip effort (*flexible choice*). ON raised effort indifference points (F(1,25)=25.9, p<.001); maximal force unchanged. **Felt pleasure was not measured.**
- **Le Heron et al. 2018, Brain** (PMC5917786) **[V-full]**. 39 Parkinson's patients, apathetic vs not (Lille scale), ON and OFF. Apathy: fewer acceptances specifically for low-reward offers; dopamine depletion: fewer for high-effort/high-reward offers; independent three-way interactions (p=.004, p=.003). Effort was not rated as more demanding by apathetic patients. **Hedonic experience not measured**; depression was not excluded.
- Human dopamine-suppression studies in which desire fell but pleasure ratings did not (Brauer & de Wit 1997; Leyton 2005) — see row 7.

## Row 7 — Wanting without liking

- **Leyton et al. 2005, Behav Neurosci** (PMID 16420164) **[V-abs]**. n=8 non-dependent cocaine users; catecholamine precursor depletion lowered cue- and cocaine-induced craving but **not** euphoria and **not** self-administration ("preliminary").
- **Evans et al. 2006, Ann Neurol** (PMID 16557571) **[V-abs]**. 8 Parkinson's patients with dopamine dysregulation syndrome vs 8 without; levodopa-induced ventral striatal dopamine release (raclopride PET) was greater in DDS and correlated with self-rated drug "wanting", not "liking". **Both wanting and liking here are felt, reported ratings.**
- **Giovannoni et al. 2000, JNNP** (PMC1736875) **[V-abs]**: 15 patients escalating dopaminergic drugs despite severe dyskinesias; tolerance to mood-elevating effects; negative affective withdrawal state on dose reduction. Hoarding/planned procurement **[R]**.
- **Lambert et al. 2006, Addiction** (PMID 16669905) **[V-abs]**. n=202, retrospective report of first cocaine use: both liking and wanting predicted later dependence; the subgroup pre-exposed to both stimulant treatment and smoking reported lowest liking and highest wanting.
- **Hobbs et al. 2005** (PMID 15517194) **[V-abs]**: alcohol priming raised consumption without raising liking; adulterating drinks lowered liking without lowering consumption.
- **Portenoy et al. 1986, Pain** (PMID 3492699) **[V-abs]**. One woman, thalamic VPL electrode; "compulsive self-stimulation associated with erotic sensations". The abstract therefore reports a **felt, positive sensation**; the oft-repeated detail that it never reached satisfaction and that she neglected hygiene/family is **[R]**.
- **Heath B-19 / NAc DBS** via Berridge & Kringelbach 2015 (PMC4425246) **[V-full of review passage]**: reviewers argue B-19 was never quoted as calling stimulation pleasurable; Schlaepfer 2008 NAc DBS patients could not tell whether stimulation was on, yet "spontaneously" formed plans (visit Cologne Cathedral, done next day; take up bowling again) — i.e. a reported desire for a specific novel goal.
- **Berridge & Robinson 2016** (PMC5171207) **[V-full, partial]**: the authors distinguish incentive-salience 'wanting' (cue-triggered urges) from "cognitive wanting … with a declarative goal" and say the former is "less connected to cognitive goals". Leyton 2022 reviews **[V-abs titles only]** question how far sensitisation is shown in humans.

## Row 8 — vmPFC patients

- **Bechara et al. 1997, Science** (PMID 9036851) **[V-abs; n = 10 controls, 6 patients R]**. Controls chose advantageously and showed anticipatory skin conductance before stating the strategy; patients "continued to choose disadvantageously even after they knew the correct strategy" and never developed anticipatory SCRs, "although some eventually realized which choices were risky".
- **Maia & McClelland 2004, PNAS** (PMC528759) **[V-full]**. 20 healthy students per condition (**no patients**). A structured questionnaire showed that when participants chose advantageously they almost always could report which decks were best and why; authors note the original pre-hunch result "did not reach statistical significance". Bears on the "before knowing" claim in controls, not on patients' know-but-don't-act. Reply: Bechara et al. 2005 **[V-abs]**.
- **Bechara, Tranel & Damasio 2000, Brain** (PMID 11050020) **[V-abs]**: patients' SCRs *after receiving* reward/punishment were not different from controls; deficit described as insensitivity to future consequences.
- **Fellows & Farah 2005, Cereb Cortex** (PMID 15217900) **[V-abs]**: vmPFC deficit on the task "largely explained by" a reversal-learning deficit; dorsolateral patients also impaired.
- **Eslinger & Damasio 1985 (EVR)** (PMID 4069365) **[V-abs]**: after bilateral orbital/lower mesial ablation, "superior" measured intelligence yet unable to meet personal and professional responsibilities, stable over 8 years. Blunted-feeling measures (absent SCR to emotional pictures; self-reported flattened emotion) **[R]**.

## Row 9 — Auto-activation deficit

- **Laplane & Dubois 2001, Mov Disord** (PMID 11746609) **[no abstract; R]**: bilateral pallidal/striatal lesions; inertia reversible by external prompting; patients report mental emptiness and blunted affect, typically without suffering.
- **Levy & Dubois 2006, Cereb Cortex** (PMID 16207933) **[V-abs]**: three apathy mechanisms — emotional-affective, cognitive, auto-activation; the last is "inability to self-activate thoughts or self-initiate actions contrasting with a relatively spared ability to generate externally driven behavior".
- **Schmidt et al. 2008, Brain** (PMID 18344560) **[V-abs]**. 13 AAD patients vs 13 unmedicated Parkinson's. Grip force to *instruction*: normal. **Skin conductance response to monetary incentives: not different from the comparison group.** Grip force failed to scale with money. No healthy control group in the abstract; felt pleasure not reported there.
- **Leu-Semenescu et al. 2013, Brain** (PMID 24026624) **[V-abs]**: 13 AAD patients; waking "mental blank" complete in 6, partial in 1; dream reports rare, short, without emotional content.
- **Riveros et al. 2018** (PMC6352737) **[V-full]**: single case; asked about feelings, "declared to have none"; low anxiety, flat affect; acted only on stimulation; drive and emotion recovered together over 3 years while executive deficits persisted.
- **Adam et al. 2013, Cortex** (PMC3639369) **[V-full]**: single case, bilateral pallidal lesions; no speeding to rewarded targets; not depressed, "little concern"; reward sensitivity and everyday initiative (got a job) restored by levodopa/ropinirole. Pleasure not measured.
