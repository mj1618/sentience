# 18 — Evidence for "Is felt valence the motivator?" (scenario 18)

Evidence only; no scoring of H/W/K. Tags: **[V-full]** methods/results read in full text ("partial" = relevant sections only); **[V-abs]** abstract only; **[R]** recalled, unchecked. PMC/PMID ids resolve at europepmc.org. Behaviour labels: *invigoration* (vigour of a pre-instructed response), *habitual*, *flexible choice*.

## Row 1 — Subliminal reward and effort

- **Pessiglione et al. 2007, Science** (PMC2631941) **[V-full, main text; n is in the supplement: 18, R]**. Manipulated: masked coin image (£1 vs 1p) shown 17, 50 or 100 ms before each trial. Measured: hand-grip force (payoff proportional to force), skin conductance, fMRI. Behaviour = *invigoration* of one fixed, instructed response; no choice between actions. Result: force higher for £1 at 100, 50 and 17 ms (all p<0.01); skin conductance and ventral pallidum differed at 100 and 50 ms, not 17 ms. Awareness: separate in-scanner forced choice afterwards ("seen/guess £1/1p"); analysis "restricted to all situations where subjects guess at chance level", force effect still p<0.01. Authors attribute the subcortical locus to "the simple and repetitive nature of the task, rendering strategic control unnecessary". No effect sizes in main text. Bijleveld 2014 states only the 17 ms condition was at chance for identity.
- **Bijleveld et al. 2014, Hum Brain Mapp** (PMC4265283) **[V-full]**. n=23. Masked Euro vs cent (17 ms vs 300 ms) before a mental-rotation trial; pay scaled with speed. Behaviour = accuracy/speed on an instructed task (*invigoration*, not choice). Reward main effect on accuracy F(1,22)=6.3, p=.020, concentrated on hardest (180°) trials; no Reward×Presentation interaction (F<1). Awareness: post-task 2AFC identification, 17 ms accuracy 50.7% (p=.75), n=22; no correlation between identification and effect. fMRI: ventral striatum and motor areas engaged by supraliminal but **not** subliminal coins.
- **Bijleveld, Custers & Aarts 2010, Cognition** (PMID 20089247) and **2012, JEP:G** (PMID 22468672) **[V-abs]**. Subliminal high reward made people faster at equal accuracy; only supraliminal reward changed speed–accuracy *strategy* or allowed strategic effort conservation.
- **Berkovitch et al. 2022, Schizophr Bull** (PMC9434445) **[V-full]**. Conceptual replication of the grip task; 31 patients, 32 controls; 33 ms masked coins. First block was unconscious-only (to exclude carry-over of a consciously formed mapping): force higher for big incentive, T(62)=1.7, p=.048 **one-tailed**. Awareness: 128-trial 2AFC post-test, group d′=0.11, **significantly above zero** (p=.048); regression intercept at d′=0 still significant one-tailed (p=.024); slope with d′ p=.070.
- **Hinze et al. 2021** (PMC8715050) **[V-full, partial]**. Masked reward raised saccade frequency in a repeated-saccade task; 17 ms identification trended above chance (p=.07).
- **Garofalo et al. 2020, Sci Rep** (PMC7368056) **[V-abs]**. Pavlovian-to-instrumental transfer: subliminal cues produced *general* transfer only; *outcome-specific* transfer (choice toward the particular reward) required supraliminal cues.
- **Critiques.** A non-significant separate awareness test does not show absence of awareness, and post hoc selection of "unaware" trials/participants produces regression-to-the-mean artefacts (Shanks 2017, PMC5486877 **[V-full, skimmed]**; Rothkirch, Shanks & Hesselmann 2022 **[V-abs]**). Pessiglione 2007's "restricted to chance-level situations" analysis is a selection of this type. No preregistered direct replication of the 2007 grip study was found.

## Row 2 — Subliminal instrumental learning

- **Pessiglione et al. 2008, Neuron** (PMC2572733) **[V-full]**. n=20 analysed (2 more scanned and **excluded** because they could describe cue fragments, were above chance, and "won unusually large amounts"). Masked abstract cues (33 or 50 ms, set per person); Go/NoGo choice where the "risky" response paid +£1/−£1/£0 depending on the unseen cue; 3 sessions × 120 trials. Behaviour = learned *choice* (binary press/withhold). Result: mean payoff £7.5 ± 1.8 (p<0.001, one-tailed); correct responding plateaued at 63% ± 5%; unmasked cues later rated in line with value. Awareness: same/different discrimination of two masked cues, before and after; group 48% ± 3%, d′=0.08 ± 0.20; correlation of awareness score with payoff r=0.26 (n.s.). Subjects were told to "follow their intuition".
- **Skora, Yeomans, Crombag & Scott 2021, Cognition** (preprint osf.io/6rq9d, marked "Accepted – Cognition") **[V-full]**. Exp 1 (masking; n=46 recruited, 37 analysed): individually set thresholds and a trial-by-trial awareness check (confident correct symmetry judgment = aware; 11.75% of trials excluded; 7 participants aware on >25% excluded). Unaware trials d′=0.02, B=0.10 (evidence for null); aware trials d′=0.72, B=420. Exp 2 (continuous flash suppression, delay conditioning, taste reward; n=39, unaware sample n=19): d′=−0.10, B=0.07; aware trials d′=0.99.
- **Skora et al. 2023, Cortex (registered replication)** (PMID 36621202) **[V-abs]**. Direct replication reproduced apparent learning but "could not establish that the full sample was unaware"; with trial-wise checks, evidence for absence. **Skora, Scott & Jocham 2024** **[V-abs]**: contingencies learned consciously were not acted on when cues were masked. **Höhmann, Jocham & Skora 2025** **[V-abs]** (preregistered, N=75): masked stimuli triggered neither goal-directed nor *habitual* responses after extensive training.
- **Jurchiș et al. 2026, Cortex registered report** **[V-abs]** (N=187/160): unconsciously learned grammar regularities supported adaptive Go/NoGo only when they yielded conscious judgments of predictive value.
- **Mertens & Engelhard 2020, Neurosci Biobehav Rev** (PMID 31747553) **[V-abs]**. k=41 studies; higher methodological quality predicted lower odds of concluding unaware fear conditioning; funnel asymmetry and p-curve indicate publication bias; "no convincing evidence".

## Row 3 — Sub-subjective drug doses

- **Lamb et al. 1991, JPET** (PMID 1762068) **[V-abs; full text paywalled]**. n=5 volunteers with IV heroin histories. Second-order schedule FR30(FR100:S): every 100 lever presses gave a brief light; the 30th completion (3,000 presses) gave an i.m. injection; one session per weekday, each dose for a week. Placebo did **not** maintain responding; 3.75 mg morphine maintained it in 4/5; 7.5/15/30 mg in 5/5. Subjective measures taken concurrently: drug liking, ARCI Morphine-Benzedrine Group scale, drug detection and identification. Lowest dose: reports not different from placebo; intermediate doses inconsistent; 30 mg produced liking and "dope" identifications. Not verified: acquisition course, response rates. Behaviour = sustained operant responding on one lever with a conditioned reinforcer every 100 presses; **no test of goal-directedness (devaluation, contingency change, or choice) reported in the abstract**.
- **Fischman 1989, NIDA Res Monogr** (PMID 2512494) **[R; no abstract]**: low-dose i.v. cocaine reported as chosen over saline without distinguishable subjective effects. Not verified.
- **Comer, Collins & Fischman 1997** (PMID 9832953) **[V-abs]**: n=5; heroin vs money ($10–40) choice on progressive ratio; self-administration shifted with the money alternative while subjective ratings did not — responding sensitive to alternatives (*flexible choice*), but at felt doses.
- **Hogarth 2020** (PMC7265389) **[V-full, partial]**: review; 8 of 11 outcome-devaluation tests in human drug users found drug seeking goal-directed. Concerns ordinary (felt) drug use, not sub-subjective doses.
- No replication of Lamb 1991 found.

## Row 4 — Pain without unpleasantness

- **Berthier, Starkstein & Leiguarda 1988, Ann Neurol** (PMID 3415199) **[V-abs]**. n=6 (5 ischaemic, 1 traumatic haematoma; left hemisphere 4, right 2); insula damaged in all 6. No primary sensory deficit. Lost: withdrawal and appropriate emotional response to painful stimuli over the whole body and to threatening gestures; 5/6 also failed to react to verbal menace; "appeared unconcerned" and "seemed unable to learn appropriate escape or protective responses". Associated: neglect, body-schema disorders. Recalled, unverified **[R]**: patients recognised the stimulus as pain; some smiled. Note withdrawal and responses to *visual* threat were lost too.
- **Prefrontal lobotomy/cingulotomy for pain** — via Gerrans 2024 (PMC10860504) **[V-full of review; primary sources not read]**: patients said intensity was unchanged and were untroubled, yet automatic responses to noxious stimuli were "intact and often exaggerated". Foltz & White 1962; morphine "hurts but doesn't bother me" **[R]**. No quantitative study of protective behaviour after cingulotomy/morphine found.
- **Rainville et al. 1997, Science** (PMID 9252330) **[V-abs]**; **Rainville et al. 1999, Pain** (PMID 10467921) **[V-abs]**: hypnotic suggestion altered unpleasantness ratings largely independent of intensity (hand in 45–47.5 °C water, 1 min); unpleasantness, not intensity, correlated with heart-rate increase. Behaviour (withdrawal, tolerance, avoidance) was **not** measured.

## Row 5 — Congenital insensitivity to pain (CIP)

- **Cox et al. 2006, Nature** (PMC7212082) **[V-full]**. SCN9A loss of function; 6 affected individuals in 3 families. All bit lips/tongue in first 4 years; all had frequent cuts and bruises; most had fractures or osteomyelitis found late. "Normal intelligence" per parents, teachers, physicians. Older individuals "realized what actions should elicit pain (including acting as if in pain after football tackles)". Index case did street theatre (knives through arms, walking on coals) and died before age 14 jumping off a roof. Authors: such patients "fail to learn pain-avoiding (severe risk-avoiding) behaviours".
- **Zhang & Haga 2014** (PMID 24953503) **[V-abs]**. CIPA (NTRK1), 91 Japanese patients: fractures 65%, Charcot joints 29%, bone/joint infection 24%; "most fractures occurred from 1 to 7 years of age" (i.e. fracture rate falls later; other complications no age trend). CIPA often includes intellectual disability, so it is a weaker test of "normal intelligence".
- **Mifsud et al. 2019** (PMC6699213) **[V-full, partial]**. Whole-population series, Malta: 9 patients (mean age 33.7; mental status "normal" in the table rows checked, not all 9): 127 limb operations and 7 major lower-limb amputations between them.
- **Learning from instruction.** Rodríguez-Blanque 2024 systematic review (PMC11204641) **[V-full, partial]**: management rests on education and prevention; no controlled data on how far instruction reduces injury. Nagasako 2003 **[not obtained]**.
- **Other negative feelings.** McDermott et al. 2019 (PMC6424805) **[V-full, partial]**: SCN9A participant rated capsaicin pain and unpleasantness 0. Danziger et al. 2006 **[V-abs]**: normal semantic knowledge of others' pain, reduced aversive response to injury videos. Fear/sadness in SCN9A patients: not established here.

## Row 6 — Anhedonia vs apathy

- **Husain & Roiser 2018** (PMID 29946157) **[V-abs only]**: frames both as disorders of effort-based decision-making; "some shared mechanisms".
- **Gard et al. 2007, Schizophr Res** (PMC1986826) **[V-full]**. Study 1: 15 patients, 12 controls, experience sampling 7×/day for 7 days: in-the-moment enjoyment not different; anticipated pleasure lower (p=.06 overall; p<.01 for goal-directed activities); patients in goal-directed activity 17% vs 35.7% of samples. Study 2 (t df=99): TEPS consummatory not different, anticipatory lower (p<.01); anticipatory, not consummatory, pleasure correlated with BAS drive. All self-report; all patients medicated.
- **Kring & Barch 2014** (PMC4020953) **[V-full, partial]**: review; in-the-moment "liking" relatively intact in schizophrenia; deficits in anticipation, effort computation, goal maintenance.
- **Treadway et al. 2009** (PMC2720457) and **2012** (PMC3730492) **[V-abs]**: trait anhedonia (n=61 students) and major depression each went with fewer high-effort choices — anhedonia and reduced effort **co-occur** here.
- **Chong et al. 2015, Cortex** (PMC4533227) **[V-full]**. 26 Parkinson's patients (depression and apathy excluded) ON vs OFF dopaminergic medication, counterbalanced: accept/reject offers of apples for grip effort (*flexible choice*). ON raised effort indifference points (F(1,25)=25.9, p<.001); maximal force unchanged. **Felt pleasure was not measured.**
- **Le Heron et al. 2018, Brain** (PMC5917786) **[V-full]**. 39 Parkinson's patients, apathetic vs not (Lille scale), ON and OFF. Apathy: fewer acceptances specifically for low-reward offers; dopamine depletion: fewer for high-effort/high-reward offers; independent three-way interactions (p=.004, p=.003). Effort was not rated as more demanding by apathetic patients. **Hedonic experience not measured**; depression was not excluded.

## Row 7 — Wanting without liking

- **Leyton et al. 2005, Behav Neurosci** (PMID 16420164) **[V-abs]**. n=8 non-dependent cocaine users; catecholamine precursor depletion lowered cue- and cocaine-induced craving but **not** euphoria and **not** self-administration ("preliminary").
- **Evans et al. 2006, Ann Neurol** (PMID 16557571) **[V-abs]**. 8 Parkinson's patients with dopamine dysregulation syndrome vs 8 without; levodopa-induced ventral striatal dopamine release (raclopride PET) was greater in DDS and correlated with self-rated drug "wanting", not "liking". **Both wanting and liking here are felt, reported ratings.**
- **Giovannoni et al. 2000, JNNP** (PMC1736875) **[V-abs]**: 15 patients escalating dopaminergic drugs despite severe dyskinesias; tolerance to mood-elevating effects; negative affective state on withdrawal. Hoarding/planned procurement **[R]**.
- **Lambert et al. 2006, Addiction** (PMID 16669905) **[V-abs]**. n=202, retrospective reports of first cocaine use: liking and wanting *both* predicted later dependence; only the doubly pre-exposed subgroup showed lowest liking with highest wanting.
- **Portenoy et al. 1986, Pain** (PMID 3492699) **[V-abs]**. One woman, thalamic VPL electrode; "compulsive self-stimulation associated with erotic sensations". The abstract therefore reports a **felt, positive sensation**; the oft-repeated detail that it never reached satisfaction and that she neglected hygiene/family is **[R]**.
- **Heath B-19 / NAc DBS** via Berridge & Kringelbach 2015 (PMC4425246) **[V-full of review passage]**: reviewers argue B-19 was never quoted as calling stimulation pleasurable; Schlaepfer 2008 NAc DBS patients could not tell whether stimulation was on, yet "spontaneously" formed plans (visit Cologne Cathedral, done next day; take up bowling again) — i.e. a reported desire for a specific novel goal.
- **Berridge & Robinson 2016** (PMC5171207) **[V-full, partial]**: the authors distinguish incentive-salience 'wanting' (cue-triggered urges) from "cognitive wanting … with a declarative goal" and say the former is "less connected to cognitive goals".

## Row 8 — vmPFC patients

- **Bechara et al. 1997, Science** (PMID 9036851) **[V-abs; n = 10 controls, 6 patients R]**. Controls chose advantageously and showed anticipatory skin conductance before stating the strategy; patients "continued to choose disadvantageously even after they knew the correct strategy" and never developed anticipatory SCRs, "although some eventually realized which choices were risky".
- **Maia & McClelland 2004, PNAS** (PMC528759) **[V-full]**. 20 healthy students per condition (**no patients**). With a structured questionnaire, participants who chose advantageously could almost always report which decks were best and why. Bears on "before knowing" in controls, not on patients' know-but-don't-act. Reply: Bechara et al. 2005 **[V-abs]**.
- **Bechara, Tranel & Damasio 2000, Brain** (PMID 11050020) **[V-abs]**: patients' SCRs *after receiving* reward/punishment were not different from controls; deficit described as insensitivity to future consequences.
- **Fellows & Farah 2005, Cereb Cortex** (PMID 15217900) **[V-abs]**: vmPFC deficit on the task "largely explained by" a reversal-learning deficit; dorsolateral patients also impaired.
- **Eslinger & Damasio 1985 (EVR)** (PMID 4069365) **[V-abs]**: after bilateral orbital/lower mesial ablation, "superior" measured intelligence yet unable to meet personal and professional responsibilities, stable over 8 years. Blunted-feeling measures (absent SCR to emotional pictures; self-reported flattened emotion) **[R]**.

## Row 9 — Auto-activation deficit

- **Laplane & Dubois 2001, Mov Disord** (PMID 11746609) **[no abstract; R]**: bilateral pallidal/striatal lesions; inertia reversible by external prompting; patients report mental emptiness and blunted affect, typically without suffering.
- **Levy & Dubois 2006, Cereb Cortex** (PMID 16207933) **[V-abs]**: three apathy mechanisms — emotional-affective, cognitive, auto-activation; the last is "inability to self-activate thoughts or self-initiate actions contrasting with a relatively spared ability to generate externally driven behavior".
- **Schmidt et al. 2008, Brain** (PMID 18344560) **[V-abs]**. 13 AAD patients vs 13 unmedicated Parkinson's. Grip force to *instruction*: normal. **Skin conductance response to monetary incentives: not different from the comparison group.** Grip force failed to scale with money. No healthy control group in the abstract; felt pleasure not reported there.
- **Leu-Semenescu et al. 2013, Brain** (PMID 24026624) **[V-abs]**: 13 AAD patients; waking "mental blank" complete in 6; dream reports rare and without emotional content.
- **Riveros et al. 2018** (PMC6352737) **[V-full]**: single case; asked about feelings, "declared to have none"; acted only when stimulated; drive and emotion recovered together over 3 years while executive deficits persisted.
- **Adam et al. 2013, Cortex** (PMC3639369) **[V-full]**: single case, bilateral pallidal lesions; no speeding to rewarded targets; not depressed; reward sensitivity and initiative (got a job) restored by levodopa/ropinirole. Pleasure not measured.
