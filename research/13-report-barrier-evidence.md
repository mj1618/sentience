# 13 — Evidence for Scenario 11 (crossing the report barrier)

Evidence only. No scoring of D / C / R. Gathered 2026-10-03.

Tags: **[V-full]** full text read (methods/results) · **[V-abs]** abstract only · **[R]** recalled from memory, not checked.
Access note: WebSearch unavailable. Sources came from Europe PMC search and PMC full-text pages. Pre-1960 primary sources (Brickner, Hebb & Penfield, Mettler, Freeman & Watts, Hardy/Wolff/Goodell) could not be opened and are only seen through later papers or recalled.

## Row 1 — Bilateral frontal loss in speaking patients

- **Boly et al. 2017** (J Neurosci, review) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5628406/ — Describes Brickner's Patient A as bilateral removal of Brodmann areas 8–12, 16, 24, 32, 33, 45–47, "sparing only area 6 and Broca's area"; Hebb & Penfield's KM as "near-complete bilateral prefrontal resection" (areas 9–12, 32, 45–47); Mettler's psychosurgery patients (various partial bilateral topectomies) "remained fully conscious"; also Mataró 2001 and Markowitsch & Kessler 2000 (large bilateral prefrontal damage, perception preserved). **Assessment is of level of consciousness and perception; the paper records nothing about felt emotion in these cases.** It concedes ventromedial areas "may contribute" contents such as "feelings of reflection, valuation, and affect".
- **Odegaard, Knight & Lau 2017** (reply) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5628405/ — Patient A's postmortem image shows "massive portions of right PFC remain intact"; he also had a posterior meningioma. Hebb & Penfield's own diagram shows Broca's area, superior frontal gyrus and posterior middle frontal gyrus remaining, and they "acknowledge only removing approximately one-third of the frontal lobes". Mettler's cases were partial removals. Claims (author R.T.K.'s personal observation plus a video, not a published series) that near-complete bilateral *lateral* PFC lesions leave patients "abulic, akinetic… almost completely disconnected", whereas extensive bilateral *orbitofrontal* lesions leave patients "cognizant of sensations and fully interactive". **No statement about felt emotion.** Such patients often cannot be formally tested.
- **Brickner's Patient A — emotion** [R]: Brickner described boastfulness, irritability, anger outbursts and reduced restraint. Observer description, not systematic self-report. Unchecked.
- **Hornak et al. 2003** (Brain) [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12805109 — Surgical prefrontal excisions; includes a self-report subjective-emotion questionnaire. Bilateral OFC lesions and unilateral ACC/medial BA9 lesions: "significant changes in their subjective emotional state". Dorsolateral/other-medial group: "unimpaired on any of these measures". Abstract does not give n or direction of change (more vs less emotion); dorsolateral lesions were mostly unilateral [R].
- **Gillihan et al. 2011** (SCAN) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3023090/ — 7 ventromedial-frontal (VMF), 8 lateral-frontal (LF) lesion patients, controls. Self-report (PANAS; visual-analogue ratings during film/music mood induction) plus observer ratings. VMF: less dispositional positive and more negative affect than controls. LF: more negative affect than controls and **greater self-rated sadness reactivity** to induction. No group reported absent emotion. Small n; LF lesions not bilateral.
- **Patient "Roger"** (Philippi 2012 [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3425501/ ; Feinstein 2016 [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4734900/) — n=1, herpes encephalitis: bilateral insula, ACC, amygdala destroyed; right medial PFC completely destroyed, left medial PFC partly (abnormal signal throughout). Lateral PFC not listed as destroyed. Cold-pressor with continuous real-time ratings: intensity and unpleasantness both reached 10/10 on 3 of 4 trials; said "extremely unpleasant"; above the 75th percentile of 29 controls.
- **Patient B** (Damasio, Damasio & Tranel 2013) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3657385/ — n=1, bilateral insula, orbitofrontal, anterior cingulate, temporal damage; lateral frontal cortex spared. Observed 1976–2003; spontaneously reported being "very happy", complained of pain, behaviour congruent. Mostly observational plus spontaneous verbal report.
- **Not found:** any published case of near-total bilateral *lateral* PFC loss with a structured self-report of feeling.

## Row 2 — Dreaming

- **Maquet et al. 1996** (Nature, PET) [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8774879 — n=7. REM blood flow positively correlated with pontine tegmentum, left thalamus, both amygdalae, anterior cingulate, right parietal operculum; negatively with dorsolateral prefrontal, parietal, posterior cingulate, precuneus.
- **Braun et al. 1997** (Brain, PET) [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/9236630 — n=37 (REM contrasts n=7–8). Limbic/paralimbic regions (insula, cingulate, mesial temporal) reactivate in REM; heteromodal frontal and parietal association cortex dissociates from them (abstract truncated before detail).
- **Maquet et al. 2005** (meta-analysis) [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16186026 — hypoactive regions in REM "limited to inferior and middle frontal cortex and to the inferior parietal lobule" (i.e. not all of frontal cortex; medial PFC not listed as quiescent).
- **Fosse, Stickgold & Hobson 2001** (Sleep) [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11766165 — n=9, 88 REM awakenings at home; subjects rated emotion line-by-line in their own reports immediately after waking. Emotion in 74% of reports; joy/elation 36%, surprise 24%, anger 17%, anxiety/fear 11%, sadness 10%. Fear was *less* intense than joy, anger, surprise.
- **Sikka et al. 2014** [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24565868 — n=17; self-ratings give more emotional dreams than external judges' ratings of the same reports (method matters).
- **Merritt et al. 1994; Nielsen et al. 1991** [R] — commonly cited as emotion in ~95% of home dream reports with fear/anxiety most frequent; not checked.
- **Sikka et al. 2019** (J Neurosci) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6561691/ — n=17, two lab nights, woken 5 min into REM; oral report then self-rating (0–4 scale). Anger in 41% of dreams, interest in 88%; dream anger higher than waking anger. Greater right-than-left frontal alpha (F4–F3; interpreted as *less* right lateral PFC activity) predicted *more* dream anger (R²=0.435, p=0.004); no relation for interest. Effect not separable from evening waking asymmetry (trait-like). Limits stated: ratings made after waking; small n; EEG source uncertain; lab dreams less negative than home dreams.
- **Siclari et al. 2017** (Nat Neurosci) [V-full, via tool summary] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5462120/ — n=32/7/7; serial awakenings. Dreaming vs no experience tracked a posterior parieto-occipital zone in both NREM and REM; frontal high-frequency activity correlated with "thinking". **Emotion was not assessed.** Odegaard et al. note a prefrontal effect was present but not emphasised; Ruby 2020 disputes the conclusion.
- **Dresler et al. 2012** (Sleep, fMRI) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3369221/ — 4 trained lucid dreamers; **one subject, two lucid episodes** ("a case study"). Lucid > non-lucid REM: precuneus (strongest), cuneus, parietal lobules, right dorsolateral PFC, frontopolar, occipito-temporal. Emotion not measured. Activity may partly reflect the eye-signal task.
- **Voss et al. 2013** (LuCiD scale) [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23220345 — questionnaire factors; "realism and negative emotion do not differentiate between lucid and non-lucid dreams" (positive emotion did, by implication). Retrospective survey; no brain measure.
- **Not found:** any study measuring regional frontal activity and dream emotional *intensity* within subjects.

## Row 3 — Deep structure active, awake, no feeling reported

- **Whalen et al. 1998** (J Neurosci, fMRI) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6793390/ — n=10, 8 analysed (reported seeing only neutral masks). Amygdala signal higher for masked fearful than happy faces. Awareness assessed by debriefing after the whole session; questions were about what was *seen*. **Felt emotion was not asked.**
- **Pessoa et al. 2006** [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15930371 — with signal-detection measures per subject, no differential amygdala response to truly undetected 33-ms fearful faces; response depended on visibility. Direct challenge to the masking results.
- **Morris 1998; affective blindsight** — review by Celeghin, de Gelder & Tamietto 2015 [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26058355 : amygdala, pulvinar, colliculus respond to unseen emotional stimuli in cortically blind fields. Very few patients; "no feeling" rests on patients denying seeing.
- **Inman et al. 2020** (amygdala stimulation) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6139084/ — n=9 epilepsy patients, sham-controlled, blinded, 1–12 V. Dose-dependent skin-conductance rise and heart-rate slowing; **7 of 9 "reported no subjective emotional responses when queried"**. 2 of 9 reported feelings above ~5 V (one intense fear). Authors: self-report may be insensitive. This is amygdala, not PAG or hypothalamus.
- **Neudorfer et al. 2021** (Brain; hypothalamic-region DBS) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8557336/ — n=58 mild Alzheimer's patients, awake programming, stepwise voltage; 627 logged responses. Tachycardia 30.9%, warmth 24.6%, flushing 9.1%, hypertension 6.9%; **mood responses (fear, pleasantness) in 4.7% of stimulations**. Fear cluster: ventrolateral lateral hypothalamus/ventral striatum; pleasantness: posterior hypothalamus. Caveats: retrospective side-effect log; feeling not systematically queried; Alzheimer's cohort; targets were fornix/BNST region and different sites gave different effects (spatially separable clusters).
- **Wilent et al. 2010** [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/19852539 — n=1, awake; ventromedial hypothalamus stimulation evoked panic; threshold fell as the electrode went deeper.
- **Green et al. 2005, 2006, 2010** (PAG/PVG DBS) [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16237319 ; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16781077 ; https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21992829 — n=15, 16, 5 chronic-pain patients, awake. Ventral stimulation lowered systolic pressure by 14.2 mmHg (n=7); dorsal raised it by 16.7 mmHg (n=6). **Abstracts do not say whether feelings were asked about or reported**; full text not obtained. Older reports of fear/dread on dorsal PAG stimulation (Nashold 1969) [R].
- **Not found:** a study that drove PAG or hypothalamus strongly in awake people *and* systematically asked about feeling at the time.

## Row 4 — Timing

- **Méndez-Bértolo et al. 2016** [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27294508 — intracranial; amygdala responses to fearful faces from 74 ms, earlier than visual cortex. Stimulus-locked; no feeling report.
- **Krolak-Salmon et al. 2004** [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15157426 — n=10; fear-specific amygdala potentials from ~200 ms, then spreading to occipito-temporal, anterior temporal and orbitofrontal cortex (2 patients). No feeling report.
- **Inman et al. 2020** [V-full] (as above) — skin-conductance change "immediately after the onset of stimulation"; the one detailed fear report came after 30 s of 5 V stimulation. No measured latency of feeling.
- **Parvizi et al. 2013** (anterior mid-cingulate stimulation) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3877748/ — n=2; sham-controlled; thresholds 6 mA and 4 mA; reported foreboding plus determination, "shakiness", "hot flashes". No onset latency found in the text.
- **Bejjani et al. 1999** (NEJM) [R] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/10320386 — n=1; acute sadness within seconds of nigral-region stimulation, resolving in about 90 s. No abstract available; unchecked.
- **LeDoux & Brown 2017** (PNAS) [V-full, via tool summary] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5347624/ — evidence cited: masked/suppressed threat activating amygdala without reported fear; blindsight; amygdala-lesion patients who still feel fear; poor correlation between felt fear and physiological responses; fronto-parietal activity when stimuli are consciously seen. No timing evidence and no PFC-lesion evidence of abolished feeling were identified in it.
- **Not found:** any human study with simultaneous deep and cortical recordings that relates the *reported moment of feeling onset* to either.

## Row 5 — Frontal disconnection, cingulotomy, asymbolia

- **Freeman & Watts 1948; Hardy, Wolff & Goodell 1952** [R] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/18911006 (no abstract) — standard account: pain still perceived and described, thresholds unchanged or lowered, reduced concern ("still hurts but doesn't bother me"). **Not verified; no quantitative intensity-vs-unpleasantness data located.**
- **Davis et al. 1994** (Pain) [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/7892016 — n=1 psychiatric patient; bilateral cingulotomy then capsulotomy. Trained ratings: heat-pain threshold raised but **increased ratings of suprathreshold noxious heat**; cold became painful; cold-pain tolerance reduced.
- **Greenspan et al. 2008** (Eur J Pain) [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3338983/ — n=1 OCD patient; bilateral cingulotomy. Water-bath pain intensity ratings significantly **increased**; unpleasantness increases non-significant (possible ceiling). Contact heat during PET: intensity 8.6→7.7, unpleasantness 8.9→8.3, no significant change. On medication.
- **Roger** (row 1): bilateral ACC destroyed, unpleasantness ratings maximal.
- **Berthier et al. 1988** (pain asymbolia) [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/3415199 — n=6, unilateral lesions (left 4, right 2), insula damaged in all. No withdrawal and "absent or inadequate emotional responses" to pain over the whole body and to threat; unconcerned. Abstract gives observer description; patients' own words not in abstract (recognised pain but said it did not hurt or bother [R]).

## Row 6 — Wada test

- **Lee et al. 1990** [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/2340155 — n=44; 33 emotional reactions in 26 patients; laughter/elation more after right injection, crying more after left. Observer-rated behaviour.
- **Ahern et al. 2000** [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11011512 — affective self-report during the test: more negative affect with left inactivation, neutral/mildly positive with right, but subgroups showed the opposite or no change.
- **Kurthen et al. 1991** [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/1879162 — severe negative reactions in 4 of 159 tests. **Masia et al. 2000** [V-abs]: 4 of 550 patients, all with prior trauma.
- **Trimble 2010** (review) [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21077573 — mood change is dissociated from the hemiplegia/aphasia and occurs later, as the EEG recovers.
- Feelings continue in all series; none reports absence of feeling.

## Extra rows

- **(7) Ketamine** — Grace 2003 [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12911367 : n=400, ketamine 1 mg/kg anaesthesia; dreams in 94.6% without diazepam, "generally positive"; later recall. Nitrous oxide: only titles found (Zacny; Kamboj 2021), not read.
- **(8) Ictal fear** — Biraben et al. 2001 [V-abs] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1737203/ : n=8 with preserved consciousness; onset temporal in 7, frontal in 1; depth recordings (n=4) implicate orbitoprefrontal, anterior cingulate and temporal limbic cortex. Zhang et al. 2026 [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42229242 : n=6, 24 fear vs 20 non-fear seizures; fear seizures consistently involved orbitofrontal, lateral prefrontal, ACC, hippocampus.
- **(9) Sleep terrors** — Oudiette et al. 2009 [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2786046/ : n=43 (38 interviewed); 71% recalled at least one mentation; 106 mentations, mostly a single unpleasant scene. **Lifetime retrospective interview**, not post-episode awakening.
- **(10) Reversible lateral PFC interference** — Krummenacher et al. 2010 [V-abs] https://europepmc.org/article/MED/19875233 : low-frequency rTMS to left or right dlPFC "did not affect pain experience" (threshold, tolerance) but "completely blocked placebo analgesia". Weissman-Fogel & Granovsky 2019 review [V-full] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6728008/ : only 3 "virtual lesion" studies measured unpleasantness; dlPFC effects mixed. Maeoka et al. 2012 [V-abs]: anodal (excitatory) tDCS of left dlPFC, n=15, lowered rated unpleasantness of pictures.
