# 15 — Evidence for "Is the mystery an illusion?" (scenario 15)

Evidence only; no scoring. Gathered 2026-10-03 via Europe PMC, arXiv API, author-hosted PDFs.
Tags: **[V-full]** full text read · **[V-abs]** abstract only · **[R]** recalled, not re-checked this session.

## 0. What each side actually says

- **Frankish 2016** (JCS 23:11–39; eprint https://keithfrankish.github.io/articles/Frankish_Illusionism%20as%20a%20theory%20of%20consciousness_eprint.pdf) [V-full]. Strong illusionism: phenomenal properties are not instantiated; introspection "delivers a partial, distorted view", "misrepresenting complex physical features as simple phenomenal ones… it bundles it all together". The general concept of a phenomenal property "might be innate, the product of individual theorizing, or culturally acquired". Distinguishes *weak* illusionism (qualities real, only ineffability/privacy illusory), which he says most physicalists hold. Frankish does **not** state that training reduces the illusion.
- **Dennett** ("Quining Qualia" 1988; 1991 "user illusion") [R]. Qualia defined as ineffable, intrinsic, private, directly apprehended; denies anything has all four. Uses trained-discrimination cases (learning to hear harmonics in a guitar note; tasters) to argue apparent simplicity/ineffability yields to training.
- **Graziano, attention schema** [R]. The brain builds a simplified model of its own attention lacking mechanistic detail; from that model the system concludes it has a non-physical inner awareness. Explicitly predicts a machine with such a schema would claim awareness.
- **Chalmers 2018** (https://consc.net/papers/metaproblem.pdf) [V-full]. A realist who takes the meta-problem seriously. Lists "introspective opacity" and "primitive quality attribution" as candidate mechanisms. On prevalence: "it is hard to know how widely shared the problem intuitions are. It is clear that they are not universal"; treats "widely shared" as an "empirically defeasible assumption". Realist constraint: whatever explains consciousness should also figure in explaining our judgments about it. On machines (fn 26): a toy program already mirrors some phenomenal reports, so "we should probably be cautious about this sort of test for machine consciousness".
- **Other realists** [R]. Phenomenal-concept strategists (Loar, Papineau, Balog) explain gap intuitions by special concepts while keeping real qualities, so predict the same seemings as illusionists. Standard objection (Searle, Strawson, Nida-Rümelin): a seeming is already an experience. Kammerer (illusionist) holds the illusion is cognitively impenetrable.
- **Wording check.** Row 1's I-prediction (finer monitoring weakens ineffability) is Dennettian; it is not in Frankish, and Kammerer-style illusionism predicts persistence. Row 2's Q-prediction: many realists accept broad introspective fallibility (Schwitzgebel is himself a realist). Row 5's I-prediction matches Graziano; Chalmers also expects such reports from machines.

## 1. Introspective skill and ineffability

- **Fox et al. 2012**, PLoS One, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0045370 [V-full]. n = 38 meditators (1–15,000 h). Rated tactile sensitivity of 20 body regions after body scan, compared with *published group-average* two-point thresholds/S1 area. Experience predicted "accuracy". Critique (authors'): no individual psychophysics; measures bodily sensation, not affect or ineffability.
- **Pain: unpleasantness separates from intensity in experts.** Perlman et al. 2010, PMC2859822 [V-abs]: long-term meditators vs novices (≈9 vs 10 [R]) reported lower unpleasantness, not intensity, during open monitoring. Lutz et al. 2013, PMC3787201 [V-abs]: experts (>10,000 h) "equal pain intensity, but less unpleasantness". Zeidan et al. 2011, PMC3090218 [V-abs]: after 4 days' training unpleasantness −57%, intensity −40% (both fell; n = 15 [R]). Shows a feeling reported as separable components and the affective component changing; no rating of how describable or mysterious it seemed.
- **Dissolution-type reports.** Lindahl et al. 2017, PMC5443484 [V-full]: n = 60 Western Buddhist practitioners, interview taxonomy; "dissolution of objects" 18%, loss of sense of ownership 18%, affective flattening 17%; valence ranged very positive to very negative. Sample selected for challenging experiences. Ataria et al. 2015 (PMID 26379087) [V-abs]: single 20,000-h meditator; with boundaries dissolved, ownership/agency/self faded but "bodily feelings do not fade away completely".
- **Micro-phenomenology.** Petitmengin et al. 2013 (PMID 23719334) [V-abs]: guided elicitation raised detection of choice-blindness swaps to 80% (vs ~20% unguided) — reports become finer with guidance. Ermakova et al. 2025, PMC12592428 [V-full]: n = 32, micro-phenomenological interviews after 5-MeO-DMT; high-dose experiences still "largely described as ineffable".
- **Emotional granularity** (Kashdan, Barrett & McKnight 2015 [R]; Hoemann et al. 2021, PMC8355493 [V-abs]). Operationalised as low inter-correlation among emotion-label ratings across occasions; increases with repeated sampling. It measures label discrimination; no study found relating granularity to rated indescribability.
- **Alexithymia.** Defined by difficulty identifying and describing feelings plus externally oriented thinking (TAS-20; multiple abstracts [V-abs]). The "difficulty describing feelings" subscale is self-rated inability to find words [R item wording] — so *poorer* monitors rate their feelings as *harder* to put into words. This indexes verbal access, not a conviction of a special inner quality. Associated with lower self-reported interoception (Van Bael 2024 meta-analysis, 32 studies, PMC11542822 [V-abs]).
- **Direct ineffability ratings in meditators:** only via mystical-experience scales (see row 4); Palmer et al. 2024, PMC11620446 [V-abs], n = 613 meditators, mystical-experience scores correlated positively with "null-directed"/non-dual techniques. No study found that asks trained vs untrained introspectors how ineffable ordinary feelings seem.

## 2. Introspective error

- **Schwitzgebel 2008**, Phil. Review 117:245 (abstract: https://faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzAbs/Naive.htm) [V-abs]. Claims "gross error… about our own ongoing conscious experience"; examples are emotion, peripheral visual clarity, thought phenomenology. Argument is largely from disagreement and cases, not experiments. Reply: Hurlburt (Hurlburt & Schwitzgebel 2007 [R]) holds carefully probed momentary reports can be trusted while generalisations cannot.
- **Nisbett & Wilson 1977** [R]: concerns access to *causes* of behaviour, not current feeling.
- **Choice blindness.** Johansson et al. 2005, Science (PMID 16210542) [V-abs]; n = 120, 3 swapped trials each, ~13% detected concurrently, ≤26% overall [R]. Hall et al. 2010 (PMID 20637455) [V-abs]: jam/tea, "no more than a third" detected. Concerns reasons for a past choice.
- **Peripheral colour.** Cohen, Botch & Robertson 2020, PNAS, PMC7306755 [V-full]: n = 160 (40/condition), VR; "almost a third" failed to notice when <5% of the display was in colour. Error about current experience, but visual, not affective; interpretable as inattention rather than misreport.
- **Inner speech.** Heavey & Hurlburt 2008 (PMID 18258456) [V-abs]: n = 30, 10 beeps each; each of five phenomena in about a quarter of samples with wide individual variation; Hurlburt et al. 2013 (PMID 24184987) [V-abs] note discrepancy with questionnaire estimates.
- **Errors about current feeling.** Dutton & Aron 1974 (PMID 4455773; record only) — bridge study, small cells [R]; no preregistered direct replication found; Kenrick & Cialdini 1977 offered a non-misattribution account; Foster et al. 1998 meta-analysis found arousal raises attraction even when its source is obvious [R]. Schachter–Singer: replication failures (Marshall & Zimbardo 1979; Maslach 1979) [R]. These concern labelling/attribution, not whether a feeling is present. Depersonalisation: normal heartbeat-detection accuracy alongside reports of profound bodily strangeness (Michal et al. 2014, PMC3937420, n = 24 vs 26 [V-abs]). **No experimental study found** showing people mistaken about *whether* they are currently in pain.

## 3. Culture and development

- **Sytsma & Machery 2010**, Phil. Studies 151:299 [R]: laypeople willing to say a simple robot "sees red" but not "feels pain"; philosophers denied both — taken to show the folk lack the philosopher's unified concept. Critiques: Talbot 2012 (PMID 21277227) [V-abs]; Buckwalter & Phelan 2013; Fiala, Arico & Nichols ("see" read informationally) [R]. **Knobe & Prinz 2008** [R]: people resist ascribing feelings to group agents; critiqued by Sytsma & Machery 2009 [V-abs]. **Peressini 2014** [R]: folk have a "blurred" phenomenality concept — details not re-verified.
- **Díaz 2021**, JCS (https://doi.org/10.48350/156357) [V-abs]: four studies; "(1) problem intuitions are not widespread, and (2) when they arise, they do so because of factors unrelated to the nature of consciousness". Numbers not retrieved (full text blocked).
- **Gottlieb & Lombrozo 2018**, Psych. Science (PMID 29095658) [V-abs]: six studies; science judged less able to explain phenomena with first-person introspective access, human exceptionality, conscious will. Relative, not absolute, judgements.
- **Fischer & Sytsma 2021**, Cognition (PMID 34153926) [V-abs]: zombie conceivability judgements shift with "zombie" vs "duplicate" framing. **Sytsma & Fischer 2023**, Synthese [V-abs]: corpus study; ordinary "experience" rarely denotes mental episodes.
- **Berent 2023**, Open Mind, PMC10449398 [V-full, partial]: Prolific, n = 30 per experiment; Mary's seeing colour rated transformative *and* likely to "show up" in the brain. Small samples.
- **Dualism.** Barrett et al. 2021, Cognitive Science (PMID 34170020) [V-abs]: six populations; "most responses of most participants across all cultures tested were not dualist". Weisman et al. 2021, Nat. Hum. Behav. (PMID 34446916) [V-abs]: adults n = 711, children n = 693, five countries; bodily sensations and cognitive abilities separate everywhere; placement of emotions varies. Hood, Gjersoe & Bloom 2012 (PMID 22902285) [V-abs]: 5–6-year-olds thought memories less likely than physical properties to duplicate. Demertzi et al. 2009 (PMID 19351351) [V-abs]: n = 250 + 1,858; dualist attitudes predominant among students, >1/3 of health professionals. Dualism studies concern self/soul, not felt quality (Chalmers makes the same point).

## 4. Altering the self-model

- **Depersonalisation.** Sierra & Berrios 1998 (PMID 9807645) [V-abs]: model of prefrontal inhibition of limbic output giving "hypoemotionality", "indifference to pain". Sierra et al. 2002 (PMID 12215083) [V-abs]: n = 15 patients; skin-conductance response to unpleasant pictures reduced and delayed, but faster to non-specific stimuli. Medford et al. 2016, PMC4890597 [V-abs]: emotions "damped down", lacking "subjective validity"; attenuated insula and autonomic response, recovering with improvement. So bodily response is blunted along with the feeling, not preserved.
- **Pain asymbolia.** Berthier et al. 1988 (PMID 3415199) [V-abs]: n = 6, insular lesions; pain recognised without withdrawal or emotional response. Klein 2015 / Gerrans 2020 (PMC7658103) [V-abs] interpret as pain felt but not as "mine". Here both affect and protective behaviour are lost.
- **Psychedelic ego dissolution and ineffability.** Griffiths et al. 2018, PMC5772431 [V-full]: n = 75 (25/group), double-blind; MEQ30 ineffability 20.1 (1 mg) vs 74.4 and 76.3 (20–30 mg/70 kg), % of maximum. Barsuglia et al. 2018, PMC6292276 [V-full]: 5-MeO-DMT, n = 20, uncontrolled retreat; ineffability 88.7%, positive mood 88.7%. Nour et al. 2016, PMC4906025 [V-abs/partial]: n = 691, 1,828 experiences; Ego-Dissolution Inventory correlates rho = 0.735 with MEQ unity; valence depends on set/setting (bliss to dread). Smigielski et al. 2019, PMC6813317 [V-full, partial]: n = 39 meditators on retreat; psilocybin raised all mysticism sub-dimensions including ineffability over placebo. Caveats: the ineffability items rate the *whole episode* as beyond words, not the intrinsic quality of a feeling; novelty and memory encoding are confounds; no direct EDI × ineffability-subscale correlation extracted.
- **Meditation.** Dor-Ziderman et al. 2013, PMC3781350 [V-abs]: n = 12, "selfless" mode with beta-band decreases. Cessation: Chowdhury et al. 2023, PMC10843092 [V-abs] — one meditator, 37 events, described as complete discontinuity followed by clarity; Laukkonen et al. 2023 [V-abs]. No ineffability or "mysteriousness" measure. Millière et al. 2018, PMC6137697 [V-full, skim]: self-loss is several dissociable things; reports "often deemed ineffable" and hard to validate.

## 5. Machines

- **Lindsey 2026**, arXiv:2601.01828 [V-abs]: concepts injected into activations; models sometimes detect and name them; "highly unreliable and context-dependent". Tests functional self-access, not claims of ineffable states.
- **Berg, de Lucena & Rosenblatt 2025**, arXiv:2510.24797 [V-abs]: self-referential prompting elicits first-person experience reports across GPT/Claude/Gemini; suppressing deception/role-play features *increases* such claims. Prompted, and models are trained on human text.
- **Perez & Long 2023**, arXiv:2311.08576 [V-abs]: current self-reports "spurious… often just reflecting what humans would say"; propose training on verifiable self-questions. **Binder et al. 2024**, arXiv:2410.13787 [V-abs]: fine-tuned models predict own behaviour better than another model does. **Comsa & Shanahan 2025**, arXiv:2506.05068 [V-abs]: critique of LLM "introspection" claims.
- **Removing consciousness talk.** Schneider & Turner's ACT requires an AI denied human consciousness literature; Udell & Schwitzgebel 2021 criticise it [R]. **No study found** that trained a capable language model on a corpus purged of consciousness talk.
- **Attention schema agents.** Wilterson & Graziano 2021, PNAS, PMC8379943 [V-abs]: deep-Q agent controls an attention spotlight better with a schema. Liu et al. 2023 (arXiv:2305.17375), Piefke et al. 2024 (arXiv:2402.01056), Farrell et al. 2024 (arXiv:2411.00983) [V-abs]. All test control or coordination; none of these agents produces reports, so the prediction is untested there.

## 6. Prevalence among ordinary people

- No representative-sample estimate found of the fraction of laypeople who find an explanatory gap for feelings.
- Closest: Díaz 2021 ("not widespread", figures unretrieved); Sytsma & Ozdemir 2019 JCS [R, title only] concluding the concept is not widespread; Gottlieb & Lombrozo 2018 (relative reluctance for introspectable states); Fischer & Sytsma 2021 (framing-sensitive); Demertzi 2009 (mind–brain separateness, not gap).
- Professional philosophers, PhilPapers 2020: ~62% accept or lean toward there being a hard problem [R] — not a lay sample.
- Chalmers 2018 [V-full] asserts it is "easy to get ordinary people to express puzzlement" but cites no data and concedes non-universality.
